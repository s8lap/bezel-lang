## 0. Summary

Since v0.87 the one owned read of an owned field or owning slot, `move x.f`, has left the location **empty** and obliged the method to put something back before every exit. Everything that has been written about that read since is about the gap between the take and the store: the exception-edge inventory (v0.255, #3240), the vacated state (v0.328, 0143), the `finally` admission (v0.332) and its withdrawal (v0.334, 0163), per-slot tracking at a trackable index (0163), the stable holder and index, the frozen collection and the `Clear()` removal (v0.335, 0170), three inventory rulings (#5796, #5797, #5799) and three open trackers (#5828, #5909, #5910). The v0.335 train filed three more questions at the window's edges (#5887, #5888, #5889) and the matrix still carries open cells there (R5/C5, R5/C6·reindex, R4–R6/C3·scope-end, the window legs of R5/C10 and R5/C11).

**Decision.** The take and the store become **one expression**:

> `move loc with e` evaluates `loc`'s receiver and index, then `e`, and then — in one step, with no intermediate state — stores `e`'s owned value into the location and yields the location's previous value, owned. Where the location's declared type is nullable, `with null` may be omitted: `move x.f` stores `null`. Where it is not, the replacement is required.

There is no between, so there is nothing to restore, no window, no inventory, no vacated state, no index to track, no holder to hold stable, no collection to freeze. **A non-nullable owned location holds a live value at every instant; a nullable one holds a live value or `null`; the *moved-from* state is a state of bindings only.** The destructor keeps K9.3's bare take (teardown reads the field no more); static owned storage keeps K9.4 (neither taken nor replaced). No new keyword: `with` is reserved, and §9.5's tie-break for `await for` serves the third use unchanged.

**Text:** ≈5,600 specification words deleted, ≈2,500 added (net ≈ −3,100 in the specification; the rationale gains ≈520; measured in §4). **Reach, measured:** 170 program sites respell `var old := move _r; _r := fresh;` as `var old := move _r with fresh;`, 50 drop a `_r := null;` line, 19 feature-test rows of the hole-until-disposal shape are newly rejected (every one a test of the slot `move` itself; zero guide, document or rule-corpus programs), 22 reject pins and one rule case flip to accept (the take of a *nullable* field, whose only ground was K9.2), about 30 rejection tests of the window itself lose their ground (they stay rejected only under the new required-`with` rule, with a new diagnostic, or are retired), 9 rule cases are retired under process §10 and 16 respelled with their verdicts kept. Compiler: the restoration analysis is deleted, the parser gains one clause, the lowering gains "load old, store new" where it already nulls the slot.

## 2. Options

- **R1 (chosen) — the replacing `move`, bare take at a nullable location.** `move loc with e`; `move loc` alone at a nullable location stores `null`; required `with` elsewhere; destructor bodies keep K9.3's bare take; K9.4 unchanged. Deletes the window and every rule about it.
- **R2 — the replacing `move` with an explicit `with null`.** As R1, but a bare `move x.f` is rejected at every location: the detach idiom is spelled `move _c with null`, and the detach of a non-null value `(move _c with null)!`. The nullable declaration already announces that the location may be empty; the explicit form costs a clause at every one of the 50 take sites and a parenthesized `!` at every detach, for no information the type does not carry. The owner decided R1's reading for this train.
- **R3 — a stdlib primitive** (`Utils.Swap`-via-`ref`, the v0.85 shape; DECLINED 851 for `Swap<T>`). `ref` may not name an owned location (§22.3), and a library member cannot see a field's declared nullability; the primitive would need the language rule anyway.
- **R4 — a new keyword** (`take`, `replace`, `exchange`). Against the standing no-new-keyword direction (0137 option B precedent); `with` is reserved and its tie-break already exists.
- **R5 (status quo).** The window with its v0.335 rules; #5887, #5888, #5889 decided one by one; the next re-run finds the next face.

## 5. Soundness, and the cells

**Argument.** Every hazard the family has recorded is a state *between* the move and the restoring store — a callee, constructor body, destructor at a scope end, continuation, alias read, structural member, rebinding or outside-written index reaching a location that holds nothing (0163 §1, 0170 §1, #5796, #5797, #5888). The replacing `move` has no between. Its receiver, index and replacement are ordinary expressions with the language's ordinary exception semantics, the location untouched by a raise in any of them; the exchange is one load-and-store that runs no user code. Exactly-once holds by construction: the displaced value leaves with one owner (the expression's result, discharged under §28.3), the location holds exactly one live value (the replacement) or `null`, nothing is dropped, and nothing is skipped by a cascade. The one run-time posture that stays is the one every field overwrite already has: a borrow of the *old* value taken before the exchange (fields and slots are not frozen, K5.6) is bounded afterwards by the taker's holder, flag-backstopped in checked mode (K4.1) — today's "the object the move took is not flagged: a borrow of it taken before the move stays valid, bounded now by the taker's holder". Statics keep K9.4 (an always-live source is neither taken nor replaced). 0143's hazard — a borrowing-match binding over `h.Slots` reading slots the taker owns — cannot arise: after `move h.Slots with fresh` the storage holds `fresh`, visible whole, which is the posture §9.4 already states for a whole-value overwrite; no sentinel, no state store, and the two 0143 corpus cases that exercised the fault lose their subject. The owned-field cycle (#5774) is unchanged: `a._next := move h._next;` still forms it — a leak the record keeps (§28.13), not a hole.

**What the type system now says.** "A non-nullable owned location holds a live value at every instant" replaces K9.2's "the object must never escape the method with a hole a destructor could observe" — a stronger statement over a shorter text. The notion of a *moved-from location* — tracked per spelled path, per slot, at a stable index, through a stable holder, with a frozen collection, a vacated state and an exception-edge inventory — disappears; *moved-from* remains what it is for bindings, with one residue: a field a destructor body takes bare (K9.3) holds no value for the rest of that body, and a read of it there is a read of a moved-from location (K1), which the compiler already tracks straight-line for destructors.
