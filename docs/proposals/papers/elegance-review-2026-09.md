# Elegance & complexity review — 2026-09 (v0.286)

*Supporting record (not a numbered proposal). One pass over the **whole normative surface** — language spec and standard library at **v0.286** (HEAD `3f044f3`, 2026-09-04) — whose only question was *where can the surface be made more elegant and less complex*: duplicate spellings of one thing, special cases that could fall out of a general rule, A-not-B irregularities, accreted rule sets, stdlib members redundant with each other or with a language feature, and presentation whose bulk exceeds its rule content. It is the elegance-program's question (v0.242–v0.246) re-asked over the shipped surface, and it is distinct from the [§10 subtraction review](../spec-change-process.md) (whose next pass is due at v0.303): a subtraction pass asks what is no longer earning its place; this pass also asks what could be said once instead of four times. Like the subtraction pass it **mints nothing** — its output is a proposal or nothing. Output: [proposal 0122](../0122-language-simplification-batch-2026-09.md) (the language docket, items L1–L18) and [proposal 0123](../0123-stdlib-regularization-batch-2026-09.md) (the stdlib docket, items R1–R10), both `under-review`; plus the sixteen incidental defects listed below, ready to file.*

## Method

Three review lanes partitioned the surface. **S1 — spec foundations:** `Bezel_Language_Spec.md` §1–§16, §35, and Appendices A–D, read in full and cross-checked against `Bezel_For_Code_Generation.md` §7 (*Looks right, is rejected*). **S2 — spec constructs:** spec §17–§34 (lines 3298–10155), read in full, cross-checked against the code-generation reference's §4/§7, proposals 0093 and 0118 (the kernel tables), and the 2026-08 subtraction records. **S3 — the standard library:** `Bezel_Standard_Library.md` §1–§11, all 5,587 lines. Every lane was held to one bar (the shared brief): cite `file:line` and quote the load-bearing sentence verbatim; **read the record before proposing** (`DECLINED.md` in full — 594 lines — plus the rationale, the changelog, and the prior elegance and subtraction records: 0093, 0098, 0106, 0121, the 2026-08 subtraction review, the 2026-08-30 duplication census); measure usage with a named grep over `tests/` (2,177 `.cpp` + 2,951 `.bzl` at HEAD — `find tests -name '*.cpp' | wc -l`, likewise `*.bzl`), the guides, and `src/`; carry the highest burden on absence claims (process §4); and trace every merge or removal through real Bezel per Appendix A and the stdlib name index, never an invented API. Findings were ranked by (benefit to elegance) × (confidence), each with a change class per process §2 and a "considered and dropped" list so the verifier would not re-derive settled keeps.

Three **independent adversarial verification lanes** (V1, V2, V3 — one per review lane, per process §4) then re-located every quote with `sed -n`, re-ran every grep verbatim plus at least one broader recipe of their own per absence claim (whole repo, all proposals, the changelog, `src/` per process §5 item 3b), re-checked the record per topic, hunted a killer counterexample for every merge/remove/generalize (a program the current rule handles and the proposed shape breaks, or a dependent rule elsewhere in the spec), and returned one of four verdicts per finding: `CONFIRMED`, `CONFIRMED-WITH-CORRECTIONS`, `WEAKENED` (real observation, failed shape, with the surviving shape named), or `REFUTED`. **The verify files override the lane files wherever they disagree**: every correction is applied in the dockets, every refuted item is dropped, every weakened item is narrowed to the shape the verifier named, and every count the verifier re-measured replaces the lane's.

## The refutations, kept honest

Each refuted finding is recorded with the record that killed it, so the next pass does not re-raise it.

- **S1-13 — move §2.8.6 (interface delta classification) to an appendix.** The lane wrote "placement was not among [proposal 0044's] declined alternatives". It was: `0044-behavioral-artifact-interface.md:268`, **Q4 — spec placement**: "§2.8.6 appended after the §2.8.5 non-goals (recommended: keeps every existing §2.8.x citation stable; the ordering oddity is acknowledged) — versus a new Appendix E, versus tool-docs-only". The lane's proposal is the declined alternative verbatim, and the ground is measurable: 107 textual `§2.8.6` citations across `docs/` would re-spell, against the 4 anchor links "anchors preserved" actually covers. The supporting absence claim ("no conforming diff tool is named anywhere") also fails — `0044:3` names `bezel interface`/`bezel diff-api` (impl #2054). No new evidence; settled.
- **S2-08 — delete the `await for … with token` clause.** Not a duplicate of the parameter form: the clause is the surface for the token parameter on the *interfaces* — `IAsyncEnumerable<T>.GetAsyncEnumerator(token := CancellationToken.None)` (stdlib `:2389`–`:2390`), `GetOwningAsyncEnumerator(token …)` (`:2423`), and spec `:8609` routes the clause to both. Two stdlib productions accept no token at all — `Task.WhenEach(tasks: Array<Task>): IAsyncEnumerable<Task>` (`:4300`) and `Channel<T>.ReceiveAll(): IOwningAsyncEnumerable<T>` (`:4427`) — so `await for var item in inbox.ReceiveAll() with token` has **no** rewrite under the proposal except the manual `await using` + `MoveNextAsync` protocol. The lane's risk line ("a compiler-generated iterator only") was false. Removal deletes capability. One presentation residue survives and is noted in 0122 §5: §9.5's `with`-disambiguation rule is stated at three sites.
- **S2-09 — let §27.3's lambda return inference use §20's common-type rule.** Ruled at v0.217 and the lane's grep missed it on a hyphen: the record spells it **"divergent-return"**. `Bezel_Design_Rationale.md:190` (§3.13): the lambda keyword "opens the one slot the old spelling structurally could not host: a **return-type ascription** … which resolves §27.3's divergent-return case at the site"; `0064-keyword-lambdas.md:17` "The refusal itself is sound and untouched"; 0064 §2.3 item 4 "the slot is where the programmer *states* the type, never where the compiler computes one"; changelog `:7004`. The lane's new evidence does not answer the ground — under §20's subtype conversion `return dog; return animal;` infers `Animal` and a later hierarchy edit silently re-types the lambda, which is exactly the instability §27.3 names — and the left-fold would make inference depend on the textual order of scattered `return`s.
- **S3-03 — delete `CopyFrom`/`MoveFrom`, keep the `To` forms.** The lane wrote "Rationale: no entry." Rationale §5.15, *Ergonomics pass (v0.109)*, `Bezel_Design_Rationale.md:635`–`:648`, rules it in one sentence: "Each is equivalent to the corresponding CopyFrom/MoveFrom on the receiving side — `source.CopyTo(dest)` is `dest.CopyFrom(source)`. **Both directions are provided because the natural call form depends on which side the caller holds.**" Changelog `:4071` repeats it. The lane's load-bearing quote was also out of scope and inverted: stdlib §1 `:58`'s no-dual rule is scoped to *fault conditions* ("One primary mechanism per fault condition"), and its "the newer spelling is the one that yields" would delete the `To` forms (v0.109) the proposal keeps. Nothing survives but a one-line cross-reference from §4.14 to rationale §5.15, recorded in 0123 §5.

Two incidental claims were refuted alongside (V2 #3: `DECLINED.md:204`'s "§27.3/§20 no-LUB posture" is accurate — both rules refuse a least-upper-bound *search*, which is all the row claims; V2 #6: §28.8.1's bare `(v0.255)` pin is sanctioned by process §7, so it is a nit, not an instance of the narration class).

## The weakened items, and what survived

- **S1-02 (four "declared-target position" lists).** The shape — one definition at §14.5's breadth — is unsound: §17.7 excludes argument position *and says why* ("overload resolution", `S:3487`), while §14.5's set presupposes a chosen callee (`S:2688`–`2692`); `new()` and a bare `Ok(v)` need a target *before* candidate selection. **Survives:** name the written-declared-type position set once for §4.2/§17.7/§35.3 and let them cite it; §14.5 keeps its own derivation clause; no widening. → **L17**.
- **S1-04 (one spelling for `Equals`/`GetHashCode`/`ToString` on value kinds).** The admission half holds (enums and choices may write `override`, matching records); the rejection half is refuted by a lit-pinned ACCEPT on a *record* declaring the plain spelling (`tests/lit/sema/sema_record_equality__ineligible_user_equals_accepted_spec_9_2.bzl`) — "one spelling" would break an accepted program or leave records dual and cure nothing. The spec's inline ground (`S:1783` "There is no override chain and no `base` access") does not distinguish records, which `S:442` puts in the same via-boxing bucket. → **L11** (admission only).
- **S1-05 (reject the redundant declaration-side `owned` on move-only values).** Both of `R:171`'s recorded costs survive the lane's rebuttal: a generic choice's classification is per-instantiation (§35.4 rule 1), so the redundancy *is* substitution-dependent beyond bare-`T` slots; and §28.1 clause 3 is defined by reference to clause 1 ("as for the concrete type (clause 1)"), so the flip either propagates into clause 3 against clause 4's stated purpose or adds an irregularity. **Survives:** consolidating three of the four restatements (`S:2903`, `S:10179`, `S:10227`) into cross-references — an editorial the docket does not carry separately (it rides L16's narration sweep if the owner wants it). Explicitly not proposed in 0122 §5.
- **S1-09 (sub-range spelling across `string`/`Array<T>`/spans).** Both removals fail: `Array<T>.Slice(r)` is the *definition* the indexer is "the compiler-recognized equivalent of" (`L:1600`, `S:3125`); `Substring(i, n)` is length-based, not endpoint-based; and §27.6 has no indexer-reference form, so `arr.Slice` as a method value has no replacement. Stdlib §1's no-dual rule is fault-scoped and does not reach it. **Survives:** nothing to remove; if anything an *addition* (a range indexer on the spans). Not proposed.
- **S1-12 (§9.2's "decides nothing" third disqualifier).** The merge is false for **choices**: §25.2 spells the co-declared member plain on a choice, so §9.2's first disqualifier never fires there and the third decides on its own — which is why the spec scopes its self-deprecation "for a conforming **record**". Becomes sound only behind S1-04's admission half. → note only, sequenced behind **L11** (0122 §4).
- **S2-06 (`_ := x;` on a named owned binding → error naming `x.Dispose()`).** Killer counterexample inside the rule: a move-only value binding has no `Dispose()` to name — `S:2906` "no `Dispose()` to invoke; the array is not a class", `S:3674`/`S:11924` put move-only values inside the discard's reach, and the compiler implements it (`src/sema/ExprTypecheck.cpp:14949`). `_ := arr;` is today's only early-disposal spelling for an owning inline array. The form is also a taught idiom (Guide 01 `:45`, `:394`; code-gen reference ×5) and priced in rationale §5.28 (`:968`) as one of three deliberate escape hatches. **Survives:** merge consume-table rows K3.9/K3.10 into one row with a two-valued Spelling column. → **L18**.
- **S3-06 (generic `Abs<T>`/`Sign<T>` over `INumber<T>`).** "Zero at call sites" is false for `Abs`: all eleven primitives conform (`L:1135`), so `Math.Abs(shortVal)` today widens to `Abs(int)` under §5.1 and would infer `T = short` with a **changed return type** at every `var` binding and follow-on; and `pure` is structurally lost (a constraint-typed body cannot call the non-`pure` `INumber<T>` members, which is why the already-generic `Min`/`Max`/`Clamp` are not `pure`). **Survives:** `Sign<T>(x: T): int` — its return type is `int` at every substitution. → **R4**.
- **S3-07 (delete the 60 per-type `Write`/`WriteLine`/`Append` rows).** §1 `:70` disclaims *performance characteristics*, not performance-motivated *signatures* — read the lane's way it would condemn `GetBytes(s, Span<byte>)` and the `TryWrite*` tier too; two adjacent declines in graduated proposal 0074 (`:160`, `:164`) take the family as load-bearing; the count is 60, not 55 (12 types); and `Console` declares no `Write(char)`. **Survives:** the additive lowering sentence beside `L:111`. → **R10**.
- **S3-08 (`TextReader.Read(): int` → `char?`).** §1 `:48`'s own neighbouring clause grandfathers it — "an existing spelling stands until its surface is re-ruled … moved only by its own graduation ruling" — and the claimed v0.231 precedent *added* `ReadKeyOrNull()` beside a still-throwing `ReadKey()`, changing no return type. Accepted proposal 0006 `:182` rules EOF stays a sentinel. **Survives:** the recorded-misfit flag §1 `:48` requires and §7.4 lacks. → **R9**.

7. **[#3812](https://github.com/s8lap/bezel-lang/issues/3812) — The compiler accepts an undocumented primitive alias `Single`.** `src/sema/TypeResolution.cpp:51`: `if (name == "Single") return PrimitiveType::Float;`. §3.4's dual-form table and reserved-name list (`:492`) contain no `Single`, and stdlib §9.2 (`:565`) states "Bezel has no separate `Single` record type; `float` is the only 32-bit floating-point type." ## What this pass teaches the next one

The verifiers' lane-quality notes converge on four systematic weaknesses, each of which cost at least one finding, one strength worth keeping, and one tooling gap the drafting lanes ran into.

1. **Search the record by topic, not by phrase — and do not stop at the first zero.** Every one of the four refutations was a recorded decision the lane's own grep should have surfaced: S2-09 died on a **hyphen** (`divergent-return` in the rationale, `divergent return` in the grep); S3-03 asserted "Rationale: no entry" over a *titled* rationale paragraph (§5.15, *Ergonomics pass (v0.109)*); S1-13 read `DECLINED.md` and a proposal's status line but not the proposal's own open-questions section (0044 Q4 had asked and answered "versus a new Appendix E"); S1-04 grepped only the rationale before writing "no rationale records the asymmetry" when the ground is stated inline in the spec (`S:1783`). The same failure undercut surviving findings: S2-06 missed rationale §5.28's three-escape-hatch passage, S2-07 cited proposal 0121 §2 but not the indexed `DECLINED.md:157` row whose reopening bar it actually satisfies. The recipe the next pass should run *per finding*: `grep -in` over `DECLINED.md`, the rationale, the changelog **and** `docs/proposals/*.md` for the topic's nouns with and without hyphens, then read the nearest proposal's open-questions section in full.
2. **Escape your grep patterns, and re-run every count before you trust it.** S3-11's usage table was produced with `grep -E` on patterns containing an unescaped `(`, and was wrong by up to 8× in both directions (`Implements(`: lane 0/0/0, real 3/4/0 — with the spec declaring the member at `:3550`); S3-01's array-receiver recipe contained a literal `…` inside a regex and returns 0, not 16; three row counts were off (write tier doubled, sinks 55 vs 60). V3's verdict — "no count in this lane should be trusted without re-measurement, even where several reproduce to the digit" — is the rule: use `grep -F` for member names, and put the exact command beside every number so the verifier can re-run it. Where the lanes *did* this (V2: 14 of 16 greps and every word count reproduced exactly; V1: all forty-plus `D:NN` row citations landed), verification was fast and the findings held.
3. **Trace the proposed signature through the stdlib's signatures and the spec's conversion tables, not just the prose.** S2-08 died because `Task.WhenEach` and `Channel<T>.ReceiveAll` take no token — one `grep -n IAsyncEnumerable docs/Bezel_Standard_Library.md` would have shown it; S2-06 died on §8.7/§15.3 (move-only values have no `Dispose()`); S3-06 and S3-07 both claimed observational neutrality ("zero at call sites", "identical output") without walking §5.1's implicit-conversion table or §22.6, which is exactly where both broke. Where a lane *did* trace into the implementation it was rewarded (S2-02's 18-file `src/` footprint is real; S1-08 turned out to be already implemented — the cheapest win in the review).
4. **Verifying that a rule is restated in four places is not verifying that the four restatements are the same rule.** S1-02's four position lists differ at the argument boundary for a reason §17.7 states out loud; S1-05's §28.1 clause 3 is *defined by* the clause the finding would flip; S1-12's third disqualifier is load-bearing on the one kind (choices) whose spelling §25.2 makes plain. In each case the observation of duplication was right and the inference of redundancy was not — which is why five of S1's fourteen findings survive only as their editorial halves. The check to add: for every "N sites say the same thing" claim, diff the N statements and account for every difference before proposing the merge.
5. **Proposals sit outside the mechanical gate, and nothing says so.** `scripts/check_docs.sh` scans exactly the ten normative documents plus `Bezel_For_Code_Generation.md` — its `DOCS` table at `scripts/check_docs.sh:78`–`90` — and its ANCHOR check (`:196`–`217`) walks only those files, dropping any link into another file as "link to a non-normative file; out of scope" (`:207`); `docs/proposals/` is named nowhere in the script. So a proposal's anchor links, its `§`-citations, and its fenced examples are gated by no script at all: this pass's anchor audit over 0122 and 0123 was done by hand — every `(../Bezel_Language_Spec.md#sec-…)` link re-located with `grep -n 'id="sec-…"'` — and a stale anchor in a live proposal would survive every gate cycle until graduation surfaced it. Two remedies, for the owner to choose between: extend the ANCHOR check's file set to `docs/proposals/*.md` (links out of a proposal resolve against the same `valid[]` sets; the proposal files themselves need no anchor inventory), or add one sentence to [`spec-change-process.md` §3](../spec-change-process.md) recording that proposals sit outside the mechanical gate — so that a future composition gate, or the next pass, does not assume coverage the script does not have. The first is a few lines of Python; the second is the honest minimum if the first is declined.

And the strength: the lanes' **"Considered and dropped" lists were load-bearing, not padding** — all three verifiers spot-audited them (V1 six entries, V2 nine `DECLINED.md` citations, V3 four items), found each correctly dropped with its record, and reported that the lists kept them from re-deriving settled keeps. Keep the section; it is the cheapest verification-time saving in the format.
