# Verdict — #1414 Refactor Plan, Adversarial Review Synthesis

Six attack reports (five stages + steelman-no-refactor). Net result: **3 stages amended, 1 narrowed and rebounded, 1 dropped and replaced, 1 new precondition stage added.** The plan header "All stages behavior-preserving" must be struck — Stage 4 Phase B is behavior-changing by design.

## 1. Per-stage rulings

### Stage 1 — GLOB test auto-registration: **AMENDED**
Proceed. Ninja generator (CMakePresets.json:9) makes `CONFIGURE_DEPENDS` reliable; all 27 of the last 120 commits touching tests/CMakeLists.txt were pure list maintenance that GLOB eliminates; no per-file properties exist to disturb.
**Exact amendments:** (a) rename `tests/codegen/iterator_test.cpp` → `iterator_test.cpp.disabled` — it is intentionally out of the suite (LLVM JIT state accumulation, commit `0112853`; a bare glob silently restores 10 unreliable tests) and the rename makes the exclusion self-documenting; (b) add the steelman's CI guard comparing discovered-vs-linked test counts, since GLOB converts loud list conflicts into silent omission. The steelman's `merge=union` alternative is **declined with reasons**: it kills only the 3 trivial conflicts, not the 27-commit registration toil, which is the real measured cost.

### Stage 2 — runtime TU split: **AMENDED**
Proceed, with the plan text corrected on four measured falsehoods. **Exact amendments:** (1) the 8-name TU list covers ~half of the 13.1k lines — replace with the actual ~12–15-domain inventory (serialization ~2.2k, sync primitives ~1k, decimal+guid ~0.9k, Shared/Weak, Events, itable, refcounting, Encoding, File I/O), each bucket with recorded rationale; (2) tasks/channels/cancel/timer are ONE coupled concurrency cluster (g_scheduler/g_timer/g_main_domain/g_current_domain shared; `bz_task_delay_cancelable` at :8855 touches all three lanes) — ship as a single `bezel_runtime_concurrency.cpp`; strike "runtime lanes become file-disjoint"; (3) rescope "mechanical" to include the enumerated linkage promotion (~9 file-local statics + 6 globals into a runtime-internal header); (4) update `tests/native_test_harness.hpp` `asanRuntimeObject()` in the same PR to `ld -r`/archive the N objects behind the existing single-path API (80 link sites depend on it); (5) keep the single `add_library(bezel_runtime STATIC)` target so the whole-archive JIT link is untouched. **Honest rejustification per the steelman:** zero conflicts were measured on this file even under concurrent lanes — the split is navigability and review-scope, not conflict repair.

### Stage 3 — builtin registry: **AMENDED**
Proceed; this is the best-grounded stage: ~609 raw type-name comparisons in three-way duplication (sema has *no* family helper — 136 inline chains in ExprTypecheck_Calls.cpp alone), 16 live `rfind("Owning",0)` prefix checks, and the LlvmBackend_Layout.cpp:512–560 hand-lists whose own comments document the miss-a-list bug class.
**Exact amendments:** (1) registry columns are **data only** — name, arity, category, owningFamily, lowered rep, iterability/element-type rule, member-name surface; typing entries as rows only where pure, function-valued hooks for the hard tail (Channel T-category, LINQ aggregates); (2) existing predicates (isCollectionTypeName, isDictTypeName, isOwningCollectionNameHead, builtinTypeArity, Layout rep lists) become one-line delegates, and sema routes through ONE TypeRef→name accessor handling `importedName` (kills the cross-module recognition drift); (3) do **not** migrate the order-sensitive `tryLower*` dispatch chain — separate, riskier stage; (4) leave LlvmBackend_ElementPolicy.cpp as-is; (5) correct the payoff claim: one-row wins the silent-miss class, not total per-builtin work.

### Stage 4 — shared ownership-facts: **AMENDED**
Proceed. The attack *strengthened* the premise by finding a second live drift bug — sema's producerState never consults `importedReturnOwned` (Ownership.cpp:1688–1721 vs LlvmBackend_Setup.cpp:7839/11701), dynamically verified as a silent leak and filed as **#1415**. This satisfies the steelman's own "second drift bug" threshold for a full module.
**Exact amendments:** (1) deliverable is a shared **core + wrappers**, not "one module": expression-shape producer/transfer classification over (Expr, SemaContext, TypeParamResolver hook) — codegen injects its `genericSubstSemaTy_` resolver; mechanism selection (disposerFns_, runtime-vs-vtable routing, unwind-ineligibility) stays in codegen; (2) **Phase A** behavior-preserving extraction (two documented entry points, gated by the suite) / **Phase B** drift closures #1388 + #1415, each behavior-changing, own PR, full in-process JIT suite + ASan; (3) stage-3/4 boundary drawn: all name-keyed tables (incl. the 16 rfind sites' semantics) live in the Stage-3 registry; the facts module consults it and owns only expression shape — the rfind-prevention credit moves to Stage 3; (4) add the fourth, weakest copy `isFreshOwnedClassProducer` (Setup.cpp:3532) to the migration list; (5) discharge facts extend the proven publish-annotation pattern (autoDropAtScopeEnd), keeping codegen's conservative fallback for mid-function lambda/thunk lowering.

### Stage 5 — Emitter decomposition: **DROPPED (replaced)**
All three justifying claims refuted by measurement: the "narrow interface" is 62 external members with `disposeStack_` raw-mutated 261 times across 10 TUs under an interleaved scope protocol, and the subsystem graph is cyclic (disposal ↔ Stmt/Expr/Async lowering; the family already spans 4 TUs); the "world rebuild" is 12 TUs / 11.5s cold / ~3 minutes per 300 commits, with the 770-TU test target insulated by its PCH; "DisposalEngine first" puts maximum regression stakes on the §28 machinery the suite demonstrably does not lock (#1387, #1388, firing-50 — all found by gate probing, not the green suite). Both independent reports converge.
**Replacement:** mechanical definition-motion TU splits of Calls.cpp (18,670), Setup.cpp (15,286 → extract LlvmBackend_Disposal.cpp), Exprs.cpp (12,525) — zero class-boundary risk; optionally partial-class-style section headers for impl.hpp sold as navigability only. Any future true extraction requires: Stages 3/4 landed first (they remove 16/29 of outbound coupling), TypeLayouts first (acyclic), and a prerequisite dispose-scope guard API PR train with fresh coupling measurement.

## 2. The steelman's strongest points, and the surviving plan's answers

1. **Churn/kloc inversion — the giants are the coldest files per line.** Conceded fully. Every surviving stage is rejustified on semantic duplication or registration toil, never on "hot file" pressure.
2. **Measured conflict cost ≈ zero.** Conceded. Stage 2 proceeds as convenience with honest framing; Stage 1's justification is 27 commits of toil, not conflicts.
3. **The suite does not lock disposal.** Conceded and converted into **Stage 0** (below): the run's ad-hoc ASan probe configurations become committed suite tests before anything touches §28 machinery.
4. **Rebuild cost is ~9s, not a world rebuild.** Conceded; it was decisive in dropping Stage 5.
5. **Opportunity cost vs. #1388/#1390/#1397/#1399/#1406.** Answered: Stage 4 Phase B *is* the fix vehicle for #1388/#1415, so the refactor train carries correctness payload; the other open defects outrank Stage 2 and the Stage-5 replacement in the merge queue.
6. **Where the steelman loses:** its "defer Stage 4 until a second drift bug" criterion was met during the review itself (#1415), and its "shared allowlist function suffices" underestimates the four measured classifier copies with three different answer sets.

## 4. Reconciliation: "Opus saw no problem" vs. the change-geometry diagnosis

Both readings were correct about the evidence each could see, and the collision is resolved by distinguishing mechanical geometry from semantic geometry. Opus's no-problem verdict is right on every mechanical axis the steelman measured: conflicts are effectively zero, header rebuilds cost seconds, the Emitter god-class is a precedented clang-style decomposition already split into 12 TUs, and the structure sustained 33 merges in two days — on those axes the refactor plan was proposing to spend a multi-train budget to buy about three minutes per 300 commits, and Stage 5 deserved to die. But the change-geometry diagnosis is right about the axis Opus's view didn't price: the same semantic fact — "this call produces an owned value," "this name is an owning collection" — is written independently in three or four places, and those copies drift silently, invisibly to a green 10k-test suite, as proven by #1388, by the rename's `rfind("Owned")` near-miss, and conclusively by #1415, a live unsound leak found *during this review* in exactly the predicted duplication. The honest synthesis is that the codebase's file geometry is fine and its fact geometry is not; the surviving plan drops everything justified by the former, keeps the narrowed registry and facts-core justified by the latter, and adds the test coverage that makes the suite able to referee the difference next time.
