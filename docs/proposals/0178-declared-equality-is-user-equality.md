## 0. Summary

Proposal 0176 (v0.336) made the own-typed `Equals` the `Object` slot on every value kind, and the v0.336 lane and critique recorded three seams it left. (1) §3.6's hashable row admits a record only if it is auto-hashable or "declares user equality", defined as "`operator =`/`<>` with `Equals(T)`/`GetHashCode` per the §25.2 co-declaration rule": a record declaring `Equals(other: Key)` and `GetHashCode` but no `=` has every member §9.2's three kinds reach and every member a `Set<Key>` consults, yet is outside **hashable** by the letter, while an enum declaring the same two members is hashable (#5915). (2) §35.7 says a value-like choice's methods "neither qualify nor disqualify" auto-hashability, so a choice declaring `Equals(other: Tag)` and `GetHashCode` keeps the generated structural `=` beside a replaced `Equals` (§35.2): `Tag.A(1) = Tag.A(2)` is `false` at the declaration and `true` through `where T: hashable` — one `=` with two definitions, the divergence §9.2's second disqualifier exists to prevent on a record (#5917 and its generic face). (3) No value kind can declare `Equals(other: Object?)`, so an interface declaring that member has no declared witness on a record, an enum or a choice (#5916).

**Decision.** One rule for the three: *declaring user equality* on a value kind means declaring its own-typed `Equals` — the slot — with the `GetHashCode` §8.3 obliges beside it, whether or not an `=`/`<>` overload accompanies them. On a record and a value-like choice alike the declaration takes the type out of the auto-hashable set (the generated `=`, `Equals`, `GetHashCode` and `ToString` go), `=`/`<>` stay the use-site compile error until the operator overloads are written (§25.2 obliging the pair beside a homogeneous overload, as today), and the type is **hashable** on the members it declares — a hash-container key, admitted under `where T: hashable`, and reached by §9.2's three kinds. §8.4 says in so many words that such a type has no `=` at all rather than a dispatch to `Equals`. An interface member spelled as one of `Object`'s virtual members — `Equals(other: Object?)`, `GetHashCode(): int`, `ToString(): string` — is satisfied by the member that fills the type's `Object` slot: on a class the inherited virtual member in either spelling; on a value kind the own-typed `Equals` and the `GetHashCode` it declares or generates, and the `ToString` it declares or generates, else the qualified-name rendering. A value kind with no `Equals` or `GetHashCode` of its own has no witness for that member and does not conform: the inherited member's answer, the box's identity, is not reached through a type parameter (§9.2), and a call through an interface constraint is one. No new syntax, no new keyword, no new mechanism; §9.2's record rule and §35.7's choice rule become one rule, and the hashable row's arm reads the members its consumers reach.

Newly admitted (zero sites each, §6): a record declaring the pair without `=` as a hash key or under `where T: hashable`; a value-like choice declaring the pair without `=` likewise (the compiler admits it today on the auto-hashable ground); a value kind with its own `Equals` and `GetHashCode` (declared or generated) listing an interface that declares an `Object`-signature member. Newly rejected (zero sites each, §6): `=`/`<>` on a value-like choice declaring `Equals` or `GetHashCode` without the overloads; a `ToString()` call or interpolation hole at such a choice's static type where it declares no `ToString`; a value-like choice declaring `GetHashCode` alone as a hash key, `hashable` argument or direct `.Equals` receiver (it was auto-hashable); and, by the admissible-component rule, a record or choice whose auto-hashability rested on a component of such a choice type. Newly defined at run time: nothing — the one answer that changes (`Tag.A(1) = Tag.A(2)`) is now a compile error.

## 1. The problem

### 1.1 Three seams, one cause

Since v0.336 the own-typed `Equals` is the slot on every value kind, and §9.2's second disqualifier reads it "in either spelling". The passages that key on *which members an author wrote* were not all re-read against that:

| Passage | What it says at v0.336 | The seam |
|---|---|---|
| §3.6 hashable row, clause (a) and its definition | "a record that is neither auto-hashable nor declares user equality"; "*declaring user equality* means (`operator =`/`<>` with `Equals(T)`/`GetHashCode` per the §25.2 co-declaration rule)" | `record Key` declaring `Equals(other: Key)` and `GetHashCode()` (0176 obliges the second) is not hashable: `new Set<Key>()` and `Hold<Key>()` under `where T: hashable` are rejected, while `Key.Equals`, `GetHashCode` and the second kind through the slot all exist (§9.2 *Through a type parameter*: "Every **hashable** S has a member for the first kind, for `GetHashCode` and for the second kind" — the converse fails). An enum declaring the same two members is hashable (§10.5). The §14.2 register's equality-search row already says the remedy is "declaring equality on the element type, which makes it **hashable**", which the row's letter contradicts. |
| §35.7, first paragraph | "Methods, computed properties, statics, and operators other than a homogeneous `=`/`<>` neither qualify nor disqualify" | A value-like choice declaring `Equals(other: Tag)` and `GetHashCode` stays auto-hashable. §35.2 carries §10.5's replacement rule, so the declared `Equals` "replaces the compiler-generated default for every consumer"; but §35.7 still describes "The generated `=`" as structural, and §8.4 says `=` dispatches "to the overloaded operator where one is declared, or to `Equals` otherwise". On `Tag` operands the two readings disagree (§8.4: `Equals`, `true`; §35.7: structural, `false`), and through `where T: hashable` the first kind reaches the declared `Equals` under either (`Same<Tag>(x, y)` is `true`), the constraint admitting the type. The matrix's example: |
| §14.2 *Interface conformance*, rules 2, 4, 7; §11.2 | a record, an enum or a choice conforms when it "declares matching members"; a member matches by name, arity and exactly-equal parameter types | `interface IEq method Equals(other: Object?): bool; end` is implemented by a class through `Object`'s inherited member (rule 1: "inherited (a base class declares them)"), overridden or not. A value kind declares no member at `Object?` (§9.2), and the rules name only declared and generated members, so `record R(X: int): IEq` has no witness by the letter — though §8.1's `Equals(other: Object?)` "already reaches every enum value, answered through the box by the own-typed `Equals`" (§10.5). The borrow-flow matrix's R3/C4 note recorded the record case at v0.334 as "must declare the forbidden member", which 0176 turned into "cannot". |

```bezel
public choice Tag
  A(n: int),
  B;
  public method Equals(other: Tag): bool => true;   // replaces the generated Equals (§35.2)
  public method GetHashCode(): int => 0;            // "neither qualify nor disqualify" (§35.7, v0.336)
end Tag;

static class Demo
  public static method Same<T>(a: T, b: T): bool where T: hashable => a = b;  // first kind (§9.2)
  public static method Run()
    var x := Tag.A(1);
    var y := Tag.A(2);
    Console.WriteLine($"{x = y}");             // v0.336: §8.4 says True, §35.7 says False
    Console.WriteLine($"{Same<Tag>(x, y)}");   // v0.336: True either way, Tag being hashable
  end Run;                                     // ▲ v0.337: `x = y` is a compile error; Same<Tag> is True
end Demo;
```

The cause is one: the hashable row and §35.7 still read "user equality" as *the operators*, while since v0.336 the member that defines a value kind's equality is the own-typed `Equals`.

### 1.2 What the compiler does today

- **Hashability of a record** (`isEqualityBearing`, `src/sema/SymbolCollection.cpp:2434–2461`): a record is hashable when it is auto-hashable or `recordHasUserDeclaredEquality` holds, and that predicate (`:2224–2234`) returns true only on an `operator =`/`<>` member. A record declaring the pair without `=` is rejected as a `Set<Key>` element and under `where T: hashable` — the hashable row's letter, with the diagnostic "neither auto-hashable (§9.2) nor declares custom equality (`operator =`/`<>` with `Equals(T)` and `override GetHashCode`, §25.2)" (`checkHashContainerKeys`, `src/sema/TypeResolution_Checks.cpp:4362–4366`; the lit needle `tests/lit/sema/hashable_constraint_2087__non_auto_eq_body_record_rejected_9_2.bzl:14` pins the text).
- **Auto-hashability of a choice** (`isAutoEqualityBearingChoice`, `SymbolCollection.cpp:2276–2330`): the only member-shaped disqualifier is a homogeneous `=`/`<>` overload; a declared `Equals` or `GetHashCode` is not consulted. `choiceHasUserDeclaredEquality` (`:2381–2433`) requires the overload *and* both members. So a value-like choice declaring the pair without `=` is auto-hashable to the compiler: `=` on it is lowered structurally (`emitChoiceEquality`, `src/codegen/LlvmBackend_Exprs.cpp:10200–10226`, whatever members the body declares), a direct `.Equals` call binds the declared method, and the choice is accepted as a hash key — reading 2 of #5917's generic face, the divergence itself, implemented.
- **The record disqualifier** (`isAutoEqualityBearingBodyRecord`, `SymbolCollection.cpp:2188`) reads `Equals` and `GetHashCode` by name, in either spelling: the shape this proposal carries to the choice already exists for the record.
- **Interface conformance** (`checkAbstractImplementations`, `src/sema/TypeResolution_Checks.cpp:7126–`; the obligation walk at `:7845–`): an interface method obligation is matched against the implementing type's own same-name, same-arity members, then checked for parameter-type equality; the one synthesized witness injected is the record's `Equals(T)` for `IEquatable<T>` at the record's own type (`:7769–7775`). No `Object` member is injected for a value kind, so `record R(X: int): IEq` with `IEq.Equals(other: Object?)` is expected to be rejected (no `bezelc` was available to this lane; the probe is named in §7).

## 2. Options

- **A — Status quo** for each seam: the operator pair stays the only user-equality arm (#5915 reading B); §35.7's methods keep neither qualifying nor disqualifying, the generated `=` standing beside a replaced `Equals` (#5917 reading 2); a value kind listing an interface with an `Object`-signature member is rejected at the declaration, having no declared witness (#5916 reading 2).
- **B — Declared equality is user equality** (this proposal; #5915 reading A, #5917's recommendation carried to the hashable row, #5916 reading 1). The pair is the arm on a record and a value-like choice; a declared `Equals` or `GetHashCode` disqualifies a choice as it does a record; the member filling the slot witnesses an `Object`-signature interface member on every kind that has one.
- **C — B, but `=`/`<>` on a value kind that declares its own-typed `Equals` and no overload dispatch to that `Equals`**, as §8.4 does on a class — the delegation reading of #5917's generic face, extended to records.
- **D — B for #5915 and #5917, with #5916's reading 2** (a value kind cannot list such an interface).

## 3. Evaluation

**Soundness.** B opens no boxing position and no borrow route. A hash container over a record or choice declaring the pair copies the value as it copies any key, and the members it consults are the declared pair — the contract the author wrote, exactly what the container consults today when the author also writes `=`. A value-like choice is copyable by classification, so the hashable gate stays "value-like" (§3.6 clause (b)'s first half is untouched; an owning or borrow-bearing choice declaring the pair is as unhashable as before). The interface witness is the member filling the slot: on a receiver boxed to the interface the call is the boxed-receiver call, the one admitted boxing position (§8.6), and through an interface constraint it reaches the same member at the value; the argument is already `Object?`-typed, so no `T`-typed argument holding borrows is boxed to reach it, and a non-`T` argument compares unequal, the answer §9.2's boxed-receiver paragraph and §35.7 already give. A value kind with no member of its own has no witness: the inherited member's answer is the box's identity, which §9.2 keeps off the type-parameter path (it would make `x.Equals(x)` and the stability of `GetHashCode` turn on how an implementation realizes generics), and a constraint call is on that path. (The draft's "otherwise the inherited `Object` member" failed exactly there; corrected at critique.) A: the generated structural `=` beside a replaced `Equals` is a soundness-adjacent divergence (`Set<Tag>` and `=` disagree, §25.2's own ground) left standing. C: sound, but see simplicity. D: sound.

**Simplicity.** B states one rule where §9.2 and §35.7 stated two ("either spelling being the override" on a record; "neither qualify nor disqualify" on a choice), and the hashable row's arm names the members its consumers reach instead of the obligation that happens to produce them. Net +686 spec words (after critique) (§6), most of them the §14.2 witness paragraph (+152) and §35.7's disqualifier sentence restated in full (+108); no stdlib edit, since every stdlib sentence says "neither auto-hashable nor declares user equality" and the term is defined in §3.6. C adds a dispatch rule the record never had: §9.2's "`=`/`<>` are a compile error unless the author also declares the `operator =`/`<>` overloads" is recorded (v0.158) and pinned (`tests/sema/equality_v0158_test.cpp:203`'s `R` among others), §25.2's "it forces the author to *state* all three, so a divergence is a visible choice rather than an accident" is the posture, and the convenience C buys is two overload lines. A keeps three rules and the divergence. D keeps a declaration rule whose only effect is to refuse a value kind the one surface it already answers through its box.

**Consistency.** B reads the enum, the record and the value-like choice alike: declaring the own-typed `Equals` with `GetHashCode` is taking over equality, and every consumer — direct calls, hash containers, the three kinds through a type parameter, boxed dispatch — reaches the declared members (§10.5's replacement rule, now with the same consequence for the generated package on a record and a choice). The one asymmetry B keeps is the enum's `=`, which §10.5 routes through the replaced `Equals` while a record's or choice's `=` needs the overloads; that is §9.2's recorded rule, not this proposal's, and is noted as a question in `Z2.md`. *(Composed at v0.337: filed as a `spec-question` at the landing.)* The interface witness reads the class and the value kinds alike: the slot, in whatever spelling the kind has.

**Migration reach** (§6). B rejects zero measured sites and admits zero measured sites; one banked lit needle changes text with the compiler's diagnostic.

## 4. Decision

**Option B.** The rules, stated at their homes:

> ▲ **§3.6 (hashable row).** *declaring user equality* means declaring the kind's own-typed `Equals` — its `Object` slot (§9.2) — with the `GetHashCode` §8.3 obliges beside it, whether or not an `=`/`<>` overload accompanies them: a homogeneous overload obliges the pair (§25.2's co-declaration rule), and without an overload `=` and `<>` on the type stay §9.2's and §35.7's compile error while the hash containers and §9.2's three kinds reach the members. Clause (b)'s choice arm: a value-like choice outside auto-hashable through its own declared equality — `Equals(other: C)` with `GetHashCode`, with or without a homogeneous `=`/`<>` overload beside them — is hashable on the strength of those members.

> ▲ **§35.7.** Methods other than `Equals` and `GetHashCode`, computed properties, statics, and operators other than a homogeneous `=`/`<>` neither qualify nor disqualify. An `Equals` or `GetHashCode` in either spelling (§35.2) disqualifies the choice exactly as §9.2's second disqualifier disqualifies a record: the author has taken over equality, the declared member replaces the generated one for every consumer, and no generated `=` stands beside it — `=` and `<>` are then a compile error unless the author also declares the operator overloads, the generated `ToString` goes with the package (§8.5), and the choice is **hashable** on the strength of the declared members where it is value-like.

> ▲ **§14.2 *Interface conformance*.** An interface member spelled as one of `Object`'s virtual members (§8.1) — `Equals(other: Object?)`, or `Equals(other: Object)` by §11.2's nullability widening, `GetHashCode(): int`, `ToString(): string` — is matched, under rules 1, 2, 4 and 7, by the member that fills the type's `Object` slot: on a class the inherited virtual member, overridden in either of its spellings or not (§8.3); on a value kind, which declares no member at `Object?` (§9.2), the own-typed `Equals` and the `GetHashCode` it declares or generates — the members §9.2's three kinds reach — and the `ToString` it declares or generates, else §8.5's qualified-name rendering. A value kind with no `Equals` or no `GetHashCode` of its own — outside auto-hashable, the member undeclared — has no witness for that member and does not conform to an interface declaring it (a generic kind's generated members, and so its witnesses, are read at each instantiation, §3.6 rule 2): the inherited member's answer, the box's identity, is not reached through a type parameter (§9.2). On a receiver boxed to the interface the call reaches the witness through the box (§8.6); through a type parameter constrained by the interface it reaches the same witness at the value, whatever an implementation's realization of generics (§11.8); either way an argument that is not a boxed value of the type, `null` included, compares unequal.

Consequences, each already the rule on at least one kind:

1. **A record declaring `Equals(T)` and `GetHashCode` alone is hashable** (§9.2's explicit-equality sentence gains the clause): a `Set<Key>` element, a `Dictionary<Key, V>` key, admitted under `where T: hashable`; `=`/`<>` on it remain the compile error §9.2 already states.
2. **`=` on such a record or choice is not a dispatch to `Equals`** (§8.4 gains the sentence): the concrete use is the compile error, the use through a type parameter at that type is §9.2's first kind and reaches the declared member. #5917's generic face is closed with no `=` left to have two definitions.
3. **A value-like choice declaring the pair loses the generated package**, `ToString` included: §8.5's other-choice bullet lists it in §35.7's non-auto-hashable set, so a `ToString()` call or interpolation hole at its static type is the use-site error until the author declares `ToString()` (one line; §35.2 admits it). A record in the same position falls back to the qualified-name default (§8.5); the choice's use-site error is §35.7's existing rendering rule for the whole non-auto-hashable set, not a new one.
4. **The "grants a call, not a property" sentence** is scoped to the classifications it was written for: the pair grants a call on every classification and the hashable property only where the choice is value-like.
5. **The interface witness** changes nothing for a class (rule 1's inherited member) and admits, on a value kind with its own `Equals` and `GetHashCode`, the conformance its box already honours; a value kind with neither of its own stays unable to conform to an `Equals`/`GetHashCode` interface member, as at v0.336 by the letter. `IEquatable<Object>`'s member is `Equals(other: Object)` under substitution, so a record listing it is matched by the same paragraph; rule 2's "satisfies no `IEquatable<U>` at a `U` other than the record type itself" gains the one exception, saying so.

## 10. Self-critique (adversarial pass)

- **Does the pair-only record open a route to a borrow?** No. Hashability admits the type as a key, and storing a key is a copy into a container slot — a position §9.7 already governs for a record that holds borrows (an equality-skipped class field already makes such a record hashable, §9.2); nothing about *which members* make a type hashable changes where its values may be stored.
- **Is a choice declaring only `GetHashCode` hashable?** No: the arm requires the own-typed `Equals` (a lone `GetHashCode` obliges nothing under §8.3 and supplies no first-kind member). It is non-auto-hashable, `.GetHashCode()` is admitted per member, `=`/`.Equals` are the use-site error — the record's mirror (0176 §8's last case).
- **A generic choice `Tag<T>` declaring `Equals(other: Tag<T>)`.** The disqualifier reads the body, so it is classification-independent (§35.11's check-once discipline); hashability is then per instantiation on the value-like gate, as today.
- **The enum.** An enum declaring `Equals(other: E)` keeps §10.5's model: the member replaces the default "for every consumer", `=` included through §8.4, and the enum stays hashable. §8.4's new sentence names "a record or a value-like choice" and leaves the enum alone. That an enum's `=` reaches a replaced `Equals` while a record's needs the overloads is §9.2's recorded posture, not this proposal's; it is surfaced as a question in `Z2.md`, not decided. *(Composed at v0.337: filed as a `spec-question` at the landing.)*
- **The `ToString` consequence on a choice** (consequence 3). A record losing the package renders by the qualified-name default; a choice draws the use-site error. The asymmetry is §35.7's existing rendering rule for the whole non-auto-hashable set (owning, borrow-bearing, inadmissible payload); this proposal adds one member of that set and no new rule. Zero sites; the remedy is one declared `ToString()`.
- **Interface member `Equals(other: Object)`** (non-nullable). On a class, `Object`'s `Equals(Object?)` satisfies it by §11.2's widening today; the paragraph says so for the slot on every kind. `IEquatable<Object>` listed by a record is therefore satisfied by the member filling the slot; rule 2's "satisfies no `IEquatable<U>` at a `U` other than the record type" would contradict that read alone (the generated `Equals(T)` *is* that member), so it gains the exception (critique fix).
- **A value kind with no member of its own** (critique fix). The draft let "the inherited `Object` member" witness `Equals`/`GetHashCode` on a non-auto-hashable record or choice declaring none. Through `where T: IEq` that is box identity on the type-parameter path, which §9.2 excludes: no box exists to have an identity, an implementation that boxes per call would give `x.Equals(x)` = `False` and a fresh identity hash per `GetHashCode` call. Such a kind has no witness and does not conform; HEAD's letter rejects it already, so nothing moves.
