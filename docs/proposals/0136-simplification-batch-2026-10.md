### 2.2 — The `r = null` seam between §8.4, §25.2 and §7.11 (S1 incidental (d)) — **clarification** (reclassified by critique B8)

**Sites.** `S:1264` (§8.4): "`a = b` invokes `a.Equals(b)`, with a null-safe wrapper: `null = null` is `true`; `null = nonNull` and `nonNull = null` are `false`." `S:6101` (§25.2): "When a record overloads `=` and `<>`, the overloaded operators are invoked for `a = b` and `a <> b` directly — they do not delegate to `Equals`." `S:1196` (§7.11): "Equality is total over an absent operand". `S:1204` (§7.11 *User-defined operators*): "A user-declared `operator` on a record or class type is likewise not defined on a nullable operand: such an operand at a user-defined-operator position is the same compile error." Read in isolation, `S:1204` would reject `r = null` for `record R` with a homogeneous `operator =(a: R, b: R)` and `r: R?`. It does not, because §7.11's own lead (`S:1196`) says "Equality is total over an absent operand and is **unaffected by this section**" — and `S:1204` is a paragraph of that section.
