## 0. The question and its stakes

**The strategy today, as recorded.** Rationale §10: *"Bezel's intended implementation specializes for value-type substitutions (where boxing would hurt) and shares for reference-type substitutions (where uniform layout is achievable)."* The commissioned question: what would it take — and buy — to emit a generic body's machine code **once, at its declaration**, and run it for *value-type* substitutions too, through a per-instantiation **value-witness descriptor** ▲ (size/align, copy-or-move, dispose, default, static-interface member table, `Type` pointer), instead of monomorphizing per substitution?

**The three candidate payoffs** — priced separately below, because §6's designs deliver different subsets, not a package:

- **(a) Total body-locality.** A compiler that emits every generic body at its declaration retains *nothing* past it — no AST, no re-elaboration per instantiation. Generic bodies are today the last construct forcing a compilation to hold earlier program text live while compiling later text (§4.1); removing that makes single-pass ("streaming") compilation possible in principle.
- **(b) A sealed artifact interface.** §2.8.2 prices carried bodies honestly: *"definition-site visibility makes a module's `internal` declarations reached by exported generic bodies part of its **binary** interface even though they are absent from its *source* interface — changing one invalidates dependents exactly as a signature change does … it is the price of monomorphizing generics across artifact boundaries."* Under shared codegen the internal machinery is baked into the declaration's machine code; the carriage requirement, the disclosure, and that staleness class shrink together.
- **(c) Binary size.** One body per declaration instead of one per (declaration × substitution).

**Not in question.** Observable semantics: §11.8 fixes resolution-at-instantiation as *"a statement about *semantics*, not implementation strategy"*, and every design here is measured against §2.8.2 clause 2 — *"An external instantiation behaves identically to a same-module instantiation of the same generic at the same arguments"* — which with §2.8.3 rule 4's as-if clause means Type identity, static state, init timing, disposal order, exceptions, and every instantiation-site rejection must be indistinguishable from specialization. Also not in question: ABI stability. §2.8.5 records it as a non-goal — *"a stable binary ABI is a separate commitment nothing yet motivates"* — and nothing here reopens it. Payoff (b) is scoped strictly *inside* recompile-the-world: it reduces which edits force dependent recompiles (0044 §4's consequence table); it promises nothing across toolchain revisions.

## 1. What the texts commit to

### 1.1 §2.8.2 read as capability versus consequence

The framing sentence is pure capability with explicit format latitude: *"The interface's required capability is semantic; its format is implementation-defined (source text, serialized AST, IR, or any mix)."* Sorting the section: **capability-normative under any strategy** — clause 1 (type-check the public surface, including *"the structural facts needed for per-instantiation evaluation ([§3.6] rule 2) for public generics"*), clause 2 (instantiate at arbitrary external arguments, behaviorally identical), clause 3's first disjunct, the definition-site paragraph's *first* sentence (definition-site visibility — a semantics statement), "No restrictions beyond visibility" sentence 1, the contract-records paragraph. **Carriage-conditional or monomorphization-consequent** — the definition-site paragraph's second sentence, *"The interface must therefore carry whatever internal machinery its exported generic bodies reach"* (a real "must", but marked derived — *"therefore"* — and predicated on bodies being in the interface); its third sentence ("Carried bodies may equally reach…"); clause 3's second disjunct (*"or from any declaration body carried in the interface"*); and the cost paragraph, which names its own cause (*"the price of monomorphizing"*).

**The finding of absence, with the near-misses named** (the highest-burden claim class): **no normative sentence mandates that bodies be carried.** The four closest, and why each falls short: (i) the definition-site "must therefore carry" — derived from the strategy, presupposing bodies; (ii) clause 3's second disjunct — conditioned on "carried", vacuous when nothing is; (iii) §2.8.3 rule 1's gloss (*"where an interface's references include those made by the declaration bodies it carries"*) — conditional, and "include" is non-exhaustive; (iv) the fallback sentence — *"An implementation unable to carry some body shape in its interface format must reject the export, or the import, with a diagnostic naming the shape; it must not alter the instantiation's semantics"* — which, read adversarially, makes a zero-carriage implementation "unable to carry" every shape and obliged to reject every generic export. The defensible reading keys the premise to *clause 2*: shipped shared code satisfies clause 2, so the fallback never fires. §7.2 names this sentence as the study's one candidate clarification-class edit.

An implementation emitting a generic's value-substitution code into the artifact therefore *satisfies* §2.8.2 as written (shipped code is part of "the artifact"; "any mix" plausibly covers it) and renders the carriage-conditional sentences vacuous, not violated. The one near-miss outside §2.8.2: rule 1's build closure is defined by interface *references*, so a sealed artifact whose shipped code links other modules must still *list* them — satisfiable today (the gloss is non-exhaustive) but worth stating, or the missing-module diagnostic degrades from the rule's *"intended standard"* metadata time to a native-link failure.

Two honest limits on payoff (b): sealing is **not** zero disclosure — clause 1 still carries private-field-derived structural facts, and the contract-records paragraph still discloses non-public state named in checked clauses (its closing analogy to the definition-site disclosure would dangle under sealing and needs re-grounding). And §2.8.6 changes meaning: the *"carried generic bodies or carried internal machinery changed, contract records unchanged → preserving — **informational**"* row (*"The regenerable interior"*) becomes inapplicable between sealed interfaces, and the Degradation principle — *"an implementation must not treat absent records as empty record sets"* — needs extending to bodies so a sealed artifact is body-opaque *by declared mode*, not misread as mass interior deletion.

### 1.2 The semantics-not-strategy passages

The spec states generic semantics as results with the mechanism open, and once names the mechanism under study:

> **§11.8:** "Resolution happens **at instantiation** — a statement about *semantics*, not implementation strategy: the binding of `T.Member(...)`, and of constraint-sourced operators ([§25.2]), is fixed per concrete type argument, exactly as if each instantiation were compiled separately. An implementation may realize this by per-instantiation specialization or by shared code carrying a per-instantiation dispatch table — rationale §10's recorded strategy does both, specializing value-type substitutions and sharing reference-type ones — so long as the observable binding is the conforming type's member"

> **§2.8.3 rule 4:** "exactly **one copy of its static state** and **one recorded initialization** … and **one runtime type identity** … Duplicate *code* copies of a monomorphization are permitted only where observationally indistinguishable from one copy."

The first is the licence *and* the conformance bar; the second the general observational rule — code arrangement is free; what is pinned per instantiation is static state, recorded initialization, runtime identity. The changelog records the project choosing this framing deliberately — the v0.181 entry: *"Resolution-at-instantiation restated as **semantics** (binding fixed per concrete type argument, as if separately compiled), not an implementation mandate — un-contradicting rationale §10's specialize-values/share-references strategy."* When spec text and §10 collided, the recorded move made the *spec* strategy-neutral rather than hardening the strategy — dispositive on change class: extending sharing to value substitutions needs at most a rationale amendment, no semantic ruling. The easy-to-miss carrier: §11.8's appositive restates the rationale sentence *inside normative text* and must move in the same revision as any §10 amendment.

### 1.3 Rationale §10 — decline record, or strategy intent?

The crux, argued openly. The reopening rule (spec-change-process.md §2): *"A change is a design change the moment it contradicts a recorded decision (a rationale decline record or a `DECLINED.md` entry). Those are reopened only with *new evidence against the stated reasons*, never by re-asking — and reopening is itself a proposal."*

**Finding: §10's strategy sentence is a strategy intent, not a decline record.** Four grounds. (1) It fails the rationale's own definition of a decline — the document header: *"the recorded form of the answer names the alternative, what it would buy, and why it was declined"* — and the sentence names no alternative to sharing-at-value-substitutions, states no buy, records no decline. The one §10 paragraph that *does* meet the form (*"Two alternatives were declined…"*, the v0.196/#1803 record) is about the `owned` marker, not codegen. (2) Intentional voice ("Bezel's **intended** implementation"), no reopening condition — where every genuine decline in the corpus attaches one (rationale §18's allocator entry, §2.8.5's bullets, D-0001). (3) The v0.181 precedent above. (4) §10 itself declares the observables strategy-independent: *"`typeof(T)` and `value.GetType()` work because the runtime carries enough type information to resolve them, regardless of whether a particular generic was specialized or shared."*

**And the stated reason is not disputed anyway.** §10's reason for specializing value substitutions is *"(where boxing would hurt)"*, priced against a two-point space whose erasure pole *"produces poor codegen and disallows reflection on type parameters."* A witness design boxes nothing (values live inline in witness-sized slots — §12.9's invariance rationale, which rejected per-element boxing, is untouched) and erases no type identity (the `Type` pointer is a descriptor entry). The subject is a **third point the paragraph never weighed**, and every §6 design *keeps specialization available* — so the study composes with §10 rather than contradicting it. That said, §5 finds the reason's *substance* — indirection hurts where specialization inlines — survives on the merits regardless of boxing, which is why §7 does not overturn the hybrid.

A parallel, uncomfortable finding about the implementation: bezelc's total monomorphization was never a recorded decision. 273 `monomorph*` mentions in `src/` include five spelling *"generics are monomorphized"* as a §14.1/§14.2 citation (`src/sema/ExprTypecheck_Calls.cpp:2449`, `src/codegen/LlvmBackend_Calls.cpp:833`, `src/codegen/LlvmBackend_impl.hpp:1828`, and kin) — but §14.1 (v0.261) is "Generic declarations" and §14.2 is "Constraints"; neither says anything about strategy. The current strategy is an implementation choice mis-cited as a spec requirement across many revisions — exactly the silent-decision failure mode CLAUDE.md forbids. Whatever the owner rules, *that* should be recorded; this study is the vehicle.

### 1.4 The postures that constrain any design here

- **§3.6's behavior-dispatch reservation** — the sharpest composition question: *"Dispatch of *behavior* (not merely admissibility) on a derived type property … is reserved to compiler-known standard-library types; it is not a user-available mechanism in this revision; two scoped exceptions are recorded."* Witness-driven copy/move/dispose for arbitrary user generics is exactly this dispatch, generalized. The available argument is the choice-classification exception's own grounds — it *"does flip per-instantiation behavior (copy vs. move on assignment, disposal vs. none at scope end), and is admitted because the dispatch is performed by the language on a language construct — derived from structure, identical for every user, with no user-programmable hook — the recorded generalization path of the compiler-known specializations (rationale §5.15); users still cannot write code conditioned on a type parameter's category"* — the two flipped behaviors named being two of the witness's own entries, and §5.15 recording the door (*"Future revisions may generalize the mechanism if other types benefit, but that is not committed work"*). The bound cuts back: the witness must never be user-addressable, and sharedness never observable from source.
- **Rationale §5.28's temperament** — the likeliest misreading. v0.238 retracted *"a hidden per-type runtime purchased for a feature whose entire recorded value is ceremony"*; §5.9's surviving posture is compiler-inserted frees at statically-known points, no runtime lifetime mechanism. A witness descriptor is **not** that mechanism — no counts, no registry of live objects, no reclamation policy; the disposal *decision* and *point* stay static, only the *operation* is selected per instantiation — but the resemblance must be confronted, not hoped past. §5.28's own content test survives: every §3.1 entry is computed from the substituted type's declaration and arguments (outgoing edges), never a global graph.
- **Two deferred features a careless design ships by accident.** Rationale §18 keeps generic-instantiation decomposition (`GenericArguments`/`GenericDefinition`) *"genuinely **deferred**"*; a witness carries the substituted arguments at runtime as an implementation fact and must not surface them as a user query. And §5.15 defers move-only value T in the buffer family precisely because it *"adds a third category to the slot-semantics dispatch (class T, non-move-only value T, move-only value T) without unlocking a clearly-motivated use case"* — a witness's transfer entries supply that third category as a by-product; taking or fencing it must be explicit.

## 2. The per-instantiation obligation inventory

The question deciding payoff (a): does any *checking* rule require the generic body to be retained past its declaration? The per-instantiation rules fall into four classes. One structural pattern holds throughout: the spec states each check's **result** and its **diagnostic** ("rejected at the instantiation site", with §3.6 rule 4's naming duties), never its mechanism — but diagnostic content *is* mandated, so summaries must be reason-bearing, not boolean.

**Class A — admission checks on the substitution alone (no body facts).** Constraint satisfaction in all forms — *"Constraint satisfaction is a property of the type alone, checked wherever an instantiation occurs"* (§14.2) — plus every signature-keyed re-check: generic `owned` fields (§28.5), owning-collection elements (§14.4), async borrow-bearing parameters (§30.1), move-only task results (§30.5), pure-member slots (§22.15), FFI gates (§32.12/§32.13), choice admissibility (§35.8/§35.11). All consume the declared signature (as a type function of T) plus the argument type's structural facts — which clause 1 already carries. Streaming-safe today.

**Class B — derived-property computation on the generic itself (§3.6 rules 1–2).** Trivial-dispose, sendable, hashable, defaultable, element category, choice classification. Needed: the generic's structural skeleton — never method bodies — and the spec says so: clause 1 mandates *"the structural facts needed for per-instantiation evaluation ([§3.6] rule 2) for public generics."* These are **not** category-finite: defaultable varies *within* the value category (*"an enum with no zero-valued constant included — is rejected at the instantiation site"*, §17.11), so the consumer runs the property computation per concrete argument from carried facts.

**Class C — interior-use checks that DO consume body facts.** The complete set found, each with the ▲ summary that discharges it:

| Rule | Body fact | Summary ▲ / sufficiency |
|---|---|---|
| §7.10 copy-demand at move-only T: *"any operation the generic body uses that requires copy semantics (e.g., reading T twice without an intervening assignment) is rejected at instantiation with a message naming the move-only type"* | the exact site set demanding copy semantics of T | the site set (an inferred implicit "copyable-T" requirement, with provenance). Sufficient: verdict = (substitution move-only) ∧ (set non-empty); must be exact and site-bearing for the mandated message |
| §17.11 `default(T)` use (`new T()`'s value arm equivalent, §14.2) | whether/where the body evaluates `default(T)` | flag + sites; consumer evaluates *defaultable* per argument. Sufficient |
| §28.3 bare-T return admission — *"enforced per instantiation category"*, the spec's own words for the §3.6 rule 2 pattern | per-category verdict + offending sites | verdict vector over the finite category set. Sufficient by the rule's own statement |
| §21.1 const-initializer ownership; §17.4 `as T` degeneracy sites | site lists | per-category verdict vectors. Sufficient |
| §9.2/§35.7 equality use on T | "body compares T" + sites | flag + sites, gated by the hashable computation. Sufficient |
| Transitive instantiation requests (an unconstrained body instantiating `Dictionary<T,int>` etc. imports those generics' obligations) | the body's T-parameterized instantiation-request set | that set, closed recursively over each target's own summary. Sufficient, but the main new interface content a sealed design must define; §14.2's `hashable` row exists precisely so such diagnostics stop landing *"deep inside the stdlib type"* |

The spec already owns the subsumption device: under a derived-property constraint, *"the per-instantiation re-check for those interior uses is subsumed by the constraint"* (§14.2) — a constraint **is** a declaration-time summary. Class C is the same idea, inferred rather than declared.

**Class D — per-instantiation runtime *semantics*** (behavior, not checks) — the witness's charter, worked in §3.

**Conclusion, and the absence claim discharged:** no rule requires retaining a generic body past its declaration for later *checking*. The closest passages, each falling short: §2.8.2's definition-site sentence 2 (strategy-consequent, §1.1); clause 3's *"or from any declaration body carried in the interface"* (carriage-conditional); §21.10's *"a public generic's carried body ([§2.8.2]) carries it as ordinary body content"* (descriptive of the current model, in a rule — instantiation-independent local-type identity — that serves sharing equally). What survives per instantiation is evaluation of carried facts at the consumer (A–C) and runtime behavior selection (D) — neither needs the body. Summary-carriage is already in-style twice over: clause 1's structural facts, and the contract records' canonical token text. One demand-side fact carried from §14.5: inference is provably expression-local (*"Inference runs after arity filtering and named-argument binding … fix the argument-to-parameter correspondence"*; no phase consults the generic body or other call sites), so the instantiations a compilation requests are enumerable while streaming the caller. The *checking* story for payoff (a) is complete; the codegen story is §3's, where the honest limitations live.

## 3. The witness descriptor, worked ▲

Everything here is proposed surface (▲) unless it cites shipped code. The schema is derived entry-by-entry from spec rules that demand it, then stress-tested.

### 3.1 The schema

| ▲ Entry | Demanded by |
|---|---|
| size / align / **stride** | T-typed locals, `ref` params (§14.1's `Swap<T>`), `[N]T` locals over the parameter (§15.1.1) — shared frames contain runtime-sized slots; stride ≠ size is the element spacing. **Hard constraint:** for C-compatible instantiations §32.13 makes the layout *normatively the target C ABI's*, §32.12 scales `Ptr<T>` arithmetic by *"`T`'s target-ABI stride"*, and stdlib §4.14's `DangerousPointer()` hands slot-0 addresses to native code — the witness layout must *equal* the specialized layout; no fattened or indirected shared representation is admissible |
| transfer: copy-init / move-relocate | §9 (assignment copies); §15.3 (move-only assignment transfers; moved-from is *static*, so relocation is bitwise, no drop flag); §7.10 (copy ops rewritten to moves or the instantiation rejected — the copy entry is simply absent at move-only T, and the body never calls it). Copy is not assumable memcpy: §28.9 leaves runtime-managed lifecycle open (*"reference counting, region allocation, or other"*), so a record with `string`/function-typed fields may need a lifecycle-aware copy — the flags word must be representation-layer, not a rename of ownership-layer trivial-dispose |
| dispose | §35.4 (owning choices: tag-dispatched, payload order, *"no per-slot moved-from state to consider"*); §15.3/§15.1.1 (owning inline arrays: each non-null slot, reverse order — over opaque T the moved-from half is vacuous). The entry the shipped compiler already proved necessary: reference-substitution body sharing was tried implicitly and **removed as a soundness bug** because a shared body could not select the right disposer for `using var a := new T()` (§4.1) |
| default / ctor | §17.11 (`default(T)` at unconstrained T; nothing promises all-zero bits — a function or validated zero-fill flag); §14.2 (`new T()` ≡ `default(T)` at value substitutions; a parameterless-ctor pointer serves the class arm) |
| static-interface member table | §11.8's named *"per-instantiation dispatch table"*. Entries are **adapters, not raw pointers**: witnesses are spelled in the *erased* substituted signature (§11.8: at a value conformer *"the witness is spelled without the marker"*), and stdlib §2.19 records that for primitives *"the **compiler-provided conformance bridges the out-nullability difference**"* between the constraint's `out owned value: T?` and the direct `out result: T` — per-entry thunks are already normative precedent |
| `Type` pointer | §17.10 (`typeof(T)` per instantiation, *"with its top-level nullability erased"*); §19.3/§31.2.1 (patterns and catch arms test against the substituted argument); §2.8.3 rule 4 (process-unique). The two identity axes diverge: T = U and T = U? share the Type entry but not their null-handling entries |
| `T?` companion block | §7.10: *"When an unconstrained `T` is instantiated with a nullable type `U?`, the substituted `T?` denotes `U?` itself — there is no two-level optional."* `T?`'s layout cannot be computed as "T plus a flag" — the companion (size, make-null, null-test, wrap, read) is witness *data*; §8.6's nullable-boxing strip rides these entries |
| field-offset vector | required once a generic *type* (not just a generic method) has a T-typed field — every `this._f` in a shared body becomes offset-load + GEP |
| statics base + run-once guard | §22.12: *"Each distinct instantiation has its own static state ([§22.13])"* — reachable from a shared body only through the descriptor. Mitigating fact, and a structural advantage over the CLR: a mutable static of bare `T`/`T?` is already *"a **compile error at the declaration site**, whatever the parameter's constraints or eventual substitutions"* (§22.12) — no static slot's layout depends on T |
| box / unbox; itables | §5.1 (T→bound conversion boxes at value T); §8.6 (fresh wrapper, payload fixed at boxing; move-only types cannot box); §35.8 (for owning choices *"constraint satisfaction and generic dispatch work without boxing"* — mandating borrow-receiver interface tables independent of any box; sound because §9.4 makes every value receiver a borrow) |

The commissioning sketch listed six entries; the worked schema is eleven. The four additions (stride, `T?` companion, offset vector, statics base) each change the cost model, and each is load-bearing: Swift's value-witness table — the closest production precedent — carries equivalents of all four, and its single-payload-enum tag witnesses exist for exactly the `T?` problem.

### 3.2 The calling convention under owned-erasure

Fixed points, from §28.1's bare-type-parameter arm: at class/interface T an `owned T` slot is ordinary transfer; at *"every **other value or runtime-managed** substitution it is **erased by the substitution** — the substituted signature is the unmarked one: no obligation exists to transfer ([§28.9]), parameters and returns pass by value, an `out owned` slot delivers a plain value"*; at move-only T both spellings are by-value-move (§15.3); `where T: value` rejects the marker at the declaration. One convention satisfies all four categories with one body: **caller-materialized, callee-consumed, indirect slots.** The caller evaluates the argument into a fresh T-slot and passes its address; the callee consumes the slot exactly once via witness operations. At class T the materialization *is* the transfer; at plain value T it is the promised by-value copy (into a temporary, never the variable's own address — the caller's binding stays usable); at move-only T it is §15.3's value-move; witness dispose is `Dispose`, no-op, or the slot cascade respectively. Nothing on the wire distinguishes transfer from copy — the distinction lives in caller-side static accounting (call sites always compile against the *substituted* signature, so the caller knows the category even when the callee is shared) and in the witness entries. `owned T` returns use a caller-allocated result slot; `out owned` uses §22.3's expressly-permitted indirection (*"An implementation may pass a hidden reference, but a partial write must never be observable on the exception edge"*), with the exception-edge discharge running the witness dispose.

### 3.3 The genuinely hard part: function-type identity

§28.1: *"the marker does not survive into a substituted function type's identity (`(owned T) -> ()` at `T = int` *is* `(int) -> ()`, [§27.1])"* — and §27.1 makes modifier matching exact with *no* conversion between modified and unmodified slots. A function value flowing between a shared generic frame and concrete code undergoes **no conversion at any site the type system can see**; there is nowhere to hang a reabstraction adapter. The options are exactly two, and both leak outside generics: (i) one uniform, maximally-indirect physical convention for *all* function values — taxing every concrete `(int) -> ()` call in the language; or (ii) reabstraction thunks minted at the generic frame's own boundary, subject to §27.7.1's lambda equality (reference equality on the closure instance) — a thunked value must compare equal to its unthunked original, forcing equality to resolve through an underlying-identity slot. A global function-value ABI decision, the largest cost item in the design, and where "totally body-local" is honestly qualified: reabstraction thunks are residual per-instantiation codegen.

### 3.4 Stress cases — each expressible with a named entry, or an honest limitation

- **Move-only substitutions**: pre-cleared by the spec — copies rewritten or rejected (§7.10), moved-from static (§15.3), disposal a closed per-type function (§35.4/§15.3), boxing unreachable (§8.6), non-boxing interface dispatch mandated (§35.8). The cost is Class C's copy-demand summary plus §1.4's third-category question. The deep constraint: copy-vs-move is a *static dataflow* difference (after a move the source is dead and undisposed; after a copy both live) — one body cannot hold both readings at a site. The sound resolutions: check the shared body under the weakest assumption (§3.6 rule 2's own discipline; Swift's `~Copyable` shape), or share only under copyability-pinning constraints. No surveyed system found a third option.
- **Generic choices and `[N owned]T` locals**: aggregate *size* depends on T, so shared frames need dynamic stack allocation — a mechanism Rust examined for opaque locals and never stabilized, and whose heap-box alternative is worse here (rationale §4.4 makes allocation failure fatal, so boxing puts a fatal-failure site in every shared body). Witnesses must be *compositional* — built at instantiation time from component witnesses — for §8.3's element-wise equality/hash and §15.1.2's iteration.
- **Borrow-bearing substitutions**: zero runtime demand (§9.7's discipline is entirely static rejection); an ordinary copyable witness.
- **Sendability**: fully static gate (§30.14, checked *"at the instantiation or call site under the rules of [§3.6]"*); the crossing is the copy entry; move-only and borrow-bearing are excluded by the property itself.
- **FFI / C-compatible instantiations**: honored only because §3.1 pins the witness layout to the specialized layout; record `Load`/`Store` marshalling (§32.12: *"A record load is not bitwise"*) is the one place a witness needs interior field structure — a marshal entry or a residual per-instantiation thunk.
- **Statics and identity at runtime**: under sharing, §2.8.4 rule 5 — *"exactly one static state and one runtime identity, regardless of link mode and regardless of how many compilations emitted code for the type"* — moves from link-time symbol merging (today's delicate `linkonce_odr` mangle, §4.1) to canonicalizing consumer-built descriptors keyed by §2.8.3 rule 3's identity tuple: the same dedup problem, on small data instead of code. Rule 6 names monomorphization as where the guarantee *"is *not* free"*; sharing makes the module-code half trivially true. Rule 6's deferred clause (static-init dependency edges the interface *"does not yet carry"*) must, when it lands, be statable without bodies — schematic per-generic edges — or sealed artifacts cannot carry it; a design-time seat to claim.
- **Polymorphic recursion** must stay a compile-time rejection. `F<T>` calling `F<Box<T>>` is compilable under runtime witness construction — which would drag in exactly the runtime metadata cache this design's biggest advantage over Swift is *not* having. Because rationale §5.18 forbids dynamic instantiation, the program's instantiation set is statically closed and every witness/Type record can be a static, linker-uniqued symbol: zero runtime allocation, zero cache. Keeping the set closed is a design invariant, not a free fact.

### 3.5 What already ships

The strongest existence proof is in-tree: **the language's most-used generics are already shared-code-with-witness.** The runtime collections are shared C parameterized by exactly the proposed facts — `bz_array_alloc(elem_size, initial_cap)` / `bz_array_free(arr, dispose_elem)`, and `bz_dict_alloc_generic(int64_t key_size, uint64_t (*hash)(const void*), bool (*eq)(const void*, const void*))` (`src/runtime/bezel_runtime.h:313`) — a literal (size, hash, eq) witness minted per key type, exercised by the whole suite. Per-instantiation runtime type identity also exists: the `instanceDesc_` descriptor globals (#1843, keyed by a recursive structural identity string) give every constructed generic type a process-unique address consumed by `is`/`as`/catch/§19 — the `Type` slot's seed, minus size/copy/dispose. What does *not* ship: `typeof`/`GetType` are hard compile errors (#487), so rationale §10's "the runtime carries enough type information" sentence is aspirational today — the witness would be its implementation vehicle, not its beneficiary.

## 4. What it buys

### 4.1 Against the compiler as it exists (measured facts, not estimates)

1. **bezelc implements neither half of rationale §10's hybrid.** Monomorphization is total — and reference-substitution *code* sharing was tried implicitly and deliberately removed as a soundness bug: `specializedMethods_`' cache key (`src/codegen/LlvmBackend_impl.hpp:1826–1846`) adds a per-type identity component precisely so `Maker<A>.Run` and `Maker<B>.Run` do *not* share a body — the erased `[ptr]` key made a shared body pick the wrong disposer for `using var a := new T()`. That recorded counterexample argues *for* witnesses (a dispose slot is exactly what it lacked) and fixes the floor (a shared body without one is unsound). Layout *does* share across reference substitutions (`specializedStructs_` keys on substituted LLVM field types); code does not.
2. **The artifact interface is implemented, and carries generic bodies as raw source text** — `methodBodyText`/`ctorBodyText`/`dtorBodyText`/`fieldInitText` (`src/module/ModuleMetadata.hpp:497` and the v5 schema block). The importer pays a full front-end re-run — fresh lexer, parser, three sema passes — per imported generic method (`src/module/ModuleLoader.cpp:2203–2270`), in a synthetic context holding *only the one type*, so any body reaching a sibling type, module function, or other import is declined: §2.8.2's definition-site carriage clause has **no implementation**, and clause 2 is not met — a family of decline gates (#783, #1937, #662/#711, #2884) stands in for it. The honest framing: §2.8.2 is implemented in its most expensive admissible format, and the cross-artifact generic requirement is still open after the whole #428-family effort. Shared codegen closes clause 2 *by construction*: the exporter compiles the body once; the importer calls it.
3. **A library artifact pays for a generic method three times**: a dead `ExternalLinkage` erased template whose bare T lowers to `i32` (`src/codegen/LlvmBackend_Layout.cpp:1331` — the placeholder layout behind the #2840/#2860/#2873/#2882/#2889/#2897 bug family, and a symbol codegen actively defends against ever calling); the source text in metadata; each importer's re-monomorphized copy. The first is a free binary-size win before any witness work.
4. **Payoff (a) is real and is a consequence, not an increment.** Lazy specialization is a re-entrant emitter call mid-way through the *caller's* half-emitted body, saving/restoring ~25 pieces of emitter state (#2281's frame-preservation note, `LlvmBackend_Calls2.cpp`); expression-bodied generic methods are textually inlined with no callable symbol at all. Body-locality is impossible today by architecture, not policy — and §2 shows nothing in the *spec* requires that architecture.

### 4.2 Quantified expectations (approximate, prior-art-sourced)

Compile model: the sealed-interface win deletes "one front-end re-run per imported generic method per importing compilation" plus the §2.8.3 rule 3 staleness class for body-reached internals — 0044 §4's row *"Body edit of a public generic member, or of internal machinery reached by carried generic bodies → recompile"* moves to the early-cutoff row. Binary size: Rust-ecosystem hand-de-monomorphization commonly reports 10–40% on specific binaries; linker ICF on template-heavy C++ binaries 3–15% (both approximate — and ICF is the *zero-risk competitor* that must be measured first, or payoff (c) is over-attributed to witnesses). Bezel-specific advantages over the Swift precedent, each grounded in a recorded rule: no reflection metadata and no runtime metadata instantiation at all (rationale §5.18's closed instantiation set → static linker-uniqued witnesses); no opaque-sized statics (§22.12's bare-T ban); a collapsed witness matrix (explicit moves and drop-on-overwrite let Swift's four copy/assign entries be emitted in the body — Rust's `{size, align, drop}` vtable floor is the nearer comparison); and §2.8.5's recompile-the-world model keeps the whole strategy reversible in a way Java's erasure commitment famously was not.

## 5. What it costs

1. **Runtime indirection — rationale §10's reason stands on the merits.** The measured story is consistent across every system that shipped sharing: the overhead is not the copy/dispose witnesses, it is *constraint dispatch* becoming an opaque indirect call that blocks inlining and everything downstream. Swift's unspecialized value-type loops run roughly 2–10× slower (approximate), worst for tiny operations; Go's dictionary calls are neither inlined nor devirtualized, and 1.18-era generic code was sometimes *slower* than the interface code it replaced — a documented, accepted tradeoff. §11.8's own showcase, `Sum<T> where T: INumber<T>`, is the worst case: today `T.Add(a, b)` at `T = int` monomorphizes to a direct, foldable call (`LlvmBackend_Calls2.cpp`'s §11.8 redirect); under a witness it is an indirect call with operands by address — 5–20× on an integer accumulation loop is the honest expectation (extrapolated, approximate). Two industrial verdicts must be quoted, not elided. The CLR (Kennedy & Syme) shares reference instantiations and specializes every value-type one, because sharing value types requires boxing or size-parameterized indirect code — though the disanalogy is real: a JIT pays nothing in shipped-artifact size, so payoff (c) was absent from the CLR's calculus, and Bezel is AOT. And Swift — the one system a stable-ABI mandate *forced* down this road — marks a very large fraction of its own standard library's generic surface `@inlinable`/`@_specialize` because the shared path was too slow for its collections and numeric code. The flagship could not eat its own dog food; any claim that Bezel's stdlib generics can go through witnesses is contradicted by the closest production evidence.
2. **Sealing and specializer recovery are in tension.** Every shipped sharing scheme also ships a specializer, and every specializer needs the body — within-module always, cross-module only where bodies are published. Bezel's one structural escape: §2.8.5's model makes whole-program specialization at final link philosophically free (the boundary is a build-time contract, not a compatibility promise) — but then the compiler carries *both* codegen paths, colliding with CLAUDE.md's recorded posture that simplicity beats speed until functional completeness, and the compile-time payoff nets out only where the runtime loss is accepted.
3. **Runtime machinery of real scope**: descriptor canonicalization across artifacts (replacing the delicate `linkonce_odr` merge whose ODR argument needed the #2871 fix to stay sound — not obviously easier, different); witness-mediated exit disposal reachable from §22.13's recorded-order registry; dynamic frame layout; and attribute loss at indirect call sites — LLVM cannot infer `readonly`/`nounwind` through a function pointer, so `pure` (§22.15) and contract facts must be stamped per call site or shared code silently loses optimizations monomorphized code gets free, widening the gap beyond raw call overhead.
4. **The function-type ABI decision (§3.3) leaks outside generics** — the one cost taxing code that never touches a generic.
5. **The interface grows summaries even as it sheds bodies** (§2 Class C), each exact and reason-bearing under §3.6 rule 4, including the recursive transitive-request closure.
6. **Regression surface**: 569 of ~1,795 test files mention "generic", 126 mention "monomorph", and the lit/FileCheck track pins IR shapes including `$spec` names (#2928 records them as load-bearing evidence). The largest change surface in the project outside disposal.

## 6. The alternatives, worked

Rubric: payoffs delivered / spec impact / bezelc impact / runtime cost / verdict.

**A — Status quo: monomorphize everything, carry bodies as source text.** Delivers none of (a)/(b)/(c); body-locality holds everywhere *except* generic bodies — a bounded, known violation. Spec impact: none, though the §14.1/§14.2 mis-citations should be corrected and the strategy *recorded* regardless (§1.3). bezelc impact: the clause-2 gate family stays open on this road until the definition-site carriage clause is actually implemented — the one §2.8.2 obligation this strategy has not closed after many revisions. Runtime cost: zero; the best codegen. **Verdict: the default, honest about its debts.**

**B — Compiler-internal streaming: retain canonical *token text*, re-elaborate per instantiation.** Keep monomorphization; change only what is *retained*: after a declaration compiles, keep its body as canonical token text (the §2.8.2 contract-records precedent — the interface already defines canonical token sequences with positional references, an equivalence, and carriage machinery) plus the Class C summaries; discard AST and sema state; an instantiation re-elaborates from tokens. Within §2.8.2's *"source text, serialized AST, IR, or any mix"* latitude, **zero spec impact** — a memory-representation choice. Delivers a *weak* (a) — bounded retention: tokens, not live front-end state; bezelc's importer already proves per-method re-elaboration works, since that is literally what `parseAndAttachMethodBody` does across artifacts, and B applies the same mechanism within a compilation minus the one-class synthetic-context restriction — and none of (b)/(c): bodies still cross the artifact boundary, disclosure and staleness stand. **Verdict: pure implementation freedom, available any time, no ruling needed.**

**C — Witness baseline + specialization as pure optimization (the Swift-resilience shape).** Shared witness bodies are the semantics-bearing baseline; the compiler specializes freely where it sees the body (always in-module; at final link cross-module, riding §2.8.5's model), and may pre-emit named specializations into artifacts (the `@_specialize(exported:)` shape). Delivers (a) and (b) fully, (c) largely; keeps hot in-module paths fast. Costs: everything in §5 — both codegen paths, the function-type ABI decision, the full descriptor machinery, the Class C records; cross-module *unspecialized* calls pay the multiplier until link-time specialization exists. Spec impact: §2.8.2's carriage-conditional sentences re-scoped, §2.8.3 rule 1's shipped-code listing, §2.8.6's body-opaque class, rationale §10 amended, §11.8's appositive moved. **Verdict: the real design if the payoffs are ever needed; far too much machinery on zero measured pressure.**

**D — Per-module opt-in sealing.** Default unchanged; a module may declare its artifact **sealed** ▲: its exported generics' value substitutions switch to witness codegen and body carriage drops for that module — with §2.8.2's fallback pattern generalized: a body shape the witness scheme cannot express rejects *the seal* with a diagnostic naming the shape (the compile-time-never-runtime discipline the fallback sentence already prescribes, and the failure mode Mono's gsharedvt teaches to avoid — its uncovered shapes historically surfaced as *runtime* AOT failures). Delivers (b) for the modules that want it and pay for it, (c) partially, (a) not at all (both paths persist; the unsealed default still retains bodies). The evidence-gathering path: real measurements of the witness multiplier at real module boundaries without committing the language. Costs: the whole §3 mechanism *plus* a mode split — two behaviors for one surface, a per-module fork in artifact semantics, §2.8.6 classification across the seal boundary. **Verdict: the least-commitment road to evidence, but it still fronts the full mechanism cost; premature while there is not one external consumer to seal against.**

## 7. Recommendation, with confidence

**Recommendation: record, adopt nothing normative now. Confidence: high on the findings, medium-high on the disposition.**

1. **Resolve the commissioned question and record the answer.** The resolution is a finding of *absence*: no normative text blocks shared codegen at value substitutions — §11.8 licenses the mechanism by name, §2.8.2's carriage language is capability-stated or strategy-consequent (§1.1's four near-misses), §2.8.3 rule 4 pins only state/init/identity, and rationale §10's strategy sentence is intent, not a decline (§1.3). **No spec edit is needed to keep the door open; the door was never closed.** The recording that *is* owed, independent of strategy: the implementation should stop citing §14.1/§14.2 as monomorphization's authority (a comment sweep plus an issue), and rationale §10 may gain one sentence noting the strategy is intent, with this study as the record of the alternatives' pricing — a rationale amendment through the docs workflow, not an implementation PR.
2. **One candidate clarification-class edit, named but not adopted:** §2.8.2's *"An implementation unable to carry some body shape in its interface format must reject the export, or the import…"* admits the adversarial zero-carriage reading (§1.1). If any sharing branch is taken up, re-key the premise to clause-2 capability, generalizing the tail — reject with a named-shape diagnostic, never alter semantics — to shapes a witness scheme cannot express. Until then it is a latent ambiguity with no live trigger, and this study is its record.
3. **Alternative B is implementation freedom, available immediately** — token-text retention with per-instantiation re-elaboration needs no ruling, changes no observable, and removes the worst of the retained-state cost whenever the compiler team wants it: the streaming tier's first step regardless of what happens to C/D.
4. **Defer C and D behind named reopening triggers**, so any re-ask is steered rather than perennial: (i) measured compile-time or binary-size pressure at scale — a real Bezel codebase where monomorphization dominates the build or the artifact, measured, not projected; (ii) external-user demand at artifact boundaries — consumers whose rebuilds the §2.8.2 staleness class actually costs (today there are none, which is also why §2.8.5's ABI bullet is unmeetable and stays untouched); (iii) the streaming-compiler tier becoming a real workstream, at which point §2's inventory and §3's schema are its starting documents. Any taking-up must arrive with the measurements this study could not honestly produce: the `Sum<int>` multiplier on bezelc's own codegen, the ICF baseline for payoff (c), and the `Weak<T>` bitwise-move question (§28.9's lifecycle latitude) that decides whether the transfer entry is memcpy.

**Why not higher than medium-high**, the doubt visible: the §3.6 behavior-dispatch argument (§1.4) is available but unruled — an owner who reads the reservation as bounding the *mechanism* rather than the user surface makes C/D contradictions, not compositions, and this study cannot settle that alone. The performance findings lean on prior-art multipliers not reproduced on bezelc (a wrong number being worse than none, they are flagged, not asserted). And §1.3's crux determination, though argued on four grounds, is contestable — a reader who takes §10's sentence as settled will demand measured evidence against "codegen quality at value substitutions" before entertaining C/D; the triggers above are written so any future re-ask carries exactly that evidence.

**If the ruling is instead to adopt something now** — most plausibly D — this study is the design record, and §3 fixes the first obligations: the dispose entry (the recorded soundness floor), the four schema additions, the seal-rejection diagnostic before the mechanism, the closed-instantiation-set invariant. **If the ruling declines even the recording**, the study still stands as the folder's record of what the texts were found to commit to at v0.261 and why the door needs no edit to stay open — so the next reviewer starts from §2's inventory and §3.3's function-type problem rather than from "is this even allowed", which is finished.

## 8. Companion note: the body-locality invariant (out of scope, one paragraph)

§2's inventory supports a design invariant worth minting independently of any codegen strategy, as a candidate Appendix-C-adjacent ratchet ▲: *no rule of the specification may require information from inside a later declaration's body to check an earlier one, and no rule may require re-elaborating a body per use — except generic instantiation, whose per-use work is confined to (i) evaluating declaration-computable facts against the substitution and (ii) selecting runtime behavior, never re-checking the body.* Every rule surveyed in §2 already satisfies it; §31.2.1's split (*"checked at the generic declaration … only the *matching* is per instantiation"*) and §35.11's check-once discipline (*"Method bodies of a generic choice type-check once … classification-dependent *admissibility* rules … are enforced at the instantiation or use site"*) are its cleanest existing statements. Minting it would turn an emergent property into a reviewable obligation on future rules — the move §3.6's closing sentence already makes for structural classifications. Out of this study's scope; recorded so the streaming tier, if it opens, starts from a named invariant rather than a rediscovery.
