## 0. Summary

Since v0.250 (proposal 0091) two same-name members may differ only by `owned` on an ordinary parameter — `Append(item: Widget)` beside `Append(owned item: Widget)` — and a call between them is resolved by the argument's ownership classification under four applicability clauses, with a 460-word **declaration guard** deciding at which parameter types such a pair may be declared at all (the erasure test: every position must be a class, an interface, or a type parameter whose constraints exclude every substitution at which §28.1 erases the marker). The lift was sound, and its premise — that `M(move x)` and `M(x)` are visibly distinct at every call site — still holds.

What the subtraction review measured is that nothing uses it. The standard library declares **zero** same-type pairs differing only by ordinary `owned` (the `WhenAll`/`WhenEach` merge the lift was meant to pay for is type-distinguished and stays; `FromResult`/`FromOwnedResult` never merged, the guard itself keeping them apart). The test corpus holds three pair declarations in test programs and four in rule cases, every one a test of the rule rather than a program needing it. Against that reach §22.6 carries about 1,500 words of rule — the four clauses, a decidability argument, conjunctive composition across positions, the guard, a §11.3 reading of `where` constraints for the guard alone, a §2.8.6 caveat for the owned member added beside a plain one, and a remedy-naming ambiguity diagnostic — and the compiler carries a guard that has already over-rejected once (#4939).

This proposal retires the lift. `owned` never distinguishes overloads, on an ordinary parameter as on an `out` parameter, and a pair differing only by it clashes at the second declaration. The workaround is the one the library has used all along and the one proposal 0091 itself kept for every `out` pair: a borrowing form and an owning form of one operation are different operations and take different names (`TryGetValue`/`TryPopValue`; `Append(item)` beside `Adopt(owned item)`); a dual whose parameter types already differ stays an ordinary overload. No call site changes shape — a single declared member takes `M(move x)` or `M(x)` under §22.3 exactly as before, and a fresh producer is passed unmarked to either. §22.6 loses about 1,500 words; §11.3, §2.8.6, §14.2, §22.3 and Appendix C.1 each lose a clause. This is a §10 subtraction, defended by no site.

## 1. The rule, in the text

§22.6 (v0.337) opens: "Overload resolution considers number, types, the `ref`/`out` modifiers, the presence of `owned` on an **ordinary parameter**, and the number of type parameters a method declares of its own — overloads differing in any of these are distinguishable (the ordinary-`owned` case under the declaration guard below …)". The subsection *`owned` and overload resolution* then states the four exhaustive applicability clauses ("An owned production is applicable only to the `owned` candidate", "A borrow is applicable only to the plain candidate", a context-typed production and an unclassified argument "applicable to both"), the decidability paragraph, the no-ranking-change-and-conjunctive-composition paragraph, the `Registry` example, the fresh-production reasoning, and the guard: "A pair differing only by ordinary `owned` is declarable exactly when, at every differing position, the declared type — nullability stripped — is a **class type**, an **interface type**, or a **type parameter whose constraints exclude every substitution at which the pair collapses**". §11.3 reads the guard into the clash rule and into its *Generic methods* paragraph ("constraints matter only where §22.6's declaration guard reads them"); §14.2 names the guard as a reader of its conformance computation; §22.3's marker bullet ends with the pair's resolution; §2.8.6 keys member matching on ordinary-parameter `owned` and records the re-routing caveat; Appendix C.1 carries the invariant row.

The stdlib's §1 row *Borrowing/owning duals are overloads* states the convention the lift was adopted for, and names its two survivors: `Task.FromOwnedResult`, "which marks its *parameter* because its would-be pair with `FromResult` is undeclarable under §22.6's declaration guard", and the return-side `ParseOwned` family.

## 3. Options

| | Option | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| A | **Status quo** | Keep the lift, the four clauses and the guard. |
| B | **Retire the lift** (this proposal) | `owned` never distinguishes overloads; a pair clashes; distinct names or distinct parameter types. |
| C | **Concrete types only** | Keep the pair at class and interface types; drop the guard and the generic arm; keep the clauses. |
| D | **Marker-only routing** | Keep the pair; route `M(move x)` to the `owned` member and everything else — fresh producers and `null` included — to the plain one. |

## 4. Evaluation

| Criterion | A | B | C | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Soundness** | sound (the v0.250 argument) | sound: one member per name, §22.3's marker unchanged | sound | unsound at a storing callee: a fresh producer routed to the plain member is a call-bounded borrow of a temporary that dies at the statement boundary (the §15.4 fresh-store hazard; the reason v0.250 routed fresh productions to the `owned` member) |
| **Simplicity** | ≈1,500 words of rule, a compiler guard | the rule is one sentence, uniform with `out owned` and `scoped`; the guard, its §11.3 and §14.2 readings and the §2.8.6 caveat go | halves the text, keeps the clauses, the composition rule, the caveat and the remedy diagnostic | keeps the clauses with a different routing table |
| **Consistency** | `owned` sometimes distinguishes (ordinary, under a guard), sometimes not (`out`) | `owned` never distinguishes, in either position | the erased-marker collapse at a type parameter becomes the one place `owned` clashes while elsewhere it distinguishes | an argument kind resolves silently where every other kind resolves by type |
| **Migration reach** (§2) | none | two gtest rows, one lit program, four banked rule cases, one doc-corpus block; zero stdlib members; zero documents outside §22.6's example | the same, less the two `where T: IDisposable` sites | none |
| **Expressiveness** | a same-name borrow/take pair | lost: the same-name pair (zero sites); kept: the operation-named pair, the type-distinguished pair | kept at concrete types | kept, differently routed |

**Decision: B.** The judgement's criterion — retire where measured reach is negligible and the workaround is a short, local, obvious rewrite with no loss of safety — is met on every count: the reach is six test sites and no program; the rewrite is a name; nothing becomes less safe (the marker discipline is per member, not per pair). C keeps the apparatus for the same zero sites; D is unsound. The v0.250 record's soundness argument is not contested and is not the ground: a sound mechanism nobody uses is exactly what the §10 review exists to find.

What B does not change: §22.3's call-site marker (a named owned binding is still written `M(move x)` at an `owned` parameter and `M(x)` at a plain one; a fresh producer stays unmarked); overloads distinguished by parameter type, arity, `ref`/`out` or a method's own type-parameter count; the stdlib's merged `WhenAll`/`WhenEach`; §11.2's exact modifier matching between an interface member and its implementation; the `out owned` and `scoped` halves of §22.6, which stand as they have since v0.250.

## 5. Soundness

After this proposal every overload set is resolved from parameter types, arity, `ref`/`out` and type-parameter count alone, as it was before v0.250, and the ownership classification of an argument is consulted only by §22.3's marker rule at the member resolution selects. There is no state in which a call reaches a member whose ownership mode the caller did not spell: at an `owned` parameter the marker is required on a named owned binding (K3.13), and a fresh producer is owned under either spelling (K1.5). No value is consumed silently and no borrow is handed to an owning slot. The retired clauses decided nothing a single member cannot decide; they only chose *between* two members, and the choice is now the declaration's.

**Cells.** `docs/research/generics-matrix/runs/v0.336.json` cell **R1/C4** quotes the guard ("a class bound (`where T: SomeClass`) qualifies — only class types satisfy it") as one of several fragments grounding a type-parameter-bound verdict; the verdict does not turn on the guard and is unchanged, and the fragment is listed as a re-quote for the next re-run. No disposal or borrow-flow cell quotes the lift.

## 9. Self-critique (adversarial pass)

(a) *Is this a reversal of a recorded decision, and is the new evidence real?* Yes and yes: proposal 0091 is graduated, and §2 of the process reopens a recorded decision only with new evidence against its stated reasons. 0091's stated reason for lifting was visibility (answered — the marker stands) *and* the stdlib program (the merge of `WhenAllOwned`/`WhenEachOwned`). The merge survives this proposal untouched, because it was type-distinguished; the lift's own capability has been measured at zero library uses and six test uses over 87 revisions. That measurement is the evidence, and the §10 review is the bar it is held to. (b) *Does retiring the pair lose safety anywhere?* No: the pair never made a call safer; it chose between two members whose ownership modes a caller already had to spell. (c) *Is "distinct names" really a short, local rewrite?* For the six measured declarations, one identifier each; call sites of a renamed owning member change their callee name, not their shape. (d) *Could a user instead keep one member and lose a form?* A single `Append(owned item)` takes `move w` and `new Widget()`; a single `Append(item)` borrows `w` and takes a call-bounded borrow of a fresh temporary — both legal today and unchanged. (e) *The `where T: IDisposable` pair the compiler was fixed to admit (#4939) now rejects again.* That fix was an over-rejection repair; under this proposal the rejection is the rule, and the fix's helpers go with the guard (the tracker names them). (f) *Anchors.* `sec-owned-and-overload-resolution` is kept on the rewritten subsection (the changelog's v0.250 and v0.251 links keep resolving); the heading text is kept; `sec-practical-pattern` and `sec-implicit-conversion-ranking` are untouched. (g) *Fragments.* Every rule-corpus `rule:` fragment homed in §22.6 at HEAD still resolves except `s22_6_0018`'s, whose case flips; the generics-matrix fragment R1/C4 is a listed re-quote. (h) *Scope seam.* §2.8.6's two edits are inside single sentences; lane W3 relocates §2.8.6 as a whole — the composition applies this lane's two sentence-level edits before or after W3's move by re-anchoring, and the md notes it.
