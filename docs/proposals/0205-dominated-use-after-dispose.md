## 0. Summary

Today the spec rejects a read of `a` after `a.Dispose()` only when the disposal was *conditional* (K7.4's maybe-consumed), draws a mandatory **warning** when it dominates the read (§28.2), and says nothing at all about a read **through a borrow** of `a`'s value after a dominating `a.Dispose()`: `var b := a; a.Dispose(); Console.WriteLine(b.Name);` compiles silently and faults as `DisposedAccessException` in a checked build (exit 70) — and in an unchecked build it segfaults (e1, e9, p01, p04, p05, p11, p13), or **prints the wrong object's name** where the freed block was reused (e5 prints `second`, e7 prints `next`), or prints nothing at all (p03, p06). §28.3.9 explicitly endorses the shape for a span: *"`var s := xs.AsSpan(); xs.Dispose(); Console.WriteLine(s[0]);` compiles, and the read faults"*.

This proposal makes a use — read, member access, argument, re-borrow, consume — **a compile error wherever the explicit disposal is certain to precede it**: (i) a use of the disposed variable itself that the `Dispose()`, direct `DisposeAsync()` or discard dominates on every path (today's §28.2 warning, promoted), and (ii) a use of a **local borrow of the variable's value** — an alias, a `const`, a `using var` pin, a span or borrow-bearing record or choice over it, a closure capturing such a holder, an iterator or query frame carrying it — taken while the variable held that value, whose **every** source was so disposed on every path to the use (a closure over the variable itself reads the variable when invoked, §27.5, and is the same-variable case: §9 item 11). The disposal itself stays legal (K5.3 is untouched; dispose-after-last-use never changes verdict), a disposal on only some paths to the use stays the checked-mode fault, and one use is exempt: a **repeated** explicit disposal of the same binding, the runtime no-op §28.2 defines, stays a warning. A reassignment of the variable between the kill and the use does not lift the error (the borrow still names the disposed value); a re-borrow after the reassignment is a fresh borrow and is admitted (e6/p14).

**Why reverse the decline.** Rationale §5.1 (v0.180) declined *"freezing explicit `Dispose()` too — converts this section's recorded trade-off into a de-facto borrow checker for no soundness gain (the backstop is real)"*. (b) does not freeze the disposal: it rejects a use the disposal makes certain to fault, which is what §5.1's own statement of the goal — programmers *"need 'free this once and don't use it after'"* — asks the compiler to say where it can. The "no soundness gain" ground was measured against a metric that counts a checked-mode fault as a success; against the spec's other conformance mode the same program is undefined behaviour, and the measured runs show it reading a different object silently. The analysis (b) needs is the §28.2 dominance analysis the compiler already runs, extended along the borrow provenance it already computes for K5 — the same liveness fact the owner-approved 2026-10-27 amendment of #4602 (master plan §4.8) now has the compiler compute at every `Dispose()` site to free the object in full. v0.184 already stepped past §5.1's posture once on the same reasoning, for the conditional kill: *"per-path tracking exists anyway, the precision is free"*.

**Measured on the kernel model** (`bzcore`, 3 variables, length 4 with a branch; 67,002 programs, 12,327 accepted): (b) newly rejects 345 programs; every one of the 189 that fault on every run is caught and so is every one of the 48 whose conditional use faults whenever it runs; the other 108 are dead aliases (`a.Dispose(); var c := a;` with `c` never read). It admits nothing new. The full freeze (c) rejects 60: 6 certain faults, 24 conditional-use faults, 30 dead aliases — and leaves the certain fault `new a; a.Dispose(); var b := a; b.Use()` admitted, since no borrow is live *at* the kill. The executable kernel model (bzcore L1, §4.5) confirms the shape of the result on its own populations: under (b) no accepted program faults on every execution any more (132 → 0 with a branch, 354 → 0 with a loop), nothing is newly admitted, nothing becomes unsound, and the only certain-fault programs (c) catches are the six whose borrow is live at the kill.

**Measured reach in the tree** (§5): ten banked rule-corpus rows change expectation (the same-variable warning rows, the checked-fault `run` rows that exist to observe the backstop, and stdlib §6.6's `l6_6_0004`, banked `fail`); about 57 ownership gtest rows that assert one of the §28.2 warning texts and about 35 runtime rows that observe the checked-mode fault through a straight-line shape flip to a rejection (re-measured by the critic; the draft's ~40 and ~28 undercounted); three lit files; no guide, specification or standard-library example other than the §28.3.9 span sentence and Guide 05 §7's `buf.Dispose()` sentence states the endorsed shape.

## 1. The text today, and what it leaves open

> The compiler additionally **must** emit a compile-time warning when a local variable is used (read, borrowed from, or consumed) after an explicit `Dispose()` or direct `DisposeAsync()` call […] — or a discard of the binding […] — on the same variable that dominates the use site on every path: the straight-line subset of use-after-dispose (derives: K4.2 + K7.4). […] A *conditional* kill is treated more strictly: at a join of an explicitly-disposed path with a still-owned path the variable is **maybe-consumed** and a later use is a compile error (§28.3). **The split is between the two kills, never between the use forms** […]
>
> — §28.2, *Use after disposal; checked disposal mode*, v0.345

> Use of an instance after its first `Dispose()` is a program error. How the error manifests depends on the *disposal-checking mode* […] **Unchecked (release) mode** — use of a disposed instance is undefined behavior; a build selects it explicitly.
>
> — §28.2, v0.345

> the K4.2 site-announced kills (rows K3.5, K3.8, K3.9) are unfrozen — each **satisfies (i)**, and **satisfies (ii)** wherever the surviving borrows are backstopped by the kill's own flag
>
> — §28.3.1 K5 clause 3, v0.345

> `var s := xs.AsSpan(); xs.Dispose(); Console.WriteLine(s[0]);` compiles, and the read faults as `DisposedAccessException` in checked disposal mode, exactly as a read through any other surviving borrow of `xs` does […] Dispose after the span's last use.
>
> — §28.3.9, *Span-carried borrows and the site-announced kills*, v0.345

> Borrow **liveness** is enforced statically for locals, returns, and captures *up to explicit disposal*: […] an **explicit `Dispose()`** of a borrowed-from source is deliberately not statically tracked (rationale §5.1's recorded trade-off […]): use through a surviving borrow raises `DisposedAccessException` in checked disposal mode and is undefined behavior unchecked.
>
> — §28.3.12, v0.345

> Alternatives declined: (a) freezing explicit `Dispose()` too — converts this section's recorded trade-off into a de-facto borrow checker for no soundness gain (the backstop is real)
>
> — rationale §5.1, the v0.180 record

Four things are decided: the kill is legal under a live borrow (K5.3); a conditional kill of the variable makes its later use an error (K7.4); a dominating kill of the variable makes its later use a warning (§28.2); and the full freeze is declined (§5.1). One thing is not: **a use through a borrow that the kill dominates**. §28.2's paragraph reaches "the same variable" only; §28.3.9 and §28.3.12 describe the borrow case as "faults in checked mode" without classifying it; no diagnostic, warning or error, is mandated for it. The spec's own posture sentence, "Use of an instance after its first `Dispose()` is a program error", classifies the program; nothing says the compiler may not say so when it knows.

The two §28.2 sentences also sit oddly beside each other. A kill on *some* paths makes a later read of the variable an **error** (uncertain fault, rejected); a kill on *every* path makes it a **warning** (certain fault, admitted). The v0.184 record explains the first as "free precision"; the second was left at a warning because the straight-line shape "is never the silent one" — the warning makes it loud. The borrow shape is the silent one.

## 3. Options

### 3.1 O0 — the status quo

The same-variable warning; nothing for a borrow; the span sentence stands. Cost: the silent faults of §2, and the inversion that a certain fault is a warning while an uncertain one is an error.

### 3.2 (a) — extend the warning to borrows

Mandate §28.2's warning for a use through a borrow the kill dominates, severity unchanged. It makes e1 as loud as e8 and changes no verdict. Declined (§7): it leaves the certain-fault/uncertain-fault inversion in place, it mandates a warning the spec's own doctrine (rationale §5.28: the spec mandates warnings only where load-bearing) would have to defend as the final state of a certain fault, and the project's one previous warn-first placement here (v0.184) was never a ramp.

### 3.3 (b) — a dominated use is an error, through the variable and through a local borrow (adopted)

As §0 states it; the normative text is §8. The disposal stays legal and unfrozen; what is rejected is the use.

### 3.4 (c) — freeze explicit disposal under a live borrow (K5.3 flipped)

The borrow checker §5.1 declined and this proposal does **not** reopen. It rejects the *kill* when any borrow is used later on any path: e1, e3 (a kill on one branch), e7 (the loop) and the dead-alias shapes alike. It is the only option that closes the e7 residual, and the only one that rejects e3, a program whose fault the backstop catches and that is sound on the other path. It also misses the certain fault `a.Dispose(); var b := a; b.Name` (no borrow is live at the kill) — which (b)'s same-variable half catches at the `var b := a`. Stays declined on §5.1's grounds, which this proposal leaves intact for the kill: the measured 60 rejections in the branching population are 6 certain faults, 24 conditional-use faults and 30 dead aliases.

### 3.5 Two variants of (b), declined (§7)

**(b-d)** also rejecting a repeated `Dispose()` / `DisposeAsync()` / discard of the same binding — the one use whose runtime outcome §28.2 defines as a no-op in both modes; rejecting it refuses a defined, harmless program, and `s28_2_0001` and `own_use_after_dispose_warn_601.spec_28_2_double_dispose_is_warning_not_error` pin the warning (the 2026-06-15 owner decision the latter cites). **(b-e)** keeping the consuming uses (`move x`, an `owned` argument) at a warning while reads become errors — it re-splits the verdict by use form, which the v0.303 ruling (#3975) closed with a `DECLINED.md` row ("keying the diagnostic on the use form"), and a transfer of a logically disposed value hands a dead object to a holder that cannot know.

## 4. Evaluation

### 4.1 Soundness

(b) admits nothing the current rules reject (the model: 0 newly accepted in both populations) and rejects only programs whose rejected use is certain to fault when it executes, plus dead aliases. In the branching population every one of the 189 accepted programs that fault on every path is rejected, once the model's artefact is set aside (its machine counts a repeated `disp` of a disposed binding as a faulting read; those 81 programs are the `Dispose(); Dispose()` no-op shape, which (b) exempts by design), and so is every one of the 48 whose conditional use faults whenever it runs. Nothing changes for a kill on only some paths to the use (e3: 60 such programs in the model stay admitted and fault on the killing path in checked mode), nor for a collection slot (K5.6: slot kills are outside the local analysis; the owned-**field** shape, whose kill is a reassignment or an explicit disposal of the field, is proposal 0206's, decided in the same train), nor for a borrow held by a task frame (K6.4) or stored in a field (K4.3's recorded trust). **The residual is stated honestly in the text**: the loop shape e7 — a kill at the end of the body, a reassignment, and a use at the top of the next iteration — is not dominated (the first iteration reaches the use without passing the kill) and stays the checked-mode fault; only (c) closes it, at the cost of e3 and the dead aliases — or [#7364](https://github.com/s8lap/bezel-lang/issues/7364)'s reading B, freezing the reassignment `a := new …` of an explicitly-disposed binding while a borrow of the displaced value is live, which rejects e7 at the store with no new state; lane Q's v0.346 ruling on #7364 declines B (its `DECLINED.md` row), because that store kills nothing — the kill was the explicit `Dispose()`, unfrozen under its flag — so B is a borrow-liveness rule disguised as a freeze, and it rejects `var b := a; if c then a.Dispose(); a := new W(); end if; b.Name` whose store-free form is e3, admitted. This proposal takes neither. Under (b) the unchecked build's silent wrong read is removed for every shape the compiler can prove, and the checked build's deterministic fault remains for the rest.

Kernel classification (§9 checklist): a **derivation**, no axiom — K4.2 (the kill is site-announced and flag-set) + K3.9 (the explicitly-disposed post-state) + K7.4's dominance (the "every path" condition §28.2 already uses) + K2.1/K2.2 (a holder's source set, read as K5.5 reads it). K5 is not touched: clause 3 still says the site-announced kills are unfrozen, and nothing in (b) freezes one. Body-locality (proposal 0105 §1): **local** — the dominance is over one body's flow graph and the source set is K5.5's body-local one; a borrow that leaves the body (a stored field, a frame no local holds, a parameter in the callee) is not reached, which is the same boundary the freeze has.

### 4.2 Simplicity

The rule is one sentence a writer can hold: *the compiler rejects a use after a disposal it can see always precedes it, whether you spell the use through the variable or through something that borrows it; dispose after the last use.* Two existing rules collapse into it: the §28.2 warning becomes the same-variable case of the error, and the §28.3.9 span paragraph's special pleading ("compiles, and the read faults") becomes the general rule's conditional-kill case. The analysis is the one the compiler runs: `checkRead`'s Disposed arm (`src/ownership/Ownership_Checks.cpp:3340`) already computes the dominating kill per variable and reports it with the disposing site; `borrowSourceForExpr` and the K5.5 liveness pass already trace a local's sources through aliases, pins, holders, closures and frames (`Ownership_impl.hpp:3216` and the *Frame locals* machinery). What is added is a generation on the source (a borrow names the value the variable held when it was taken, so a reassignment between kill and use does not clear it — e5) and an "all sources killed on every path" test at each use of a holder. The #4602 amendment (master plan §4.8, owner-approved 2026-10-27) has the compiler evaluate "does any borrow of this value survive this `Dispose()`?" at every explicit disposal to free the object in full; (b) is the dual question — "was every source of this borrow disposed before this use?" — over the same facts.

### 4.3 Consistency

- **Certain versus uncertain.** After (b) the severity order is monotone: an uncertain fault through the variable is an error (K7.4), a certain fault through the variable or a borrow is an error, an uncertain fault through a borrow is the backstop's. Today a certain fault is the only one that is *not* an error.
- **A borrow is judged no more harshly than the variable.** Rejecting `b.Name` while `a.Name` stays a warning would invert the kernel's reading of `var b := a` as a borrow of `a` (K3.13): the same program state, the same fault. That is why the same-variable warning is promoted in the same ruling rather than left behind.
- **The kill-split, kept.** v0.303 (#3975) ruled that the verdict is keyed on the kill, never on the use form. (b) keeps that: every use form draws the error under a dominating kill. The one carve — a repeated explicit disposal stays a warning — is keyed on §28.2's own definition of that call as a runtime no-op, not on a syntactic position; it is the one "use" with a defined, non-faulting outcome.
- **K5 untouched; §28.14 and §30.12 untouched.** K5.7 and K5.8 still freeze the kill where the flag is no backstop; (b) adds no third exception and does not restate them.
- **Reassignment.** §28.3.3's state match says an explicitly-disposed LHS "requires an owned RHS too and displaces a logically disposed value: the store is that value's release point". The borrow of the displaced value is dead at that store; (b) rejects its later use (e5) and admits a re-borrow of the new value (e6) — the reading the state-match rule already implies.
- **The join rule's "no flow around a back edge into a use" (K7.4)** is for the variable; (b) does not extend it to holders. That is the e7 residual, stated as such.

### 4.4 Migration (measured, §5)

What becomes newly rejected: (i) every program the §28.2 warning fires on today except the repeated disposal — a straight-line `x.Dispose(); x.M()`; (ii) a use through a local alias, pin, span, borrow-bearing value, closure or frame of a value whose explicit disposal dominates the use. Both have one mechanical remedy: dispose after the last use (or, for the e5 shape, re-borrow from the new value). Nothing is newly admitted; nothing is newly warned; the double-dispose warning is unchanged. Ramp: none — this is a design change, not a ratchet (the v0.184 promotion of the warning from *should* to *must* scheduled no rejection), so §2's ratchet class does not apply and a Migration note states the end state.

## 6. The case against the recorded decline

The decline index row (`DECLINED.md`, *Compile-time borrow-liveness checking*) states the bar as *—(none stated)*, so the governing bar is the process's own: *reopened only with new evidence against the stated reasons, never by re-asking* (spec-change-process §2, §6). The stated reasons are two, and this proposal brings evidence against each, while leaving the thing the row protects — that the kill is legal — standing.

**"No soundness gain (the backstop is real)."** The claim is true of a checked build and was re-measured by the research run with a metric that defines a checked-mode fault as a sound outcome; under that metric a compile error can never be a gain over a deterministic fault, so the result was foreordained, and the run's own caveat (2026-10-27) says so. Two pieces of evidence it did not weigh: (i) §28.2 defines a second conformance mode in which the same use is undefined behaviour, and the measured programs show that behaviour is not a crash but, in e5 and e7, a *successful read of another object's state* — the class of defect proposal 0162 and the #5832 decline were written to prevent; (ii) in the model, every program (b) rejects faults on every execution of the rejected use — there is no sound program among them except dead aliases — which is a precision the v0.184 record already called "free" when it rejected the conditional kill for the variable.

**"A de-facto borrow checker."** A borrow checker rejects the *kill* while a borrow lives — option (c), which stays declined here with its reasons. (b) rejects a *use* that a visible kill is certain to precede, which is the ordinary business of a use-after-free diagnostic and is already what §28.2 does for the variable. It adds no rule about exclusive access, no rejection of dispose-after-last-use, no rejection of a conditional kill, and no analysis the compiler does not run: the §28.2 dominance and K5's provenance. The decline's own framing names the goal (b) serves — *"free this once and don't use it after"* — and §28.3.12 already says liveness *is* enforced "up to explicit disposal"; (b) moves that boundary from the kill to the dominated use.

**What stays declined.** Freezing the kill under a live borrow (c): §5.1's reasons hold for it — it rejects e3 and the dead aliases, and the flag is the right backstop for a kill on some paths. Scoping to domain-crossing sites: untouched. The §28.14 and §30.12 exceptions: untouched.

**Precedent.** v0.184's join ruling is the same step on the same ground: *"a deliberate step past this section's runtime-checked posture for site-announced kills: the per-path consumption tracking exists regardless […], so the rejection is free precision, not a borrow checker"*. (b) makes the same argument for the certain case the warning left behind, and for the borrow the warning never reached.

**Process class.** A design change (§2's third rule: it contradicts a recorded decision), so a proposal first, then a ruling-class landing with the adversarial critique, the composition check against anything else touching §28.2/§28.3 in the same train, the corpus sweep (§5 item 3c; ten rows), the code-generation reference update, and two `spec-tracking` issues (§8).

## 9. Self-critique (adversarial pass)

1. **Is (b) a borrow checker after all?** The test: does it reject any program whose rejected operation does not fault when executed? Yes, one class — a dead alias (`a.Dispose(); var c := a;` with `c` unread; 108 of 345 in the model), and the read of a null-test or identity comparison through a borrow (p16, p12). The same-variable rule already rejects (warns on) these reads today; §28.2's own sentence counts a bare read as a use, and the spec's posture is that use of a disposed instance is a program error whether or not the mode faults on it. Recorded as an accepted over-rejection with a one-line remedy, not hidden. Everything else (b) rejects faults whenever it executes.
2. **The two-source borrow.** `var b := (if c then a else d); a.Dispose(); b.Name` (p09) is admitted — only one source is killed — and faults when `b` names `a`. Rejecting it would be the uncertain-fault rejection (c) makes; the text says "every value in the holder's source set" so the line is drawn where the fault is certain.
3. **Field-path borrows are conservative.** A borrow reached through `h.f` is receiver-sourced at `h` (§28.3.4), whether `f` is `owned` or a plain reference; after `h.Dispose()` a borrow through an *owned* field is certainly dead (the cascade), one through a non-owned field is not (its referent is owned elsewhere). (b) keys on the source set, as K5 does for the silent kills, so the latter is rejected although it would not fault. The rewrite is to borrow from the referent's own binding. This is the one place (b) over-rejects a live referent; `s9_4_0008` is the owned case. Stated as a cost; the alternative (reading field ownership along the path) is a precision the source-set machinery does not have and this proposal does not add.
4. **Does the same-variable promotion remove the way to observe the backstop?** A straight-line `x.Dispose(); x.M()` can no longer be compiled, so every test and corpus row that observed `DisposedAccessException` through it must use a kill the compiler cannot see — a conditional kill observed through an alias with a true condition (a conditional kill of the variable itself is K7.4's error), a slot kill, a frame-held borrow. That is about 22 read rows and three corpus rows of mechanical churn (§5). The 13 rows that observe the fault at a *consume* (`move x` of a disposed `x`) cannot be re-cut over a local — a borrow cannot be moved, and a conditional kill makes the `move` K7.4's error — so they flip to compile-rejection rows, and the run-time check at a consume of a logically disposed value stays reachable only through a field's replacing `move`. That is the intended verdict ((b-e) declined), not a lost test: the rows' subject is the shape this proposal rejects. The implementing commit names each flipped row and its new oracle (a behaviour flip, `CLAUDE.md`). The backstop itself is unchanged and still observable.
5. **Is the repeated-disposal exemption a use-form split?** It is keyed on §28.2's definition of the repeated call's runtime outcome (a no-op), not on the syntactic position: no other use has a defined outcome on a disposed receiver. The v0.303 row declined keying on the use *form* to buy a severity difference the backstop "already covers in both directions"; here the difference is that one of the two is not a fault at all.
6. **The loop residual.** e7 compiles and faults (checked) or reads the wrong object (unchecked) under (b). The text says so. Closing it needs (c), a K7.4-style "no flow around a back edge into a use" for holders (which rejects the first iteration's sound read), or #7364's reading B — freezing the reassignment of the explicitly-disposed binding while a borrow of the displaced value lives, declined in lane Q's v0.346 ruling because the store kills nothing and B rejects a conditional-kill program whose store-free form is admitted (§4.1); none is proposed. The model's loop population puts the residual at 6 of 45,663 accepted programs (§4.5).
7. **Other passages stating the old rule.** Swept by grep over the twelve documents for `use-after-dispose`, `DisposedAccessException`, `site-announced`, `dominat`, `faults in checked mode`, `AsSpan`+`Dispose`: §17.13's "a later read falls under the use-after-dispose regime of §28.2" stays correct (regime, not severity); §28.3.11's "an explicit `Dispose()` … on [a temporary] is K3.9's consume" names no later use; K5's conjunct (ii) and clause 3 describe the kill, which (b) does not change; §28.14's and §30.12's K5.7/K5.8 texts are untouched; stdlib §4.14/§6.2 name the modes without the shape; Guide 04 §4 is the frame clause. The research document (`docs/research/soundness/RELAXATIONS.md`) is non-normative and already carries the caveat. Lane F's re-sweep found one passage the draft had missed: §28.6's "A later **read** of the slot is not statically diagnosed — §28.2's use-after-dispose **warning** is a straight-line rule over a local" — stale under this proposal and now edited (with proposal 0206's window) in `0206-spec-28-6-later-read`.
8. **Composition with v0.345's trains.** Proposals 0200/0201 (which types have `Dispose()`; no method reference to it) touch §8.7, §27.6 and §28.2's first lines, not the use-after-disposal paragraph; every anchor here re-verified against `d9f11d15f`.
