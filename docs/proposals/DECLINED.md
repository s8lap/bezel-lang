# The decline index

**This file is the decline index: the single ground-truth for *where* every recorded Bezel decline, deferral, non-goal, and retirement lives.** Bezel's declined-with-reasons content is recorded in **five** homes, not one — (1) the rationale's explicit-rejection register (§17), (2) its deferred register (§18), (3) this file's own declined register (part 2 below, plus the gap review it points at), (4) per-section decision records scattered through the specification and the rationale — where most of the substantive declines actually live — and (5) decline records inside graduated proposals. The topology is stated in [proposal 0106 §0](0106-language-lessons-survey.md); its research run paid the five-register walk repeatedly, once per verdict. The project's reopening discipline — *revisiting an entry requires new evidence against the stated reasons, not re-asking* — depends on being able to find the stated reasons. This index is the fix, and it is an **index, not a move**: no record is relocated and no reason is restated here. **A row points; the cited record is always the authority.** Where a row's summary and its authoritative record disagree, the record wins and the row is a defect.

**Verdict classes** (closed set — every row carries exactly one):

| Class | Means | Archetype |
|---|---|---|
| `rejected` | Decided against on principle. Reversible only against the stated reasons; many rows still carry a bar, which is what it would take, not a promise. A row that has *already* been reversed stays here rather than moving — the entry survives as the precedent that a rejection can move (rationale §17.6's shape). | rationale §17 |
| `deferred` | Not in this revision; the design is open. Reopenable by the stated condition. | rationale §18 |
| `declined-at-proposal` | Declined in a proposals-folder record — this file's D-numbers, the gap review, or a decline record inside a graduated proposal. | `DECLINED.md`, proposal records |
| `non-goal` | A deliberate absence of a whole area, recorded normatively in the specification. | spec §2.8.5 |
| `retired` | Shipped, then removed. The record is the retirement's reasoning. | the `event` member kind (spec §24) |
| `recommended — awaiting owner ruling` | Not yet a verdict: a draft proposal recommends recording a decline. No reader may cite one of these as settled. | a draft proposal's recommended decline |

The archetype column names where the class *usually* lives, not the only place it may live: a rejection recorded in a per-section spec decision record is still `rejected`, and a deferral recorded inside a graduated proposal is still `deferred`.

**Reading the table.** Rows are grouped by verdict class, alphabetical by topic within a group. **Authoritative record** is a file plus a section: `rationale` is `docs/Bezel_Design_Rationale.md`, `spec` is `docs/Bezel_Language_Spec.md`, `stdlib` is `docs/Bezel_Standard_Library.md`, `changelog` is `docs/Bezel_Changelog.md`; proposal citations link to the file in this folder. Section numbers are as of **v0.264**; cite them onward with the revision, per `CLAUDE.md`. **Reopening bar** quotes or tightly paraphrases the condition the record states, or reads `—(none stated)` when the record states none — which means *no path is recorded*, not *no path exists*.

**Keeping it true.** Every landing that records a new decline, deferral, non-goal, or retirement — or graduates or declines a proposal — adds or updates its row here in the same commit ([`spec-change-process.md` §9](spec-change-process.md)). A row without a resolvable citation is a defect in this file, not in the record.

## D-0001 — A dependency-injection container (runtime service resolution)

**Declined 2026-07-03 (owner-confirmed).** Full reasoning in [proposal 0003 §3.3](0003-locked-t.md); summary:

DI does two jobs, and Bezel keeps one while declining the other.

- **Wiring** (constructor injection) needs no framework: it is constructor parameters from a composition root, and Bezel's `owned`/borrow annotations make the dependency contract *more* expressive than any container can (`constructor(owned db: Database)` states who owns the dependency; no container expresses lifetime-of-ownership at all).
- **The container** (`AddSingleton`/`Resolve<T>()`) is declined on three grounds:
  1. **It is the same global state, hidden.** A container's singleton table *is* a process-global mutable `Dictionary<Type, object>`; every §22.12/§30.14 data-race hazard of a mutable static applies identically — ASP.NET's own "singleton services must be thread-safe" rule is §22.12 enforced by documentation instead of a compiler. A `static readonly x: Locked<T>` states the sharing, the type, and the synchronization in one visible line.
  2. **It trades compile-time for runtime.** Service-locator resolution fails at request time ("no registration for `ICache`"); a constructor parameter fails at compile time. Bezel's design consistently moves this class of error to compile time (e.g. sendability checked at route registration, proposal 0002 §4.1).
  3. **The machinery is reflection-shaped.** Runtime containers reflect over constructors; Bezel deliberately has no member-access reflection (rationale §18) — a foundational decline this would reopen.

The lifetime taxonomy maps without a framework: *singleton* → composition-root local passed down, or a §22.12 synchronized static (`Locked<T>`) when genuinely ambient; *scoped/per-request* → handler locals; *transient* → construct it.

**What would reopen this:** large real codebases demonstrating that hand-written composition roots do not scale — and the remedy would then be **compile-time generated wiring** (Dagger/source-generator style), a tooling project, never a runtime container.

## D-0002 — Anonymous types (C#-style ad-hoc object shapes)

**Declined 2026-07-03 (owner conversation on data projections).** Three structural grounds:

1. **They require structural typing inside a nominal system** — two same-shaped anonymous types must unify, which imports a second typing discipline for one convenience.
2. **They cannot cross method boundaries** — C#'s own recorded limitation, mitigated there only via `object`/`dynamic`, neither of which Bezel will have. A shape that cannot be returned, stored, or passed is a local-only convenience…
3. **…whose killer habitat is reflection.** Anonymous types earn their keep in ORM expression trees that read property names off the shape at runtime — machinery Bezel deliberately lacks (rationale §18). C# itself largely migrated to *named* ValueTuples once available.

**The accepted remedy** is method-local `record` (and `enum`) declarations — **graduated into the normative documents at spec v0.225** (proposal [0066](0066-method-local-type-declarations.md); language spec §21.10; the design record is now rationale §9.10, which supersedes this paragraph as the remedy's home): the shape lives next to its single use, keeping the recorded records-over-tuples stance ("a name and a documented shape is a feature") while removing the distance cost. Cross-type mapping stays explicit (constructors/factory methods; `with` for same-type variants) — automatic mapping is reflection-shaped and would be reconsidered only as compile-time generation, per D-0001's wiring posture. Truly dynamic data goes through the JSON DOM (gap review item 21): runtime-shaped values behind typed faults, quarantined at the parse boundary, never a `dynamic` type.

**What would reopen this:** tuples being reopened first (the recorded deferral names evidence-from-real-code as the bar) — named tuples would then be re-weighed against anonymous types, and would still win.

## D-0003 — Unifying single-statement member/lambda bodies on `do … end` (#745)

**Declined 2026-07-07 (delegated ruling, 2026-07 spec/design run; owner review pending).** The draft RFC (#745) proposed one uniform `do … end` spelling for every single-statement body position (members, lambdas, arms). Three grounds:

1. **The load-bearing seam is already closed.** The v0.170 uniform-closer revision (#1374) fixed the actual inconsistency the RFC was written against — every block now closes with `end;` or a labelled repeat of its introducer. What remains is a *spelling triad* (`=> expr` arrows, `do … end` blocks, full member bodies) whose members are each locally consistent and non-overlapping; unifying them buys no rule deletions in the grammar, only migration.
2. **The churn cost has inverted.** When the RFC was drafted the compiler was young; today ~10,500 tests, the conformance corpus, both guides, and every proposal encode the current spellings. A whole-corpus migration to retire zero rules is the definition of churn.
3. **The surviving argument depends on a declined feature.** The RFC's strongest remaining case (arm bodies as uniform blocks) reads naturally only under block-*expressions*, which rationale §5.13 declines on recorded grounds; without them the unified form is strictly more ceremony in the common case.

**What would reopen this:** block expressions being reopened first (their own register entry governs), or field evidence that the triad measurably confuses users — not re-asking.

## D-0004 — `Option<T>` in the standard library

**Declined 2026-07-19 (0041 graduation, v0.200).** `T?` is Bezel's option type: it has language-level syntax (`?.`, `??`, narrowing, the §19.6 nullable clause), stdlib-wide integration (the Try shapes, `FirstOrNull`), and a design lineage the rationale defends throughout. Shipping `Option<T>` beside the `choice` kind would create two spellings of absence, two conversion questions at every API boundary, and an eternal style war. A choice with one meaningful payload and one empty case *is* what `T?` already provides. Revisiting requires evidence that `T?` structurally cannot serve where `Option<T>` could — not preference.

## D-0005 — A value-form try expression (`(try E catch …)` / `(try E else fallback)`)

**Declined 2026-07-21 (expression-forms review, post-v0.214).** The first of two paren-atom candidates weighed together in that review — the second is D-0006 — each by the admission test recorded in proposal 0061: **a value-form try expression** (`(try E catch … )` / `(try E else fallback)`) — fails test 3: it makes exception-based *expected* failure ergonomic, precisely the path Guide 2 §6 (v0.214-era numbering; the table is §7 since the 2026-07-30 restructure) routes to `T?`/Try/`Result`; the doctrine would be undermined by its own syntax, and the borrow-scoped catch model (§31) offers no owned exception value to carry anyway.

Revisiting requires new evidence against these reasons.

**Declined 2026-07-21 (expression-forms review, post-v0.214).** The second of the two paren-atom candidates weighed in that review — the first is D-0005 — by the same proposal 0061 admission test. **Comprehensions** (`(for x in xs where p => f(x))`) — fail test 1: pure sugar over the lazy-query operators (`xs.Where(p)…`, affine per §29.11), whose ownership story is already settled; also a loop expression, which the owner separately declined absent unique capability. ## D-0007 — Delegation-by-forwarding sugar (Go embedding / Kotlin `by`)

**Declined 2026-07-21 (writer-seat sweep).** **Delegation-by-forwarding sugar** (Go embedding / Kotlin `by`): implicit method promotion hides the forwarding call and blurs receiver identity — squarely against announced-flow/no-hidden-magic — and composition-by-explicit-forwarding is fully expressible today; the absence was boilerplate, not capability. Revisiting requires evidence that forwarding boilerplate is a measured, recurring burden the one-line members do not adequately express.

## D-0008 — Newtype deriving (Haskell GND, Nim `distinct` borrowing, OCaml-style derived conformance)

**Declined 2026-08-20 (v0.263, recorded from proposal 0106 §5.5).** A mechanism by which a one-field wrapper automatically obtains the wrapped type's interface conformances and members — Haskell's `deriving newtype`/GeneralizedNewtypeDeriving, Nim's `distinct` with `borrow`, and their relatives — so that a `Port` wrapping an `int` gets `int`'s comparison, formatting, or numeric surface without restating it.

Recorded explicitly rather than reached by adjacency to D-0007. **The adjacency is real but partial, and that is why this entry exists.** Both forms are implicit method promotion at the one-field case, so D-0007's ground carries: promotion hides the forwarding call and blurs receiver identity, against announced-flow/no-hidden-magic. But the mechanisms differ in a way a reader must not have to reconstruct — Kotlin `by` forwards to a *stored delegate*, an object the wrapper holds, while newtype deriving derives a *conformance* by representational coercion, on the ground that the wrapper and the wrapped type have the same representation. The extension of D-0007's ground to that case: a conformance obtained by representational coercion is a claim that the wrapper is interchangeable with what it wraps at every member of the derived interface — which is exactly the claim a distinct wrapper is declared in order to deny. Where the wrapper genuinely wants the whole surface, an alias already gives it (spec §27.8, and the constrained-alias variant is declined at rationale §17.12); where it does not, deriving the whole surface is the wrong default, and the members it actually wants are one line each.

The boundary Bezel draws around automatic derivation is drawn, not absent: equality and hashing are auto-derived for records with a per-member opt-out (spec §9.2, the `equality skip` clause), and serialization is derived by conformance (spec §9.8) — both cases where the derived behavior is a function of the type's own structure rather than a claim of interchangeability with another type.

**What would reopen this:** the same bar D-0007 states — evidence that forwarding boilerplate is a measured, recurring burden the one-line members do not adequately express — together with a design that says which conformances derive and which do not, since the failure mode is deriving the ones that assert interchangeability.
