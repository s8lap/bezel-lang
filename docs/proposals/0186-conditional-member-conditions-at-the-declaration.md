## 0. Summary

Five standard-library member families exist only at some instantiations of their enclosing type: `Array<T>.Sort()` where `T: IComparable<T>`; `OwningBuffer<T>.DangerousPointer()` where `T` is an FFI pointee; `Result<T, E>`'s `Map`/`MapError`/`AndThen`/`UnwrapOr` where the receiver and result are value-like; the equality search (`Contains`, `Remove`, `IndexOf`, …) where the element type has an `Equals` for §9.2's first kind; and every member whose signature forms the nullable companion of an element type, where that type is not an inline array. Since v0.277 (proposal 0116) §14.2 has carried them in a **register**: a definition, five rules, a five-row table, five "No row of this register is a standing grant" dispositions explaining why each row exists and how it would lapse, and an exclusions paragraph keeping the table honest — about 1,830 words, of which the dispositions alone say, of every row, that it "lapses with the sorting row's second way out: a member-level condition, once admitted, is the member's declared form".

This proposal takes that way out, for the standard library only. Each conditional member states its condition **at its own declaration**: `public method Sort() where T: IComparable<T>;` — a `where` clause on the enclosing type's parameter, the form rule 1 still refuses a user declaration — where a §14.2 constraint spells the condition, and the defining section named otherwise — at the declaration for `Result`'s combinators, the equality search and `DangerousPointer()`, as their stdlib paragraphs already do, and for the nullable-companion members (too many, partly extension methods) in the index row with §15.1, which states their condition in full (§9 b). The closed set is indexed once, in stdlib §1's *Conditional members* conventions row, where `check_docs.sh`'s REGISTER check now reads it. §14.2 keeps what decides a program: the definition, the reading rule (decided after substitution at the use, as §14.5 *Constraints* reads a generic method's own clause; the v0.321 generic-body reading unchanged), the diagnostic contract, the ordinary-call rule and the user bar. It loses the table, the dispositions and the exclusions — about 790 words in the subsection — and the five other documents' "register" wording goes with them.

**No verdict moves.** Every condition is the one its row stated, decided at the same site with the same remedy; the user bar (rule 1) is unchanged, and the user-writable member-level `where` stays declined on proposal 0116's ground. This is a carrier change under the §10 review, measured: the conditions keep their 142 natural-order `Sort()` and 78 `Result` combinator call sites in the test corpus; the register as a document structure has zero.

## 1. The register, as v0.337 states it

§14.2 *Conditionally-admitted members* (v0.337): the definition; rule 1 ("**Compiler-known standard-library types only.** A conditionally-admitted member may be declared only on a type this specification's standard-library companion declares, and only where a row of the register below names it"); rule 2 (decided after substitution at the use, with the generic-body reading); rule 3 (the diagnostic); rule 4 (an ordinary call otherwise); rule 5 (part of the public contract); "**The register.** The set is closed and grows **only by recorded ruling**"; the five-row table (*Member(s)* / *Condition on the enclosing type argument* / *Condition defined in* / *Remedy the diagnostic states*); "**No row of this register is a standing grant.** Each carries a recorded disposition off it, and the register is expected to empty rather than to grow" followed by five bullets; "(Two exclusions, so the closure is checkable. …)" with the two-presentation definition and its generic-body reading; and "**This is not the `where` clause.**".

The dispositions name the carrier this proposal adopts, row by row: the sorting row — "Two ways out are open and neither is decided here — give the natural-order form a name the receiver does not declare, or admit a member-level condition (declined at this revision, Appendix C.1). The row lapses when one is taken"; `DangerousPointer()` — "The row lapses with the sorting row's second way out … a member-level condition, once admitted, is the member's declared form"; the equality search — "The row lapses with the sorting row's second way out"; the nullable-companion members — "The row lapses with the sorting row's second way out, or with a nullable inline-array form". Only the `Result` row names a different exit (a classification constraint opening §26.2), and it too is a member-level statement today (stdlib §2.20: "admissible only where the receiver instantiation **and the member's result instantiation** … are both **value-like**").

Rationale §5.15 (v0.277) recorded the decline of the *user-writable* per-member `where` ("The clause would reopen `where`'s meaning for four rules that already depend on it") and called the register "a holding pen, not a grant". A standard-library-only statement at the declaration reopens nothing for user code: rule 1's bar stands, and the four rules the v0.277 record names (constraint inheritance on overrides, overload ranking, the artifact interface, the breaking-change table) each already read a member's clause the way this proposal needs — §14.2 *Constraints on overrides* inherits a method's constraints; §14.5 *Constraints* checks a method's own clause on the selected candidate after resolution, never during it; §2.8.2 carries constraints as signature facts; §2.8.6's constraint rows classify a change to one as breaking.

**Decision: B.** The register's own text asked for it, and the judgement's criterion (negligible reach, a short rewrite, no loss of safety) is met trivially — nothing a program sees changes. C and D each retire one row at a real cost and leave the apparatus; E is declined on its standing bar. D-K — dropping §2.8.6's dispatched-generic and requirement-summary rows — is **not** done: those rows classify requirement summaries of §3.6 rule 2 and the dispatched-method exclusion, which this proposal does not touch; nothing in them becomes redundant.

## 4. The carrier, precisely

1. **Where a §14.2 constraint spells the condition**, the declaration carries it as a `where` clause whose constrained parameter is the enclosing type's: `public method Sort() where T: IComparable<T>;` (stdlib §4.4, §4.5). Grammatically this is Appendix A.4's `GenericConstraints` on a method; semantically it is the form rule 1 rejects in user code and admits in the companion. It is read exactly as §14.5 *Constraints* reads a generic method's own clause: on the candidate a use selects, after substitution, never as a filter on applicability — which is rule 4's "the condition never makes an overload inapplicable or selects another", now derived rather than stated. In a generic body it is read by *A type parameter as the type argument*: `xs.Sort()` over `Array<T>` needs `where T: IComparable<T>` on the body's `T`, as before.
2. **Where no §14.2 constraint spells it**, the declaration names the section that defines the condition, as the four declarations already do (stdlib §2.20's comment and paragraph for `Result`; §4's *Equality search* paragraph; §4.14's `DangerousPointer()` paragraph; §15.1's *No nullable inline-array types* for the companion members, which the stdlib row names). Rule 2's generic-body reading for these — an interior use decided at the instantiations — is unchanged from v0.321.
3. **The index.** Stdlib §1 gains the row *Conditional members*, listing every conditional member with its condition and remedy and ending "No other member is conditional; the set grows only by recorded ruling." `check_docs.sh`'s REGISTER check (added for #3332) keeps both of its directions — every positive use of the term names a listed member nearby, every listed family is declared in the stdlib and claimed conditional by a passage — reading the row instead of the table (`W4.scripts.json`; the check reports "5 conditional-member families; 17 positive term-sites, all mapped" on the composed tree).

## 5. Soundness and the cells

Nothing in a program's verdict depends on where the condition is written; the proposal is sound exactly where v0.337 is. The one place a reader could suspect a move is rule 4's derivation from §14.5: §14.5 *Constraints* says an unsatisfied constraint "does not make a candidate inapplicable … and if resolution selects it the call is a compile error naming the constraint; resolution never falls back to another overload", which is rule 4 word for word in its effect, and v0.328's #5634 ruling already read a reference's condition "as §14.5 *Constraints* reads a generic method's own constraints on the candidate a call selects".

**Cells** (`docs/research/generics-matrix/runs/v0.336.json`): R3/C12, R4/C12, R5/C12, R7/C12, R9/C12 and route R3–R7/C12 quote the two-presentation generic-body paragraph, which is kept verbatim; R5/C12, R7/C12 and R9/C12 also quote "the nullable-companion row's not-an-inline-array condition ([§15.1](#sec-inline-arrays))", respelled "the nullable-companion members' not-an-inline-array condition" — a listed re-quote (one fragment, three cells), the verdicts unchanged. ## 9. Self-critique (adversarial pass)

(a) *Is a stdlib-only `where` on an enclosing parameter "inventing" a declaration form?* The grammar admits it (A.4); the semantics are the ones §14.5 already gives a method's own clause; what is new is only that the companion may write what a user may not — which is the privilege rule 1 has stated since v0.277, now with the privilege's *form* named. (b) *Does the `*OrNull`/`Try*` family fit "stated at the declaration"?* Those members are many, some extension methods (§5), and their condition is §15.1's rule, not a constraint; the declaration-site statement for them is the stdlib §1 row naming the family with §15.1, and §15.1 itself, which already states the condition, the sites and the remedies in full — the same two homes the register row had. (c) *Is the stdlib §1 row just the table moved?* Yes, deliberately: the index must live somewhere for the closure to be checkable (#3332), and the companion is the document that declares the members; the spec no longer restates what the declarations say. (d) *Re-quotes.* One fragment in three generics-matrix cells (§5); every rule-corpus `rule:` fragment homed in §14.2 at HEAD still resolves. (e) *The `(Two exclusions …)` paragraph.* Its first half (the hashable sequence operators and the two-presentation split are not register rows) is retired with the register; its second half (the generic-body reading of a two-presentation type) is a rule and is kept verbatim as *Two-presentation declarations*. (f) *D-K.* Declined with its reason in §3; the rows are not redundant. (g) *Seam with lane W3.* W3 relocates §2.8.6; this proposal cites §2.8.6 (rule 5) and edits nothing in it.
