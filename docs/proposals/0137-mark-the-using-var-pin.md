## 0. Thesis

Today one spelling means two things, and which one it means is decided by a fact about the right-hand side that the reader has to look up. `using var y := x;` over a class- or interface-typed named owned binding `x` is a **borrow-pin** (no obligation moves; `x` keeps it; `x` is frozen against moves and reassignment to the block's end), while `using var y := move x;` is an **adoption** and `using var y := x;` over a *move-only* `x` is also an adoption (§28.10, lines 7839 and 7864). The corpus shows what that costs: of the about **225** code sites in `tests/` that write the unmarked form over a named owned binding, about **189 meant adoption** (critique-round-1 recount, §2; the script's first pass said 240 and 175) — they work only because the pinned source auto-drops at scope end under the must-use warning — and the compiler's own remedy text for that warning has to explain that the spelling the author reached for does not do what it looks like it does (`src/ownership/Ownership.cpp:1165-1174`, naming a marker retired at v0.246 while doing so). The normative documents, the standard library and the guides contain **zero** such sites in example code (§2).

This proposal makes the unmarked form over a class- or interface-typed named **owned** binding a compile error with a two-remedy diagnostic. It then asks what the pin remedy should be. Four options are worked (§3): a contextual `pin x` marker at the `using var` right-hand side (A); no new word — adoption is spelled `move x` and a pinned view is spelled by the forms that already exist (B); reuse of `scoped` (C); flipping the default so that the unmarked form adopts (D). The measurement points one way. Every pin intent in the corpus (about 36 sites) is either a test of the pin mechanism itself or is expressible by declaring the source `using var` and binding a `const`/`var` view. Owned parameters, owning loop variables and owning-match bindings cannot be redeclared, but they are live to their scope's end by construction, so over them the pin adds only a freeze that adoption (`using var y := move x;`) supplies more strongly, under a new name. What B gives up is listed exactly in §5.1: a dead view's freeze, which rationale §5.27 records as one of the pin's purposes and which no non-test site uses. **Recommendation: B — no new keyword** (§5), presented to the owner as a choice against A. Kernel: a derivation of K3.13 and K3.4 (§5.2); body-locality: local (§5.3). Option A is recorded in full as the fallback if the owner wants the named-binding pin to keep a spelling; C and D are declined with reasons (§5.6).

## 1. Ground truth

### 1.1 The rule today

- **§28.10 *Adopting a named owned binding* (line 7839, derives K3.4):** "`using var y := move x;` transfers a named owned local or `owned` parameter into a scope-bound binding: `y` takes over the disposal obligation and `x` is left moved-from … An **unmarked** `using var y := x;` where `x` is a **class- or interface-typed** named owned binding does *not* adopt: it is a borrow, pinned per the subsection below, and `x` retains the obligation. Where `x` is a local of **move-only value type** (§15.3) the unmarked form **adopts**: a move-only binding has no borrow reading to disambiguate, so the plain read moves (K3.12 …)".
- **§28.10 *`using var` initialized from a borrow* (line 7864, derives K2):** the borrow shapes are "a method call without an `owned` return … an indexer access, a field read, or an *unmarked* read of a **class- or interface-typed** named owned binding"; the `using var` "does **not** assume a disposal obligation … still **pins** the borrow's source for the duration of the scope … the frozen kills (a move, a transfer, a reassignment of the binding) are compile errors, and the **site-announced** kills stay unfrozen under their flag backstop (K4.2, K5.3): an explicit `Dispose()` of the source is permitted". And: "The pin adds no exception to K5's two recorded shapes; the §28.14 storage admission remains the one admission whose captured sources are frozen against explicit disposal as well".
- **A5 (line 6963):** "`using var` is a scope pin: non-reassignable, never movable out … scope-pinning is its entire meaning".
- **K3.4 (line 6864):** the marked binding transfer, `using var y := move x;` and `using var _ := move x;` included. **K3.12 (line 6865):** move-only values move at "any store into an owning location", unmarked. **K3.13 (line 6877):** "A consume is spelled by the transfer marker (`move x`) except at a **marker-free position** … An **unmarked read of a class-typed binding never consumes**: `var b := a;` is always a borrow for class-typed `a`."
- **§28.3 *Ownership state of locals* (line 6980):** "`using var x := expr;` where `expr` is an owned producer or a marked transfer … is **owned** … When `expr` is a borrow — including an *unmarked* read of a …" and **§28.3 *Borrows* (line 7065):** "an **unmarked** local binding or assignment RHS over a **class- or interface-typed** binding — `var y := x;`, `y := x;`, `using var y := x;`: a plain alias binding is a borrow of `x`".
- **§29.2 (lines 8236–8267):** the `for` desugaring writes `using var __source := source;` and, when `source` is an owned local, "the desugaring is adjusted: `using var __source := source;` is replaced by a borrow that pins `source` for the loop's duration without assuming the disposal obligation". The pin is compiler-internal and unnameable; nothing here changes it.
- **§28.12 `await using var`** (line 8044): the asynchronous variant shares §28.10's admission; **§22.4 `out using var`** (line 4814): a recipient form with no right-hand side; **§17.13 `using var _`** (line 7841): "the same scope-end disposal, non-reassignability, admission rules, and borrow-pin case as a named `using var`".
- **Code-generation reference §4.2** (lines 437, 454–459): `move` is "*Required* … when a **named owned binding** feeds … a local binding (`var y := move x;`, `using var y := move x;`)"; then the three `using var` bullets: adoption, the unmarked **borrow-pin** ("does not adopt … `x` keeps it … an explicit `x.Dispose()` is **not** [rejected]"), the move-only adoption.
- **Guide 01 §9** (lines 393–407): teaches `using var c := move conn;` for draining and `using var l := move line;` for yielding; it never teaches the named-binding pin.
- **Rationale §5.27** (lines 975–989): reason one, "*the pin has no substitute* … The borrow-pin (spec §28.10) is the only spelling of 'keep this source live to the end of this scope'"; and the concession, "It does not tell the reader whether the binding is owned: its own owned-versus-pin split is dispatched by the right-hand side (spec §28.10), the one genuine reader ambiguity in this area."
- **`DECLINED.md`** rows touching `using var`: *Folding `using var` into plain `var`* (`rejected`, rationale §5.27), *Owned `const` locals* (`rejected`, rationale §17.8/§5.27), *Suppressing the must-use warning on a move-only value's implicit drop via `using var`* (`rejected`), the `out owned var x` retirement (`retired`, v0.289). None records a decision about the unmarked named-binding pin itself; the only recorded posture is §5.27's reason one, which §5.6 addresses.

**What the pin over a named owned binding buys that nothing else can.** Over an **owned local**, nothing the kernel can see: an owned local is live to its scope end anyway (K3.14's auto-drop), so the pin's only content is the freeze — "a move, a transfer, a reassignment of the binding" in the pinned range is rejected — and declaring the source itself `using var` gives A5's stronger guarantee (never reassignable, never movable out), while a `const`/`var` view gives the alias. Over an **owned parameter**, which cannot be redeclared `using var`, the parameter is live to the method's end by construction (K3.14 at the exit), so again only the freeze remains; and the freeze adoption supplies — `using var c := move conn;` leaves `conn` moved-from, which rejects every later use of it, not merely the kills — is what every one of the 85 sink sites was reaching for. The one observable the pin gives that adoption and the `const` view together do not is *a dead view's* freeze: with `const view := x;` the source may be moved after `view`'s last use; under the pin it may not. No site in the corpus depends on that, and the consume-site freeze (K5.1) already rejects the move while the view is live. Over a **borrow-state** source and over a **call, field or indexer** right-hand side the pin is unchanged by this proposal, so §5.27's "keep this source live to scope end" keeps its spelling exactly where the source's liveness is not already guaranteed.

## 3. Options

The common core of A, B and C is one rule (▲, the §28.10 *Adopting a named owned binding* paragraph, replacing its second sentence):

> ▲ An **unmarked** `using var y := x;` is a **compile error** when its right-hand side is a named owned binding whose declared type is a class or interface type. This includes the null-forgiving read `x!` (read as the bare `x` is, [§17.8](#sec-the-null-forgiving-operator), line 3642), the anonymous `using var _ := x;` and `await using var y := x;`. A *named owned binding* here means:
>
> - a local whose ownership state at the declaration is owned ([§28.3](#sec-local-variables-ownership-and-borrows) *Ownership state of locals*) — a `var` or `using var` local, an owning loop variable, an `out owned` recipient, an owning-match arm binding ([§35.5](#sec-owning-match)); or
> - an `owned` parameter.
>
> Two shapes are **not** covered. A captured owned binding inside a closure is in borrow state ([§28.3](#sec-local-variables-ownership-and-borrows) *Captured owned bindings*). A bare identifier that resolves to a field is an implicit-`this` field read. Over both, the unmarked form remains the borrow-pin of *`using var` initialized from a borrow*.
>
> The covered spelling does not say whether `y` adopts `x` or views it, and the compiler does not choose. The diagnostic names the remedies the source admits:
>
> - `move x` to adopt — except over a `using var` source, which is never movable out (A5);
> - a `var`/`const` view;
> - under option A, `pin x`.
>
> Over a `Task`-family source, the anonymous form is reported under [§17.13](#sec-the-discard-_)'s rejection (A6) first, and `using var _ := move x;` is never offered as its remedy.
>
> A **bare type parameter** is a class or interface type for this rule: the generic body type-checks under the class-or-interface assumption ([§28.1](#sec-where-the-owned-modifier-can-appear) clause 3). So `using var y := x;` over an `owned x: T` is rejected once, in the body, before substitution, and `move x` adopts at every instantiation — redundant at a move-only substitution and erased at an erasing one, as for the declaration marker (clauses 3–4).
>
> Where `x` is a local of **move-only value type** ([§15.3](#sec-move-only-value-types)), the unmarked form **adopts** [unchanged text follows].

Move-only kinds do not belong under the marker. A move-only binding "has no borrow reading to disambiguate" (§28.10, line 7839). `move` is rejected at its marker-free positions (§28.8.1; codegen reference line 448). And there is nothing a pin could mean over a value that cannot be viewed by reference.

The rule keys on the **declared, pre-substitution** type of the binding and on the binding's ownership state at the site. This is §28.1 clause 3's class-or-interface assumption for generic bodies, the same pre-substitution keying 0133 uses to classify set-erasing positions. The rule stays body-local (§5.3), and a generic body's acceptance never depends on its instantiation set. *(Critique round 1, B2–B4: the first draft enumerated three source kinds, exempted bare type parameters as "per-instantiation dispatch", and named `move x` unconditionally. See §8.)*

### 3.1 Option A — a contextual `pin x` marker at the `using var` right-hand side (the study's candidate e)

```bezel
using var guard := mutex.Acquire();        // unchanged: a call
using var c := move conn;                  // adoption, unchanged
using var u := pin item;                   // NEW: the borrow-pin of a named owned binding, marked
using var u := item;                       // ERROR under the core rule
using var v := conn.View();                // unchanged: a borrow-returning call pins as today
```

**Rule text (▲ appended to the core paragraph).** "`using var y := pin x;` is the **marked borrow-pin** of `x`: `y` is a borrow of `x` pinned per the subsection below, `x` retains the obligation, and the form is admitted exactly where the unmarked form is rejected — `x` a class- or interface-typed named owned binding (a local, an `owned` parameter, or an owned loop variable). `pin` over a borrow-state binding, a call, a field, a move-only or value-typed binding, or anywhere but the entire right-hand side of a `using var` / `await using var` declaration is a compile error naming this rule."

### 3.3 Option C — reuse an existing word: `using var y := scoped x;`

The study rejected `scoped` because "`scoped` freezes the source against explicit `Dispose()` as well … while the pin deliberately permits it". Re-checked precisely: **§28.14** (line 8132): "the captured local sources are **frozen** (derives: K5.7): they may not be moved from, reassigned, or disposed — the consume-site freeze (§28.3) **plus explicit disposal**"; the call-site argument "is **frozen** until the bound's scope exits" (line 8151); **§28.10** (line 7864): the pin "adds no exception to K5's two recorded shapes; the §28.14 storage admission remains the one admission whose captured sources are frozen against explicit disposal as well". The claim is exact: `scoped` is K5.7's shape (frozen against `Dispose()`), the pin is K5.3's (site-announced kills unfrozen, flag-backstopped). Also exact: `scoped` is an *argument modifier* in `ArgModifiers` (A.4, line 11417), "marks a lending mode, not an action" (§22.3, line 4713), and the §28.14 holder "must still be a `using var` local (or `this`) with the source declared before it" — so `using var y := scoped x;` would read as "`y` is the bounding holder of a lent `x`", which is a plausible reading of a pin.

**Would aligning the two freeze rules be harmless?** Two directions. *Weaken `scoped` to the pin's rule* (permit `Dispose()` of a `scoped` source): unsound — the stored callback or borrow in the bounding object can run after the source is disposed with no flag check at the invocation (§28.14's *Quiescence* argument depends on the freeze); declined. *Strengthen the pin to `scoped`'s rule* (freeze the pinned source against `Dispose()` too): sound, but (a) it adds a **third** exception to K5's recorded shapes — a kernel change (K5.7's family gains the pin), where today's text says the pin adds none; (b) it flips four rule-corpus cases that exist to witness the opposite (`s28_10_0006`, `s28_10_0012`, `s28_2_0011`, `s28_2_0012`: "an explicit Dispose of a borrow-pin's source is a site-announced kill the pin leaves unfrozen") and the `dispose_aliased_*` rows; (c) it would also have to apply to the *unchanged* pin shapes (call, field, indexer) or give one word two freeze rules after all. Under option B none of this arises. **Verdict:** C is coherent only with the kernel change (b-a), which the one-word saving does not pay for; recorded as `declined-at-proposal` (§5.6), reopenable if K5.7 is ever generalised to "every scope-bound holder".

Other existing tokens: `owned x` is the retired use-site marker (v0.246; `DECLINED.md` line 700's retirement row) and would say the opposite of a pin; `ref x` / `out x` name locations and are rejected on owned state (codegen reference line 480); `const` is a binding keyword, not an initializer marker — but `const y := x;` is precisely option B's view remedy.

### 3.4 Option D — flip the default: unmarked = adoption, the pin requires a marker

**Rule text.** §28.10's second sentence would read "An unmarked `using var y := x;` where `x` is a class- or interface-typed named owned binding **adopts**, exactly as over a move-only `x`"; K3.4's row would gain the unmarked `using var` spelling; K3.13 would need a carve-out ("an unmarked read of a class-typed binding never consumes — except at a `using var` initializer"); the pin would need a marker (A's `pin`, or nothing — the capability retired as in B).

**Soundness.** Not unsound in itself — adoption is a K3.4 transfer with the consume-site freeze. **Teaching:** it breaks "the single most important spelling fact" the code-generation reference leans on (line 448: unmarked reads never consume; `move` is required wherever a named owned binding feeds a binding, line 437) and makes `using var` the one binding keyword that retroactively changes what a read does to its source — the study's inconsistency 1 (§4 item 1), now in the opposite direction. **Silent behaviour change, quantified:** of the 240 affected sites, the 175 adoption sites change *observably for the better* (the must-use warning disappears; disposal timing is unchanged — the adopted value is disposed at the same scope end the source's auto-drop used, the `do … end` cases excepted); the **65** pin sites change behaviour, and most change **loudly** — any later read of the source becomes a moved-from read error (`s28_2_0011`'s `w.Dispose()` after `using var view := w;`, `consume_site_freeze_1537`'s `mine.N := …` is fine but a later `c` read is not). The **silent** subset is the shape where the source is never read again *and* its disposal moment differs: `do using var _ := v; end; …` in a nested block, where adoption disposes `v`'s object at the inner block's end and the pin left it to `v`'s own scope end; **4** such sites in `tests/` (`owned_collection_trypop_831_test.cpp:125`, `exception_disposal_test.cpp:1201`, `:1222`, `:1244`; the drain-loop bodies and `generic_owned_drain_990_test.cpp:101`-style `using var _ := w;` at the end of a body are not among them — same scope end). Four silent flips is small, but it is four more than B or A, and the rule it buys contradicts K3.13 and the code-generation reference's central fact. **Verdict:** declined (§5.6).

## 5. Recommendation

### 5.1 Answer to the owner's question: no new keyword is needed — recommended, as a choice against A

**Recommend option B.** The measurement is the reason. The named-binding pin over an *owned* source is, over an owned local, a freeze that `using var` on the source already states more strongly and a view that `const`/`var` already spells; over an owned parameter it is a freeze that adoption states more strongly, on a value that is live to the method's end regardless. The corpus's about 36 pin-intended sites (65 in the first pass; §2's recount) are tests of the mechanism, typed views whose source is already scope-bound, checked-disposal witnesses that a plain alias reproduces, or the vacuous-pin lint's own repros; the normative documents, the stdlib and the guides never once write the form. A keyword earns its place by naming something the reader needs that nothing else says (the study's P6); `pin` over a named owned binding would name a freeze the kernel already supplies by other spellings. Option A is the right design *if* the owner wants that freeze to stay spellable by a single token; it is recorded in full in §3.1 and its cost is one more binding-position verb, forever.

**What B makes unwritable** (critique B6). The owner should rule with these in view:

- **(a)** An owned local pinned in an *inner* scope and consumed after it: `var x := …; do using var v := x; … end; Consume(move x);`. Inside the block, the freeze after `v`'s last use has no spelling except the dead-code dummy use that rationale §5.27 reason one cites. Reason one names exactly this drift as a purpose of the pin: "a borrow's source consumed under a view the author meant to hold whole-scope".
- **(b)** A scope-long freeze of an owned parameter, owning loop variable or owning-match binding **that stays readable by its own name**. The substitute is `using var y := move x;`, under a new name.
- **(c)** The bare freeze idiom `using var _ := x;` over an owned `x` that the author means to keep. Under A it is `using var _ := pin x;`; under B it has no spelling.

The corpus uses (a)–(c) only in tests of the mechanism: `using_var_unmarked_borrow_pin_2071:177` is the one dead-view-freeze test, and there A5 on the source rejects the move anyway. The guard case is examined and not lost. Holding a lock's critical section to scope end is spelled by pinning the interior borrow, `using var v := g.Value;`, a property read that is unchanged under B and frozen by K5 item 8 / §30.12. Option A keeps (a)–(c). Its marker would cover only one of the five pin shapes, though: calls, fields, indexers and borrow-state sources stay unmarked pins. So `pin` would read as "this owned source is not adopted" rather than "this is a pin".

Under B the `using var` reader's rule has no exception left: **a producer adopts; a borrow pins; a named owned binding is marked `move`**. It is the same marker, in the same slot, that §28.3 already requires to transfer at `var y := move x;`, `y := move x;`, `M(move x)` and the arm transfer. One asymmetry remains and is deliberate (critique N1). `var y := x;` stays a silent borrow, while `using var y := x;` over an owned `x` becomes an error. `using var` is the one binding keyword that announces ownership (A5's declared intent), so it is the one place where a silent borrow contradicts the declaration.

### 5.2 Kernel classification (spec-change-process §9)

**Derivation, with no kernel row changed.** The new rejection is an admissibility restriction at a K3.4 position: K3.4 names `using var y := move x;` as the marked binding transfer. It is grounded in A5's declared intent, not in K3.13. K3.13 alone would equally reject `var y := x;`, which stays an admitted borrow (critique N1), and K3.13 continues to hold vacuously because the rejected form consumes nothing. The proposal removes the one *silent* non-consuming reading of an unmarked owned read at the one binding position whose keyword announces ownership. It adds no axiom (A5 unchanged), extends none (the pin keeps K5.3's shape for the sources it still covers), and amends no principle. Under option A the `pin` marker is likewise a derivation (a spelled K2 borrow at a K3.4 position). Option C would be a K5.7 extension and option D a K3.13 amendment, which is part of why they are declined.

### 5.3 Body-locality (proposal 0105 §1)

**Local.** The rule reads the right-hand binding's declared type (class/interface vs. bare type parameter vs. move-only vs. value), its ownership state (owned local — tracked in this body; `owned` parameter — on this body's own signature), and the presence of the marker: every fact is of the body being checked. A bare type parameter is decided once, before substitution, under §28.1 clause 3's class-or-interface assumption (line 6748), so acceptance never depends on the instantiation set. This is the pre-substitution keying proposal 0133 uses. No cross-body fact is consumed. *(The first draft exempted bare type parameters as "per-instantiation dispatch". Critique B4 replaced that, because the exemption kept the silent pin in generic bodies, and kept it with instantiation-dependent disposal timing.)*

## 7. Self-critique (adversarial pass)

**C1. "B contradicts a recorded decision — rationale §5.27 reason one — and §2 of the process says such a change is reopened only with new evidence against the stated reasons."** Granted that it touches the sentence; denied that it contradicts the reason. Reason one argues that a *declared constraint on the binding's future* cannot be inferred; B keeps every such constraint spellable: A5 on the source (`using var`), adoption (`move`), the pin over every source whose liveness is not already guaranteed. What B retires is one *spelling* of a constraint that, over an owned source, the kernel already enforces by scope end (K3.14). The new evidence is §2: zero normative, stdlib or guide uses; every corpus use is a test of the mechanism or has a spelling-preserving substitute. The proposal amends §5.27 by one clause rather than silently narrowing it, which is the process's requirement.

**C2. "The measurement's intent classification is heuristic; 'adoption intended' is the author's inference, not the test's."** True for the 14 owned-local sites; for the 85 sinks and 76 drain loops it is not an inference — the sites read `method Sink(owned c: Cell) using var k := c; end` and `for var item in arr.Drain() do using var u := item; end`, the test comments say "adopt", and Guide 01 §9 teaches `move` at the drain site. **C3. "Four silent flips under D is a small number; why is D not the simplest option?"** Because the four are not the cost. D's cost is K3.13: the code-generation reference calls "an unmarked read never consumes" the single most important spelling fact, and D makes `using var` the one keyword under which it is false, in a language whose v0.246 marker unification was done precisely to have one rule. **C4. "Under B, an owned parameter can no longer be pinned at all; §5.27 says the pin guards against maintenance drift — a later `move` of a source the author meant to hold whole-scope."** The guard survives in two forms, both stronger: `using var c := move conn;` makes every later use of `conn` an error (not only the kills), and `var view := conn;` rejects a `move conn` while `view` is live (K5.1). **C5. "The bare-type-parameter carve-out means `using var y := x;` over `owned x: T` still pins silently at a class instantiation — the ambiguity survives in generic bodies."** *Conceded in critique round 1 (B4); the carve-out is withdrawn.* §28.1 clause 3 (line 6748) has the generic body type-check under the class-or-interface assumption. **C6. "The §28.3 *Borrows* bullet (line 7065) lists `using var y := x;` among the borrow spellings and K3.13 cites it; removing one spelling from a list does not remove the state it names."** Correct, and the edit in §3.2 keeps the bullet's borrow classification for `var y := x;` / `y := x;` and marks the `using var` spelling as rejected over an owned `x` and a pin over a borrow-state `x`. No ownership state is deleted: the pinned binding over a borrow-state source is still "Owned + implicitDischarge with the borrow flag" in the compiler and a borrow in the spec.

**C8. "Option A's `pin` is a LocalDecl-level token, so `using var y := pin;` reads a local named `pin` — a parse that depends on the next token. Is that B.2-conformant?"** Yes: B.2's `old` row ("where followed by `(`") and `env` row ("the following `:` disambiguates") are one-token-lookahead contextual keywords already; `pin` followed by an Identifier is the same mechanism. Recorded so that if A is chosen the grammar comment says it.

**C9. "The proposal counts 240 affected sites but the study said ~360."** The study counted 323 bare-identifier plus 52 field-read right-hand sides over *all* `using var` sites, including borrow-state sources, prose and metavariables; this proposal's 350 matches reconcile to it (34 prose, 13 inert/move-only, ~63 unaffected, 240 affected).
