## 0. Thesis

Since v0.190 the language has had a doctrine — **calls mint, storage lends**. A call whose declared return type announces a frame produces a caller-owned frame; a read of storage lends a borrow of that storage. v0.190 applied it to the `Task` family and, in the same revision, ruled the consequence for the stored-task idiom: *"a **stored** task is re-exposed through a property, field, or slot, never a method return"* (changelog v0.190). Rationale records the cost by name: *"the task stored-view is routed to the property face — a recorded cost of v0.190"* (rationale §5.26, A2's row-reason gloss).

Axiom **A2** keys that doctrine to the `Task` family **only**. Its recorded reason is one sentence:

> Uniform calls-mint is unsound for iterables — the stored-collection-view idiom (`Items(): IEnumerable<Item>` returning `_items`) is legitimate and common (axiom register, v0.255)

That reason is not a soundness argument. It is a **migration-cost** argument about one idiom — the identical idiom v0.190 migrated for tasks, by the identical remedy, at a cost the record calls acceptable. The proposal is to finish the doctrine: **iterable and enumerator returns mint at every callee kind, exactly as `Task`-family returns do; the stored view respells as a property; A2 becomes a tombstone.**

What licenses reopening a recorded decision here is not a better argument about `Items()`. It is the **ledger of what the split has cost since** — §1.

## 2. The rule

**Amendment sketch (§28.3, replacing *Task-family calls produce owned frames* and deleting *Iterable-interface returns at instance and extension callees*):**

> **Calls mint for the mint-announcing families (derives: [K6.2](#sec-kernel-k6) + [K6.3](#sec-kernel-k6); no axiom).** A **call** whose declared return type — read from the signature *before* generic substitution, with nullability stripped — is a **mint-announcing type** produces an **owned** frame at the call site, whatever the callee kind: instance, static, extension, interface-dispatched, or an invocation through a function value. No factory warning is emitted: the declared type is the announcement. The mint-announcing types are the **`Task` family** (`Task`, `Task<T>`, `OwningTask<T>`) and the **iterable family** — the §29.3/§30.7 iterable and enumerator interfaces (`IEnumerable<T>`, `IEnumerator<T>`, `IAsyncEnumerable<T>`, `IAsyncEnumerator<T>`, `IOwningEnumerable<T>`, `IOwningEnumerator<T>`, `IOwningAsyncEnumerable<T>`, `IOwningAsyncEnumerator<T>`) and the lazy-query type. Reads of **storage** — a property, field, indexer, or local of such a type — are not calls and remain **borrows of their storage**. Callee-side, an unmarked mint-announcing return type is itself ownership-transferring for the frame: every return path must supply a fresh or owned frame, or `null` under a nullable declared type; **returning a borrow — a bare field or property read, a plain parameter, a captured binding — is a compile error**, with the remedy named: re-expose stored state through a **property**, a field, or a collection slot, whose reads are borrows. An explicit `owned` on such a return remains admissible with identical semantics — the same contract stated with emphasis (`GetEnumerator(): owned IEnumerator<T>`, §29.1; `Domain.Spawn(...): owned Task`, stdlib §6.8).

Three consequential edits ride it:

- **§27.1.1** — the announcement-bearing return-slot rule reads *the mint-announcing families* in place of *the `Task` family*: covariance applies within a family (`() -> IEnumerable<int>` is usable as… nothing wider; `() -> Task<int>` as `() -> Task`) and never across a family boundary. The `Task`-family sentence's reasoning transfers verbatim, the words *"calls-mint announcement"* now covering both families.
- **§27.6** — the formation bar reads *a callee whose declared return type is mint-announcing*, at **any** callee kind. The instance carve-out sentence **deletes** (instance iterable calls no longer lend). The recorded open question — *"Iterable-to-iterable bridging and the iterable-typed slot's own invocation classification remain an open question"* — **deletes**: bridging is within-family covariance, and the slot's invocation classification is the mint the row now claims.
- **§28.3's bare-parameter exclusion** — the family list gains the iterable family, exactly as its own text anticipates (*"a future ruling extending the family extends this exclusion with it"*). Residuals 6 and 7 **delete**; #3246 is discharged by the same edit that discharges the rest.

**Kernel classification.** Pure derivation: K6.2 (the declared deferred-activation type is the announcement) + K6.3 (the frame is minted at the call) + K1.7/K2.3 (the callee may not re-expose storage through a minting return) + K3.13 (announcement by declared type; no silent consume). **No axiom is added; A2 is retired to a tombstone** — the register's census moves to eleven live, three tombstones, and A2's tombstone row points at the graduating revision's changelog entry (the register's own stated discipline for retirement).

## 3. The v0.223 owning-head rejection and its `Drain()` carve retire naturally

C1 folds the v0.223 incoherence into this proposal rather than patching it twice. The evidence, re-verified at the v0.255 snapshot:

**(a) The carve's §29.11 ground does not fit `Drain()`.** §28.3 files `Drain()` under §29.11: *"The standard library's deferred-operator results (`Where`, `Select`, …), `Drain()`, and `ReceiveAll()` are outside this paragraph: they are the provenance-tracked affine lazy values of §29.11, whose obligation is discharged at the single consumption point (`Drain()` — an instance owning-head return — lives in that recorded carve)."* But §29.11 defines its subject as *"a **lazy query** — a value produced by a **deferred LINQ operator** (stdlib §5, e.g. `var q := xs.Where(...)`)"*, and every normative apparatus it then states — the three-item consumption-points list, the terminal-accessor provenance warning, the materialization remedy — is keyed to that definition. `Drain()` is not a deferred LINQ operator and produces no lazy query: it is an ownership-transferring egress member declared on the owning collections (stdlib §4.5/§4.7/§4.9/§4.10/§4.11). The carve names a home whose door it does not fit.

**(b) §28.3 files `Drain()` under a *second*, different ground, two paragraphs earlier.** *Iterator and iterable returns* — the **static** iterator convention — ends: *"The standard library's iterator-returning methods (`IEnumerable<T>` extensions like `Where`, `Select`, etc., and the owning-collection `Drain()` methods returning `IOwningEnumerable<T>`) follow this convention."* `Drain()` is an **instance** method. So §28.3 files the same member under the static factory convention in one paragraph and under §29.11's affine-lazy carve in the next, while its own owning-head rule rejects the declaration outright. Three readings, one member.

**(c) The prescribed remedy is unsound at `Drain()`, and the spec's own example is the demonstration.** The v0.223 rejection's remedy is *"expose the stored view at the concrete collection type or the non-owning interface … or declare the return `owned` to transfer."* §14.4's `OwningCache<T>` example takes the second branch:

```bezel
public method Drain(): owned IOwningEnumerable<T>
  return _items.Drain();
end Drain;
```

Under stdlib §4.5, *"`Drain()` on `OwningArray<T>` returns **the array itself** at that static type — no separate enumerable object."* So the returned value **is** `_items` — the receiver's `owned` field. Announcing it `owned` transfers to the caller an object the receiver still owns and whose destructor cascade will dispose (§28.5): two owners. Read the other way, it is an unannounced take-out of an owned field, which K9.1 admits only as `move x.f` under K9.2's restoration rule. Either reading rejects the example. The `owned`-return remedy cannot be the answer for the member the carve exists to protect. *(Re-verified: the spec declares `Drain()` with `owned` at exactly **one** site — §14.4's example — while the stdlib declares it **unmarked** at all **six** of its owning-collection homes. The two documents disagree about the member the rule is about.)*

**(d) The corpus.** Re-measured at HEAD over `tests/**/*.bzl` plus Bezel source reconstructed from `.cpp` string literals: **99** unmarked owning-head-returning method declarations, of which **25 are instance or extension** — precisely the shape §28.3 says is *"rejected at the declaration"* — spread over **19 files**, and only **2** of those sit in reject-named files. The suite accepts the rest. In the normative docs the count is **8** unmarked instance owning-head declarations, all in the stdlib (the six `Drain()`s, `ReceiveAll()`, and one more). *(The charter cites the review's figure of 92 accepted declarations; this re-measurement finds 99 unmarked / 97 outside reject-named files by the methodology stated here. The two agree on the fact and differ on the count; the fact is that the corpus and the stdlib both declare, at scale, a shape the rule rejects.)*

**Under unification all three grounds become unnecessary.** An instance owning-head return mints, like every other iterable return, at every callee kind. There is no "borrowed owning view" to reason about, because there is no unmarked-instance-lends rule left: the declaration-site rejection **deletes**, the §29.11 carve **deletes**, and the deferred operators keep §29.11's affine machinery on the ground that actually fits them — they *are* lazy queries.

**One real cost, stated plainly (§5 consequence 3):** `Drain()` must return a genuinely fresh frame. The `OwningArray<T>` optimization — return `this` at the owning static type — is incompatible with a minting return, because the `for` desugaring binds the minted frame to a `using var __source` (§29.2) that disposes it at loop end; if the frame *were* the collection, loop end would dispose the collection. The stdlib sentence changes and the implementation allocates one small drain-view frame per `Drain()` call. That is the price, and it is the price of `Drain()` having a coherent classification at all.

## 4. The stored view respells as a property — the v0.190 precedent, cited

A2's whole recorded reason is the stored-view idiom, `Items(): IEnumerable<Item>` returning `_items`. Under unification that method mints, so the idiom respells:

```bezel
// before (v0.255): a method whose unmarked instance iterable return lends the receiver's field
public method Items(): IEnumerable<Item>
  return _items;
end Items;

// after: the storage face — a property read, which lends (calls mint, storage lends)
public readonly property Items: IEnumerable<Item> => _items;
```

This is **exactly** the migration v0.190 performed for tasks, by exactly the same remedy, with the record already written:

- The rule: *"a **stored** task is re-exposed through a property, field, or slot, never a method return (generalizing v0.184's TCS source-owns-task/`.Task`-property decision)"* — changelog v0.190.
- The cost, acknowledged at the time: *"the task stored-view is routed to the property face — **a recorded cost of v0.190**"* — rationale §5.26's A2 gloss.
- The migration category: v0.190's own changelog lists *"stored-task borrow returns from non-`async` methods and lambdas — **re-expose through a property**"* under newly **rejected**, and the compiler-catch-up rode #1786.

The respell is not a workaround; it lands the idiom on the face the language already designates for it. **Axiom A4** — *"Accessor read faces are lend-only: property and indexer types carry no `owned`, and a getter may not return a fresh owned value"* — is the exact complement: the property face is guaranteed to lend and structurally cannot mint. So after unification the doctrine reads as one sentence with no exceptions: **calls mint, storage lends; the storage face is the accessor face.** A2's retirement makes A4 load-bearing rather than incidental.

## 5. Worked consequences

1. **Instance and extension iterator methods become legal unmarked.** Today *"a **freshly-produced frame** … returned through an *unmarked* instance or extension iterable return is a compile error … an instance or extension **iterator method** therefore declares `owned IEnumerable<T>`."* Under unification the unmarked spelling is the announcement and the `owned` is optional emphasis. Measured: **22** unmarked instance/extension iterator bodies and **20** unmarked instance/extension fresh-frame bodies (`return new …`) in `tests/` are accepted by the suite today against that rule; unification makes them legal as written.
2. **`owned`-marked instance iterable returns are unaffected** — emphasis, not contrast, the v0.190 `Domain.Spawn` disposition verbatim. Measured: **16** in `tests/`, **9** in the spec, **6** in the stdlib. Zero migration.
3. **`Drain()` and `ReceiveAll()` become ordinary minting members** and leave §29.11 for §28.3's producers row. `Drain()`'s fresh-frame requirement is §3's stated cost; `ReceiveAll()` already returns a fresh drain enumerable (stdlib §6.9's single-consumer rule is a *channel* invariant, not a frame-identity one) and needs no implementation change. §29.11 keeps its affine machinery for the deferred LINQ operators, which is what it was written for.
4. **The static iterator convention is unchanged in effect and simpler in statement.** Static iterable returns already mint via the factory rule; after unification they mint via the same rule as everything else, and *Iterator and iterable returns* shrinks to the sentence that `owned` on such a return is redundant emphasis.
5. **The deferred LINQ operators are unaffected.** They are declared as extension members on `IEnumerable<T>` with unmarked iterable returns and produce fresh composed frames — a minting classification is what they already need and what §29.11's single-consumption obligation already assumes. Measured: **17** unmarked instance/extension iterable-returning declarations in the stdlib document, of which ~10 are the callback-free deferred operators.
6. **§28.14 is untouched.** *Call-bounded × deferred activation* already treats task frames and iterator frames as one kind under K6.1/K6.2, bounding each by its own life. Unification removes an asymmetry §28.14 never had.
7. **Interface conformance is unchanged.** The announcement is by declared return type, which signature matching already reads; no marker moves.

**The property respell is the whole migration, and it is 13 test declarations.** Of those 13, three are `GetEnumerator(): IEnumerator<T> return this;` — the self-returning-enumerator idiom (`tests/codegen/iter_selfreturning_wrap_2384_test.cpp`, `tests/codegen/iter_temp_source_reclaim_2424_test.cpp`), which §9 Q2 treats separately because it is **already** a seam under the current `owned IEnumerator<T>` protocol spelling and unification neither creates nor worsens it. The remaining ten are `return field;` stored views in the ownership-area tests that pin the v0.223 rules themselves (`owning_iterable_head_borrow_return_2369`, `deferred_activation_frame_return_1660`, `drain_view_residual_surfaces_2175`) — i.e., the tests whose *subject* this proposal retires; they are re-pointed at the new rule, not merely respelled.

In the normative documents the respell surface is smaller still: the stored-view idiom appears as an **illustration** (rationale §12.10's `method Items(): IEnumerable<Item> => return _items;`, quoted twice more in the register and changelog) rather than as a declared API. **No stdlib member is a stored iterable view returned from a method**: the stdlib's 17 unmarked instance/extension iterable returns are the deferred operators, the six `Drain()`s, and `ReceiveAll()` — all minting members, none of them stored views.

**What deletes.** Ledger entries 1, 5 (the carve-out sentence and the open-question sentence), 6, 7, and 8, plus A2's register row (→ tombstone) — by paragraph count: one long §28.3 paragraph, one §28.3 parenthetical, one §28.3 italic residual paragraph, two §27.6 sentences, one §28.3 producers-row parenthetical, one §28.3 sentence in *Iterator and iterable returns*, and one axiom row. Entries 2, 3, and 4 **generalize** (one word each: *family* → *families*). Entry 9 is subsumed.

## 7. Alternatives, each declined with its ground

- **Alt A — narrow the rejection** (C1's option (a)): keep A2, keep instance-lends, and narrow the v0.223 owning-head declaration-site rejection so that `Drain()`-shaped members are admissible without the §29.11 carve. *For:* small, local, no migration. *Against:* it repairs §3(c) (the unsound remedy) and §3(a) (the misfit ground) while leaving §3(b) (the double filing) and the entire §1 ledger untouched — and the narrowing itself needs a new predicate distinguishing "an owning-head return that drains its receiver" from "a borrowed owning view", which is precisely the drain-through-borrow semantics v0.223 declined-not-foreclosed as *"every candidate answer silently consumes receiver-owned elements or reinvents an obligation-splitting mechanism the kernel lacks."* **Net: buys a local repair by re-opening the question v0.223 correctly refused to answer.**
- **Alt B — `owned` on static iterable returns** (C1's option (b)): require the marker on the static face too, making every frame-minting iterable return marker-announced and dissolving the fences by making the *marker*, not the family, the announcement. *For:* one uniform announcement rule, no family concept at all. *Against:* it is v0.190's own declined alternative transplanted — *"requiring `owned` on every fresh-task-returning signature … churns every async signature in every program … and silently reintroduces the leak wherever the marker is omitted"* (rationale §12.10) — and here it churns **421** static iterable declarations in `tests/` plus every stdlib deferred operator, to buy a marker whose omission would reinstate the leak. It also collides with §28.3's own recorded verdict that requiring `owned` on iterator returns is *"redundant noise over an announced transfer."* **Net: the largest migration of the three options, for the announcement the declared type already carries.**
- **Alt C — status quo, plus #3246's fence** (do nothing beyond the defect train). *For:* zero risk; #3246 closes the two admitted leaks; the fences are individually cheap. *Against:* this is the option the ledger indicts. #3246 would be the **fourth** fence for one seam, it is conservative by its own text (*"conservative rejection of sound instance-callee inhabitants at such slots"*), and it leaves §3's three-way filing of `Drain()`, the unsound `owned`-return remedy, and 25 corpus declarations of a rejected shape all standing. **Net: the honest decline path; if chosen, rationale §12.10 gains the re-examined-and-kept record and this proposal is its evidence.** The ruling is Alt C declined.

## 8. Migration and compatibility (changelog house categories)

- **Newly rejected:** an unmarked **instance or extension** iterable/enumerator return whose body returns a **borrow** (a stored view) — remedy: the property face, diagnostic naming it, exactly v0.190's stored-task wording. Corpus impact: 13 test declarations (§6).
- **Newly accepted:** every unmarked instance/extension iterable return of a **fresh frame** — iterator bodies, composed operator results, fresh enumerables (42 test declarations rejected by the letter today, accepted by the suite); every unmarked instance/extension **owning-head** return (25 test declarations, 8 stdlib declarations); iterable-to-iterable method-reference bridging and iterable-typed function-slot invocation, both currently a recorded open question.
- **Behavior change on letter-legal programs:** an unmarked instance iterable call at a `for` source now binds a caller-owned frame rather than a receiver-sourced borrow. Where the callee genuinely returned a stored view, the program is *newly rejected* (above) rather than silently changed — the compile error is the migration signal.
- **Stdlib contract change:** `Drain()` returns a fresh drain-view frame rather than the receiver at the owning static type (§3's cost). `Drain()`'s observable semantics — drains as iterated, partial iteration leaves the remainder — are unchanged and are the reason the fresh frame is required.
- **Doc-only:** the fence deletions, A2's tombstone, the §12.10 reversal record.
- **Unchanged:** every `Task`-family rule; every static iterable return; every `owned`-marked return; K1–K9; §28.14; every disposal point.

## 9. Drafting seams for the graduation train

The rulings are closed; these are the two places the drafter must make a call and record it.

- **Q1 — the lazy-query type's place in the mint-announcing list.** §29.11's lazy query is already affine and already discharged at a single consumption point, which is a *stronger* obligation than minting. Draft it **inside** the mint-announcing family (recommended: one classification, and §28.3's producers row already lists "a lazy query" beside the iterable interfaces at every site) with §29.11's single-consumption rule stated as the frame's discharge discipline rather than as a separate ownership regime — or **beside** it, keeping §29.11 self-contained. The recommendation is *inside*; the cost is one careful sentence reconciling "discharged at the single consumption point" with "discharged by the standard rules".
- **Q2 — the self-returning-enumerator idiom.** A class implementing both `IEnumerable<T>` and `IEnumerator<T>` returns `this` from `GetEnumerator()`. Under the interface's declared `GetEnumerator(): owned IEnumerator<T>` (§29.1) this **already** announces a transfer of the receiver, and the compiler already special-cases it (`tests/codegen/iter_selfreturning_wrap_2384_test.cpp` — three corpus sites; fix #2384/v0.235). Unification does not change its classification, but it does remove the "instance returns lend" reading that made the shape look benign. Draft the graduation train to **state the idiom's status explicitly** (recommended: a §29.1 sentence recording that the self-returning form is a receiver-borrow special case of the protocol, exempt from the minting classification because the enumerator *is* the receiver and the `for` desugaring's `using var __source` is a §28.10 pin over it, not an obligation-assuming bind) rather than leaving it to be inferred. If the drafter cannot make that exemption precise, file it as a defect rather than improvising: it is a pre-existing seam, not one this proposal creates.
