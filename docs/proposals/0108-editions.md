## 0. Charter

An **edition** is a per-module pin on the *source language*: the compiler reads a module's files under the language version that module names, one compiler serves every edition at once, and artifacts compiled at different editions interoperate without restriction. It is Rust's mechanism, and the only one the industry has found that lets a live language make source-breaking changes without either freezing (Go) or forking its ecosystem (Python 2/3, Swift 3).

This document **designs the mechanism and proposes nothing be activated.** Its concrete recommendation is small: decide now where the pin will live, and reserve the one artifact-interface field the mechanism needs. The first half is the time-sensitive one — a pin's home and its absence rule are settled cheaply while the corpus is one project's, and expensively afterwards; the field itself is cheap whenever it lands, and this document does not pretend otherwise (this proposal's §1). No edition is declared, no boundary is scheduled, no rule of the current language changes, and §2.8.5's ABI non-goal is untouched (this proposal's §3). Method follows 0103/0104/0106: candidates worked against the landed text, priced, recommended with the doubt visible.

## 1. Why now

**The register's own justification has a named expiry date.** 0106 §4 found six decision records justifying a decision by citing the absence of external users — rationale §3.13 (*"with no external users a deprecation window serves nobody"*), rationale §9.5 (*"the first external user makes the pair permanent"*), two changelog ledgers, proposals 0083 and 0100 — with *"no successor policy anywhere in ten normative documents, 105 proposals, or five registers."* The absence is total: at HEAD, `edition` occurs **zero** times in all ten documents of `docs/` (re-verified: `grep -ric edition docs/*.md` returns 0 for every normative document).

**The kernel is still gaining rules, and two different things get confused here — so this proposal separates them.** §14.6's bounded-instantiation-closure rule carried, at this proposal's v0.262 pin, *"(Compiler status: not yet enforced — tracked #3271.)"*: the spec already states a rejection and the compiler has not caught up. (That note was deleted at v0.287 as editorial — [#3408](https://github.com/s8lap/bezel-lang/issues/3408) — once both halves of the §14.6 check had landed; the same changelog entry records that §22.12's sibling note, [#2331](https://github.com/s8lap/bezel-lang/issues/2331), *"is untouched — its arm is still unenforced"*, so the shape the argument needs still has a live instance.) The v0.263 process-doc text draws exactly this line and this proposal adopts it rather than blurring it — *"Do not confuse a ramp with a **spec-ahead-of-impl** delta: a rule the spec states as a rejection and the compiler has not yet enforced is item 3's business, not a ratchet"*. So §14.6 is evidence that the kernel is still **gaining rules**; it is not an example of a warning becoming an error. The genuine ratchet material is the standing **mandated-warning** set: §15.4's store lint, whose warning is normative while *"the program remains accepted"*, and the three lints rationale §5.28 records, for which *"No per-site suppression spelling exists"*. Promoting any of those to a rejection would break programs that legitimately compile today — pre-1.0 a Ratchet-class revision, post-1.0 a change with nowhere to go without a boundary.

**Swift is the counterexample, and it is a sequencing lesson.** 0106 §4.2, in one line: *"Swift 3→4 is the sequencing warning: a migrator without a pin still hurts."* Swift shipped a source migrator with no per-module pin, so an ecosystem's move was all-at-once — a package could not be at Swift 3 while its dependency was at Swift 4 (secondary-sourced). The pin converts a migration from a coordinated ecosystem event into a per-module, per-schedule, mechanically-assisted edit.

**Today it costs one reserved field and zero behavior — and the honest form of the urgency is narrower than "reserve or pay later".** The mechanism's whole compile-time surface is (i) a place a module names its edition, (ii) one interface fact recording the edition its source was read at, and (iii) a rule that this fact never affects another module's type-checking. With one edition in existence, (i) is a clause nobody writes, (ii) is a constant, and (iii) is vacuously true. This proposal declines to claim that (ii) is expensive later: by its own §2.2 the field is provenance that no consumer's compilation reads, so a late arrival degrades gracefully — an artifact without it makes no edition claim, the posture §2.8.6's **Degradation** paragraph takes for absent contract records, though that paragraph's own subject is a *carriage capability gap* and its stated cost (*"a wall of clause additions"*) has no analogue for a field with no verdict consequence. **What is genuinely cheap now and dear later is (i)**: the pin's home and the absence rule bind every file that will ever be written, so they are a one-revision ruling today and a mass source edit — or a permanent second mechanism — once a corpus exists that this project does not control. Decide it while it is free; land the field whenever §2.8 is next open. Meanwhile most of the normative vocabulary is already written — 0106 §4.1's finding, verified: §2.8.6 supplies the verdicts (*"classified **identical**, **preserving**, **widening**, or **breaking**, in that severity order"*) and their status (*"the verdicts are normative, so that no two conforming tools classify one delta differently"*), §2.8.3 rule 3 supplies the interop invariant an edition must never cross, and §2.8.2 supplies the carriage.

## 2. The design

### 2.1 (a) Where the pin lives

**Candidate A ▲ — a module-header clause.** §2.1: *"Every source file declares its module on its first non-comment line; any `import` declarations follow the module declaration"*, with Appendix A.2's `SourceFile ::= ModuleDecl Import* TopDecl*` and `ModuleDecl ::= "module" QualifiedName ";"`. Sketch, spelling deferred to a syntax pass:

```
ModuleDecl    ::= "module" QualifiedName EditionClause? ";"      /* ▲ */
EditionClause ::= "edition" EditionName
```

```bezel
module BankingApp.Transactions edition 2;   // ▲ sketch only, not a proposed spelling
```

`edition` need not become a reserved word: like `as` in §2.2's alias form, it is decided from position in a header the parser is already inside — the *decided-from-tokens* posture §2.7 uses for chain heads.

**Candidate B — a build-request property.** §2.8.4 rule 1 is the landed precedent for putting a compilation-shaping property outside the source: *"Whether a compilation produces an executable or a library ([§2.4](../Bezel_Language_Spec.md#sec-compilation-unit)) is a property of the *build request*, not of the source text; how the request is spelled is part of [§2.6](../Bezel_Language_Spec.md#sec-build-phases)'s implementation-defined build-tool surface."* Concretely: a key in proposal 0040's Stage-1 manifest, which is where Rust puts it (`Cargo.toml`).

**Candidate C — the artifact interface only.** No source and no build surface; the compiler records the edition it read each module at.

**Evaluation.** *C is not a pin*: it records an edition but nothing selects one, so every compilation happens at the newest edition and no source is ever frozen — the status quo with extra metadata. C is not a competing design but the *reservation-only subset* of A or B, and it is what this proposal recommends taking now (this proposal's §2.2). It must not be mistaken for a decision about the selector: taking C without deciding A-vs-B is how a project acquires a format field nobody can populate.

*B is workable, and is declined as a preference rather than on a rule.* The temptation is to decline it by citing §2.8.5's conditional-compilation bullet — *"**Conditional compilation (`#if`, feature flags).** One source text, one meaning: a file that means different things under different flag sets defeats spec-derived testing; platform variance belongs in the build tool selecting *which files* compile, not in files that change meaning."* That bullet is **adjacent posture, not a governing decline**, and this proposal declines to over-read it: its subject is *intra-file* conditionality (one file, several meanings within it, chosen per flag set), and its stated reason — defeating spec-derived testing — is not obviously triggered by a whole-file pin under which each file still has exactly one meaning per edition. Nor is the stronger layering claim available: a normative rule keyed on a build-request property *can* be stated, and §2.8.4 rule 1 states one two paragraphs above — *"An **executable** build requires exactly one qualifying `Main`"* — while leaving the spelling implementation-defined. §2.8.1 rule 2's closure-by-compilation likewise already makes *which files constitute a module* a build-supplied fact. And B can carry A1's absence rule unchanged: Rust fixes absence normatively at its first edition, from a manifest.

What is left is a preference with reasons, not a rule. **Meaning-locality:** under A a `.bzl` file determines its own language from its own bytes, so reader, reviewer, test harness and grep see what the compiler sees, and no rule of the form "under edition N, this means X" is keyed on an input the specification does not describe. §2.8.4 rule 1's precedent is real but narrower than it looks — artifact kind is a consequence-level property, where an edition changes what a declaration *means*. **The agreement rule as an asset:** A's whole-module rule (below) diagnoses a mismatch at the exact file, where a manifest key is silent about the files it governs. Neither reason is decisive against B, and Q1 puts the choice to the owner.

*A carries one obligation and one friction.* The obligation: a module is many files (§2.1 — *"Multiple files with identical `module` declarations **presented to the module's one compilation** … belong to the same module"*), so a per-file clause needs a **whole-module agreement rule** — every file presented to a module's one compilation pins the same edition, disagreement being a compile error naming both files; checkable at parse time from the header alone, the same shape as §2.8.1 rule 3's duplicate-name diagnosis. The friction: the clause must be optional, or the design retroactively invalidates every existing `.bzl` file and every example in ten documents.

**Recommendation: A, in the form A1 ▲ — an optional module-header clause whose absence is *normatively* edition 1, never a build-supplied default.** That distinction is the whole design: under A1 a file with no clause means edition 1 on every toolchain, forever, so meaning stays a function of source text alone — the meaning-locality preference above, held to rather than surrendered at the first convenience. A `--edition` flag that *selected* the language for unpinned files would give up exactly that property (and would sit close enough to §2.8.5's bullet to deserve the argument it never had); one that merely *checks* (rejecting files not pinned at N, as workspace policy) does not, and is admissible tooling. Consequences: the current corpus is edition 1 with zero edits; the first pin ever written is `edition 2`; and writing that line is the first action of the boundary's migrator (this proposal's §2.5) — one deterministic line per module header, which is why the pin's home must be mechanically writable. Both A and B pass that test; the choice between them is the meaning-locality preference, and Q1 leaves it open.

**Sub-question, flagged not decided — SCC mixing.** §2.5 makes an import-cycle SCC one compilation over several modules, so under a per-module pin one invocation may read files at two editions. That is sound — an edition changes only the map from source text to declared facts (this proposal's §2.4), and each file is read under its own module's edition, exactly as separately-compiled artifacts already mix — but it is a real front-end requirement (an edition-parameterized parse per file, not per invocation). The cheap alternative is a normative SCC-wide agreement rule, restrictive and liftable later. Recommendation: per-module pin; owner ruling requested (Q4).

### 2.2 (b) The artifact-interface field, and the interop rule

**What §2.8.2 must carry: one fact — the edition of the *source* the artifact was compiled from.** Not the edition a consumer must use; not per-member; not a range. §2.8.2's format latitude already covers it: *"The interface's required capability is semantic; its format is implementation-defined (source text, serialized AST, IR, or any mix)."*

**And the honest statement of what the field is for: it is never an input to another module's compilation.** §2.8.2's clauses are what a consumer needs to type-check uses of the public surface, instantiate its generics, and describe its dependencies; under the attribution rule below a consumer reads a dependency's edition for none of them, nor for any diagnostic. A module's *own* edition is of course an input to its own compilation — but that comes from its own source (this proposal's §2.1), never from an artifact. So the carried field is for diagnostics *about artifacts*, for the §2.8.6 report and the migration tooling that consumes it, and for auditability of the invariance rule, which is unenforceable if no artifact records what it was compiled at. It is deliberately *not* claimed to be expensive to add later (this proposal's §1).

**The interop rule.** Artifacts compiled at different editions link and interoperate freely, with no conversion, no shim, and no restriction on which types may cross: an edition fixes the meaning of *source text* at the compilation that reads it, so by the time anything reaches an artifact boundary it is a declared fact, and declared facts are edition-independent. Equivalently, and this is what an implementation checks itself against: **an edition may change source-level meaning only, never interface-level identity or the meaning of any fact the interface carries.**

What it does **not** claim: that two modules' *own sources* are judged by one language — precisely what a pin exists to prevent. Free interop is a property of artifacts (they link), values (they cross), and facts (they mean one thing everywhere); a consumer may see its *own* declaration rejected where a differently-pinned neighbour sees a warning, with nothing about the dependency's artifact changed.

**What can never differ across editions.** Each row is a landed rule the invariance protects.

| Invariant | Landed rule | Why an edition may not touch it |
|---|---|---|
| **Type identity** | §2.8.3 rule 3: *"A type is identified program-wide by its **declaring module name, its qualified type name … and its generic arity**"* | The edition is not in the tuple and may never join it. Two editions never fork a type: `Base.Money` compiled at edition 1 *is* `Base.Money` consumed at edition 2, so §2.8.3's worked diamond behaves identically across a mixed-edition closure. |
| **Runtime oneness** | §2.8.3 rule 4 (*"Identity is one type at runtime, too."*); §2.8.4 rule 5 (*"One copy per process"*) | One static state, one recorded initialization, one runtime identity — per *type*, never per (type, edition). Anything else silently duplicates statics: the defect 0034 filed as #1513. |
| **The type system's meaning at interfaces** | §2.8.2 clause 1's carried surface — signatures, ownership modifiers, default argument values, variance and constraints, base types, `const` values, contract records, purity | `owned` means `owned` in every edition. An edition may change how a modifier is *spelled*, never what the carried fact means to a consumer. |
| **Derived-property verdicts** | §3.6 rule 1 (*"Computed, never declared."*), rule 3 (a flip *"is a breaking change for clients relying on it, exactly as a signature change is"*) | If an edition changed the computation, one type would carry two verdicts and §2.8.6's *"derived-property verdict lost"* row would fire at a consumer that changed nothing. |
| **Module identity and closure** | §2.8.1 rules 1–5; §2.8.3 rule 1 | One module, one compilation, one artifact; duplicate-name detection; the implementation-provided modules of rule 5. None of it is edition-indexed. |
| **The ABI** | §2.8.5's non-goal (this proposal's §3) | Unchanged in both directions: editions neither require ABI stability nor supply it. Recompile-the-world remains the model at every edition. |

**The test this yields — the interface-invariance test ▲, in two clauses.**

1. **Carriage.** *If a proposed change is visible in the artifact interface, an edition cannot host it.* A change altering a signature, a carried fact, or a derived-property verdict is a plain breaking change travelling the ordinary revision route with its §2.8.6 verdict. This clause is mechanical — §2.8.6 already computes exactly this visibility.
2. **Locality ▲.** *A change to how any rule evaluated over the compilation closure treats an unchanged carried fact is edition-eligible only if the changed verdict is reported at a declaration of the module being compiled and is decided by that module's own edition.* No rule may consult a **dependency artifact's** edition.

Clause 2 exists because clause 1 alone is under-inclusive, and the gap is where this document's headline payoff lives. A warn→deny promotion (this proposal's §2.4) changes no carried fact and no fact's meaning, so it passes clause 1 unconditionally — yet a rule like §14.6 is *"checked over the compilation closure [§2.8.3](../Bezel_Language_Spec.md#sec-transitive-closure-and-type-identity) rule 1 supplies"*, its verdict computed from facts spanning modules that may carry several editions. Without clause 2 the design must choose between two unacceptable answers: evaluate at the *consuming* edition, and one unchanged interface acquires different consequences per consumer; evaluate at the *declaring* edition, and a consumer's compilation must read a dependency's recorded edition, making the field load-bearing and contradicting this subsection.

**The edition-attribution rule ▲, which clause 2 is stated in terms of.** *Every diagnostic is evaluated at the edition of the module holding the declaration at which it is reported* — never at a consumer's edition for a dependency's declaration, never at a dependency's edition for a consumer's declaration. §14.6 already writes its own diagnostic in the shape the rule needs: the rejection is *"a **compile error at the declaration holding the edge**"*, and that declaration belongs to exactly one module, which under §2.8.1 rule 1 has exactly one compilation, whose sources carry its pin. So the input the verdict needs is source-local in every case, and a dependency's artifact field is never consulted.

Two consequences, both load-bearing elsewhere in this document. **Q6 stands**: the edition is not an input to any consumer's compilation, so it does not belong in 0040's interface-hash cutoff key. And **the interop rule survives intact**, because per-consumer differences are confined to how a consumer's *own* declarations are judged — the dependency's artifact, its interface, and every fact crossing the boundary are unchanged.

*One reading is this proposal's own and is flagged as such* (CLAUDE.md rule 1): an instantiation-request edge runs from a declaration to a generic that declaration names or infers, so the source's module must reach the target's, and a *cycle* of such edges implies a cycle of module dependencies — which §2.5 makes one SCC and one compilation. If it holds, every §14.6 rejection site and every declaration in its cycle sit in a single compilation, so a cross-compilation edition mismatch cannot arise and the SCC-mixing sub-question (Q4) is the whole of it. The attribution rule is stated to decide the case **either way**, keying on the reported declaration rather than on the cycle; if the reading is wrong, only this paragraph is.

### 2.3 (c) The relationship to §2.8.6's delta classes

Two readings: an edition bump is a **new delta class** (a fifth verdict), or an **orthogonal axis** leaving every verdict unchanged. Worked: take module `M` at edition 1, recompiled at edition 2 with the boundary's migration applied. By the interface-invariance test a meaning-preserving migration changes no carried fact, so the delta over §2.8.6's compared surface is **identical**, the only difference between the interfaces being the recorded edition; and if the author also changed a signature in the same commit, the delta is that change's verdict, computed exactly as today. The invariance rule forecloses a third possibility by construction — so a fifth verdict would have to answer, for every existing row, "and across editions?", where the answer is always "unchanged". A verdict class that never changes a verdict is not a class.

**Recommendation: orthogonal axis, with one row added ▲**, modelled on the row §2.8.6 already has for the regenerable interior:

| Delta | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| recorded source edition changed, all compared facts unchanged | preserving — **informational** ▲ | Provenance, not contract. The module's source was read under a different edition; nothing a consumer type-checks against, links against, or soundly relies on differs. |

The precedent is the landed row for the regenerable interior, whose reason transfers verbatim: *"The regenerable interior: no declared contract changed. The classification's claims stop at declared contracts; it never compares the behavior of bodies."* The paragraph and the row say one thing: over §2.8.6's **compared surface** the delta is *identical*, while the reported **whole-artifact verdict** is *preserving — informational*, because the tool has something true to report (provenance changed) that is not a contract change — the regenerable-interior row's exact shape, and a qualifier the table already uses rather than a fifth verdict.

Two clarifications the row needs. **The edition field is provenance, not compared surface** — a tool reports the edition *pair* alongside the verdict, as `bezel diff-api` already reports level-layered verdicts, rather than folding it into the maximum. And **editions are not a third conformance level**: §2.8.6's layered verdict exists because *consumption* of some facts is level-gated (Appendix D.2's contract-proving feature), while the edition axis governs what source means at the *producer*. A verdict at a level consults exactly the facts consumable at that level, in every edition.

### 2.4 (d) What an edition may change, and how revisions map

**The one-line formulation.** An edition may change the map from source text to declared facts. It may never change what the declared facts mean.

**May change.**

- **Surface syntax meanings** — a contextual word becoming reserved; a spelling acquiring a different meaning; a construct's desugaring, where the desugared result is expressible in both editions. (Rust's `dyn Trait`, disjoint closure capture, `array.into_iter()` — secondary-sourced.)
- **Source-level defaults.** §2.3's always-available set is the clean Bezel example: which library names are visible without `import` is a source-level visibility default (*"All other library modules require explicit `import`."*), and changing it changes no type's identity — `System.Console` is the same type auto-imported or not. Rust's 2018 prelude and path changes are the same move.
- **Warning→error promotions — where the landed Ratchet class runs out of road.** Bezel's warnings are normative (§15.4's *"must warn"*; Appendix D classifies outcomes — *"a mandated warning by any warning"*). 0106 §4.4 found no ratchet *policy*; **that gap is now closed**, and this bullet is written against the closure rather than the gap: the v0.263 train landed a fifth change class in `spec-change-process.md` §2 — **Ratchet**, for a scheduled tightening whose warning was its ramp, gated at *"ruling-class gate (§3–§4) **plus a stated ramp**: the Migration note must give both ends (§5)"* — together with the §5 requirement that a ratchet-class change record *"which revision landed the warning, which revision lands the rejection, and what a program that compiles today sees in between"*. **The landed class makes this proposal's case sharper, not weaker.** A ramp requirement is a requirement to name *where the far end lands*, and after 1.0 the language has no answer: "which revision lands the rejection" is, for a program someone else shipped, a promise that some revision will break it. An edition boundary is the only place that answer can be given without freezing the ratchet or breaking users — **warn in the current edition, deny at the next boundary**. The standing instances are the mandated warnings, not §14.6: §15.4's store lint (normative *"must warn"*, with *"The warning does not narrow the admission: the program remains accepted"*) and the three lints of rationale §5.28, which deliberately have no suppression spelling. §14.6 is *not* one of them — by the same §5 text it is a spec-ahead-of-impl delta — and this proposal says so rather than borrowing its urgency for the wrong argument; §14.6's relevance here is a different one, worked at this proposal's §2.2 and §5: it is a **closure-evaluated** diagnostic, the shape that forced clause 2 of the test.
- **Removal of a source form** whose replacement is expressible in the *previous* edition — the precondition that makes the migration mechanical and lets both editions express the migrated program.

**May not change.** The ABI (nothing to change — this proposal's §3); §2.8.3 identity and runtime oneness; any interface-carried fact's meaning; any §3.6 derived-property verdict (the table in this proposal's §2.2). **Conformance-level content**: Appendix D's Core/Extended partition is a property of the specification, not of an edition — were it edition-indexed, *"Core conformance"* would no longer name one thing and D.1's rejection obligation, *"with a diagnostic naming the construct and the feature"*, would have no fixed feature set to name. **Standard-library semantics**: the stdlib modules are implementation-provided (§2.8.1 rule 5) and their types are interface facts to every module in the program; an edition may change how source *names* them (the spec §2.3 example above), never what they do.

**The relationship to revision numbers.** Two instruments, kept separate. **A revision (v0.NNN) moves the one current language**, and the changelog stays the migration-normative record of that movement — *"Entries are normative for migration … but are not part of the language definition: the current rule on any point is always the latest text of the specification itself"* — unaltered here, so an edition never becomes a second place to look up a current rule. **An edition freezes past surface** *once a later edition exists*: its content is the specification as of its closing boundary, named rather than numbered by revision. **The exception is stated rather than glossed, because edition 1 is the case everyone stands in: the *current* edition is never frozen — it is the one revisions move.** Edition 1 is open text until 1.0 closes it, so a file pinned to edition 1 today is pinned to the language's current definition, not to a document that does not exist; the freeze attaches at the boundary and applies thereafter to every edition with a successor. **The proposed mapping ▲: edition 1 is the language as frozen at 1.0; before 1.0 there is exactly one edition, no boundary, and nothing frozen** — every pre-1.0 revision being a revision of edition 1's still-open content, which is why the register's breaking flips are free today and why the "zero external users" justification is coherent right up to the freeze. After 1.0 a boundary is declared by a revision that says so, making editions a sparse sequence over revisions, not a parallel numbering (Rust: four editions in ten years — secondary-sourced). **Deliberately not decided here:** *what may land outside a boundary* after 1.0 — whether a source-breaking revision is permitted at all, and under what soundness carve-out. That is 0106 §4.5's compatibility-promise item, carried without a vehicle, and this proposal must not pre-empt it; editions give such a promise its *"escape hatch (an edition boundary, which is why this needs this study's §4.1 for its teeth)"*, they do not write it.

### 2.5 (e) Mechanical migration as a stated requirement

**The obligation, recorded now ▲:** *no edition boundary may be declared without a mechanical migrator that rewrites conforming edition-N source into edition-(N+1) source, preserving meaning, for every change the boundary makes. A change that cannot be migrated mechanically is not edition-eligible.*

It is stated now, before any edition exists, because it is a **design constraint on which changes qualify**, not a tooling wish appended afterwards. This is the rustfix lesson in its load-bearing form: `cargo fix --edition` is what makes Rust's boundaries affordable, and the affordability is what makes breaking changes acceptable to an ecosystem at all (secondary-sourced). The counterexample is 0106 §4.2's: *"Swift 3→4 is the sequencing warning: a migrator without a pin still hurts"* — a migrator whose output cannot be pinned per module forces the whole graph to move together.

**It composes with the pin.** The migrator's first action at any boundary is to write the pin: under A1, one deterministic `edition N` clause per module header, so a module can be migrated and pinned in one mechanical pass while its dependents stay at the old edition and keep building.

**Feasibility, from the tree rather than from hope.** 0106 §4.2 found six in-tree `scripts/migrate_*.py`, one per breaking flip — *"including a rustfix-shaped one that drives `bezelc`, reads a diagnostic's (line, column), inserts the marker, and re-runs to fixpoint"* — so the shape is already practised here, never recorded as doctrine. Two vehicles exist for the substrate: proposal 0052 (diagnostics API — confidence-tagged fixits, today scoped to the agent loop) and proposal 0040 (build system, which has no fix stage). The sharpening for an ownership language, also 0106 §4.2's: the needed migrations are *ownership-marker* migrations, which a human cannot do by eye at scale, and the compiler is already normatively obliged to compute the remedy while being architecturally unable to emit it as data.

**Where the obligation is written** is a question, not a decision (Q5): `spec-change-process.md` binds revision authors and is the natural home for the eligibility test; a future §2.8.7 would bind implementations of a future edition. Lean: both — the test in the process document now, one spec sentence at the first boundary.

## 3. What this proposal does NOT do

1. **No edition ships, and nothing activates pre-1.0.** With one edition in existence there is no boundary, no migration, and no behavior to implement.
2. **No normative text is drafted for landing.** The two ▲ reservations (grammar slot, interface field) and the ▲ informational row are named so the *decision* is recorded; their text is a later revision's work, gated on Q1/Q2.
3. **The ABI non-goal is explicitly untouched.** §2.8.5, verbatim:

   > **ABI stability across recompilation.** The model is recompile-the-world: an artifact's interface is a compile-time contract for *this* build ([§2.8.3](../Bezel_Language_Spec.md#sec-transitive-closure-and-type-identity) rule 3 makes staleness an error, not a compatibility negotiation); a stable binary ABI is a separate commitment nothing yet motivates.

   And 0106 §4.1's binding correction, which this proposal obeys: *"**Keep editions and ABI apart**: §2.8.5's ABI non-goal is a recorded decision with a stated reason, attacking it is re-asking a decline, and editions do not require a stable ABI — Rust has none either — so a proposal bundling them will be correctly rejected on the ABI half and lose the good half with it."* A mixed-edition program is still recompile-the-world; §2.8.3 rule 3's staleness error is unchanged; the interop rule holds because declared facts are edition-invariant, not because binaries are compatible across time.
4. **No compatibility promise is made.** 0106 §4.5's item stays carried without a vehicle: editions are the escape hatch such a promise would need, not the promise, and no start condition, scope, or guarantee to users is named here.
5. **Conditional compilation is not reopened, and §2.8.5's bullet is not stretched either.** Nothing here admits intra-file conditionality; A1's "absence *is* edition 1" rule keeps every file's meaning a function of its own bytes. This proposal explicitly declines to *use* that bullet as a decline of candidate B (this proposal's §2.1): its subject is `#if`, and reading it wider would be re-using a recorded decision against a case it was not written about.
6. **§2.8.6's verdicts are not modified.** One informational row is proposed; no existing row's verdict changes and the layered-verdict machinery is untouched.

## 4. Alternatives

**(i) Do nothing until 1.0 — the Swift path.** Priced with 0106's evidence: six decision records with an expiry condition and no successor policy (0106 §4); a kernel still gaining rejections (§14.6, #3271); and the timing asymmetry, which this proposal states in its corrected form (this proposal's §1): the *field* is cheap whenever it lands, but the pin's home and the absence rule are cheap only while the corpus is one project's. The honest counter: doing nothing costs zero today, and a language that stops changing never needs editions — but §14.6's status line is direct evidence that this one has not stopped changing, and Swift 3→4 is what "decide it later" looked like for a project that had users by then. **Declined as the primary path.** Note that this alternative and "ship editions now" are *both* declined; the recommendation sits between them, as 0106 Q4 anticipated, recommending that if the answer is "later", *"the *reservation* half of editions … be taken anyway, since it surrenders nothing."*

**(ii) A Go-1-shaped compatibility promise, with no mechanism.** Go's promise works because Go bought it with a decade of feature conservatism: the promise is the visible half of a discipline whose expensive half is everything declined to keep it. 0106 §2.5 is candid that Bezel is a large language, and it is still adding rules. A promise with no escape hatch either freezes the wrong language — 0106 §4.5's counterweight, *"a premature promise is worse than none, because freezing while the kernel is still gaining rules would freeze the wrong language"* — or gets broken, which is worse than never making it. One half is genuinely attractive (a Bezel promise could be *mechanically checkable* where Go's is prose, §2.8.6's verdicts being normative and tool-computable), but that checkability is over **module** interfaces, not the language surface, so §2.8.6 does not by itself give a promise teeth. **Declined as a substitute for editions; retained as the separate open item it already is (0106 §4.5).**

**(iii) Per-file pins.** Rust rejected these; the reasons transfer and sharpen. §2.1 makes the files presented to one compilation *one module* sharing `internal` access, and §2.8.1 rule 2 makes that closure *"closed by compilation, not by name"* — two files of one module at different editions would be a single type-check under two source languages, with §2.8.2's interface obligation unable to say which language the module's surface was written in and §14.6's instantiation check spanning both. The pin's natural granularity is the unit that has one interface: the module. **Declined.** What survives is the *clause*'s file-level position under A1 — written per file because the module declaration is, with the whole-module agreement rule of this proposal's §2.1 making the pin per module.

## 6. Questions for the owner

- **Q1 — The pin's home.** Recommendation **A1**: an optional module-header clause (§2.1 grammar slot) whose *absence is normatively edition 1*, with a whole-module agreement rule and no build-request selector. The contest is proposal 0040's Stage-1 manifest key (candidate B), where Rust puts it. **B is not declined on a rule** — this proposal's §2.1 records that §2.8.5's conditional-compilation bullet is about `#if` and does not reach a whole-file pin, and that §2.8.4 rule 1 shows a normative rule *can* be keyed on a build-request property — so the recommendation is a stated preference for meaning-locality plus the whole-module agreement rule's per-file diagnostic. If the Cargo precedent weighs higher, B is the alternative, it can carry the absence rule unchanged, and only this proposal's §2.1 changes. **Confidence: high on A-over-B; medium on A1's absence-rule spelling.**
- **Q2 — Reserve the §2.8.2 field at the next convenient revision, or wait for a boundary?** Recommendation: **reserve now, but as hygiene rather than as urgency** — a rider on any revision already touching §2.8, one carried fact with no compile-time effect, whose value is that it makes the invariance rule auditable from the artifact rather than from trust. This proposal does *not* claim the field is expensive later (this proposal's §1 and §2.2); the time-sensitive decision is **Q1**, and an owner who rules Q1 now may defer Q2 to the first boundary at no recorded cost. The change class is itself a question: reserving a field is arguably a **ruling** (a required carriage fact, deciding nothing about program meaning), but `spec-change-process.md` §2's *"When unsure between two classes, take the stricter gate"* points at the design-change gate this proposal is written to satisfy.
- **Q3 — Edition naming and the 1.0 mapping.** Is the mapping proposed in this proposal's §2.4 — edition 1 is the 1.0 freeze, and pre-1.0 has exactly one edition — the intended one? And are editions named by ordinal or by year (Rust's convention)? The choice is permanent, and it is **not** cost-free as this document stands: this proposal's §2.1 and §2.4 are written in ordinals throughout ("absence *is* edition 1"; "the first pin ever written is `edition 2`"; "edition 1 is the language as frozen at 1.0"), so a year-named scheme is a rewrite of both, and the 1.0 anchor loses its natural name.
- **Q4 — May the members of one SCC pin different editions?** Per-module pinning is sound (this proposal's §2.1) but requires an edition-parameterized parse *within* one invocation; an SCC-wide agreement rule is a cheap normative restriction, liftable later. This proposal leans permissive and does not decide.
- **Q5 — Where does the migrator obligation live?** `spec-change-process.md` (binds revision authors), a future §2.8.7 (binds implementations of a future edition), or both. Lean: the eligibility test in the process document now, the spec sentence at the first boundary.
- **Q6 — Does the edition field join 0040's interface-hash cutoff key (0044 §4)?** Recommendation: **no**. Under the attribution rule (this proposal's §2.2) a dependency's edition is an input to neither a consumer's type-check nor any diagnostic reported at the consumer's declarations, so hashing it would invalidate dependents' caches for a fact nothing consumes. This answer is *contingent on the attribution rule*: were the owner to prefer consumer-edition evaluation for closure-checked diagnostics, the field would become load-bearing and this answer flips to yes.
