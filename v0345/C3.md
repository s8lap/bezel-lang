## #6887, #6897, #6935 — how a range literal and a pattern constant are typed (proposal 0204)

**Decision.** One rule, stated in §16.1 and applied in §17.5 and §19.3. (1) A range literal — a `RangeExpr` with at least one written endpoint — takes its `T` from its position where that supplies an integer type or `char`: a declared `Range<T>` location, an index position, the left operand of `in` and the argument of a `Contains` call on the literal, and a range pattern's scrutinee; it is read as a `Range<T>` when every written endpoint converts to `T` under §5.1 (a constant by its value, any other endpoint by the table). (2) Otherwise, and in an untyped context, the endpoints give `T` as the operands of a binary arithmetic operator do (§6.2 with §5.1's constant narrowing): `5..10L` is `Range<long>`, `5U..10` and `0..3000000000U` are `Range<uint>`, `-5..10U` is `Range<int>` (the constant `10U` fits `int`, as `3U + -1` is the `int` `2`, #6984), `-1..3000000000U` has no common type and is a compile error at the range expression. A literal typed by its endpoints in a target-typed position is an ordinary `Range<T>` value there, so at `in`/`Contains` the operand converts to `T` as to any range value's (`b in -100..0` over a `byte` is a `Range<int>` test; `u in -5..10` over a `uint` is rejected). (3) A pattern has no fallback: over an integer scrutinee, nullable or not, a constant pattern's constant, a relational pattern's constant operand and a range pattern's bounds convert to the scrutinee's type under §5.1 or are compile errors at the pattern. §16.6's "agrees exactly with `in`" becomes a consequence and its sentence says so.

**Class and why.** Design change: the compiler's pattern rule rejects what §19.3's "equality test of §8.4" (typed by §3.5's promotion) admits, and the interim `in` rule admits what §17.5 read with §16.3 rejects; whichever way the three go, a stated admission narrows or a stated rejection widens. Proposal 0204 (§2 there). Recommendations followed: #6897's reading 2 (the implemented clauses B and W, with the ordinary check as the fallback); #6887's reading (B), the §6.2 common type; #6935's §5.1 conversion for patterns. **Deviations:** the triage's sub-recommendation that the equal-width constant tie (`5U..10`) be an error in an untyped context is declined — §6.2 decides `5U + 10` as `uint` (rule corpus `s6_2_0003`/`0004`, banked `pass`) and a range should not type its endpoints differently from a sum of them; the interim clause A (the operand widening to the endpoints' common *widening* type) is dropped — a third notion of common type under which `0..3000000000U` is `long` at `in` and `uint` everywhere else; #6935's triage reading 2 (pattern bounds typed as the promoted expression, admitting `case b of -5..10` over a `byte`) is declined for the pattern side — it would flip twelve lit pins to admitted-never-matching and leave the pattern looser than §5.1's "never a silent truncation" posture, while the expression side (`in`) does get that reading through the fallback, exactly as `b > 300` is admitted and `case b of > 300` is not today.

## #6772 — a fact established on the only `break` path out of `while true`

**Decision.** Reading 2. §21.3 now states: a `while` whose condition is written as the literal `true` is left only through a `break` that targets it; the point after it is reached through those `break`s and through nothing else, and definite assignment (§7.9), flow narrowing (§7.4) and §21.7's every-exit-path rule read its exits as exactly those `break`s — each a path carrying its own facts, in whatever iteration it runs. So after `var s: string? := null; while true do s := "zz"; break; end;` the read `s.Length` is admitted, and a `while true` no `break` targets completes normally on no path. Any other condition, a constant expression folding to `true` included, is opaque. §7.4's every-path clause gains the cross-reference; rationale §18's post-condition-loop entry notes that the idiom composes with narrowing.

**Class and why.** Ruling: no normative sentence mentioned a constant `while` condition; §21.7's "ordinary reachability over its completion edges" does not define the false exit of `while true`.
