## 0. Summary

A range literal (`lo..hi`, `lo..<hi`, `lo..`, `..hi`) has a `T`, and the text gives it only one way: "A range over `T` has type `Range<T>`" (§16.3), with the endpoints understood to supply it. Nothing says what `T` is when the endpoints disagree (`5..10L`, `-5..10U`), and nothing says whether a literal written against an `in` operand, a `Contains` argument or a `case` scrutinee of another integer type is read at its endpoints' type or at the operand's. The compiler takes `T` from the low endpoint alone and never checks the high one, so `for var i in -5..10L` reaches LLVM with an `i32`/`i64` compare and dies in verification (MEASURED in #6887). For `in`, the #6853 fix-forward implemented an interim target-typing rule with three clauses, one of which (clause A: the operand widening to the endpoints' common *widening* type, so `i in 0..3000000000U` over an `int` is a `Range<long>` test) has no analogue anywhere else in the language. For patterns the compiler has always converted the constant to the scrutinee's type under §5.1 and rejected one that does not fit (`case b of 300` over a `byte`), a rule with twelve lit pins and no sentence behind it.

This proposal states the rule once. **A range literal is target-typed where its position supplies an integer type or `char`** — a declared `Range<T>` location, an index position, the left operand of `in`, the argument of a `Contains` call on the literal, a range pattern's scrutinee — **when every written endpoint converts to that type under §5.1**, a constant by its value and any other endpoint by the conversion table; **otherwise its endpoints give its type as the operands of a binary arithmetic operator give theirs** (§6.2 with §5.1's constant narrowing), and a pair with no common type is a compile error at the literal. A literal that falls back to its own type in a target-typed position is then an ordinary `Range<T>` value there, so `in` and `Contains` apply their ordinary conversion of the operand to `T`. A **pattern** admits no fallback: over an integer scrutinee a constant pattern's constant, a relational pattern's constant operand and a range pattern's bounds convert to the scrutinee's type under §5.1, and one that does not is a compile error at the pattern. §16.6's "agrees exactly with `in`" becomes a consequence: an admitted pattern's bounds and the literal at the same operand are read at the same type.

**Reach, measured (§6; re-measured by the critic):** every in-tree program keeps its verdict except one row of the #6853 fix-forward's own tests, which pinned the interim rule "pending this question's ruling" (QUOTED, the #6897 comment): `cross_signedness_literal_operands_spec_16_3`'s two lines testing `i in 0 .. 3000000000U` and `(0 .. 3000000000U).Contains(i)` over an `int` — `0..3000000000U` is a `Range<uint>` by §6.2, and only clause A admitted the `int` operand. The untyped `var r := -5 .. 10U` declarations and the `s in -40000 .. 10U`, `b in -5 .. 10U`, `b in -5 .. 255` tests keep their verdicts and values: the constant `10U` fits `int`, so those literals are `Range<int>` (§6.2 with §5.1's constant narrowing, as `3U + -1` is the `int` `2`, pinned by `uint_literal_left_operand_6984_test.cpp`), and `short` and `byte` widen to `int`. No rule-corpus or doc-corpus case moves. Newly admitted: a literal at a declared `Range<T>` location of another integer type (`var r: Range<byte> := 0..100;`, zero sites, today written `byte(0)..byte(100)`), and the fully open `..`'s admission of §16.2 is unchanged.

## 1. The text, and what it does not say

> A range over `T` has type `Range<T>`. `T` is restricted by the compiler to the integer types (`byte`, `sbyte`, `short`, `ushort`, `int`, `uint`, `long`, `ulong`) and `char`.
>
> — §16.3, v0.344

> Because neither endpoint supplies `T`, the form has **no standalone type** and is admitted only where a written type supplies one — a declared `Range<T>` location (`var all: Range<int> := ..;`), an index position, or an argument position whose parameter type is a `Range<T>` — and is a compile error in an untyped context (`var r := ..;`), the diagnostic directing to an ascription; this is the same admission [§4.2](#sec-integer-types) gives its target-typed integer constants.
>
> — §16.2, v0.344

> `in` states no nullability rule of its own: `x in c` *is* the call `c.Contains(x)`, so the left operand is admitted exactly when it is admissible as that call's argument under the ordinary conversion rules ([§5.1](#sec-implicit-conversions))
>
> — §17.5, v0.344

> **Range patterns:** a range pattern `lo..hi` / `lo..<hi` (necessarily stepless, [§16.4](#sec-step)) matches `v` iff `lo <= v <= hi` (resp. `< hi`) read directionally as written. […] With the `Contains` rule this **agrees exactly with `in`** on the stepless forms
>
> — §16.6, v0.344

> **Constants:** `1`, `42`, `-7`, `"hello"`, `Priority.High`. A leading `-` on a numeric literal is part of the constant pattern (grammar A.8).
>
> — §19.3, v0.344

> Constant patterns over a nullable scrutinee are unaffected: a constant pattern is the equality test of [§8.4](#sec-=-and), which is total on `null` and does not equal a constant.
>
> — §19.3, v0.344

> When operands have different types, the smaller-range operand is implicitly promoted to the larger type using [§5.1](#sec-implicit-conversions). The result has the larger type. If no implicit conversion exists between the operand types (e.g. `double` and `decimal`), the operation is a compile error and an explicit cast is required.
>
> — §6.2, v0.344

Three things are decided: a range's static type is `Range<T>`; the fully open `..` is target-typed; `in` is the `Contains` call. Three are not:

1. **`T` when the endpoints have different types.** §16.3 presupposes one endpoint type. §6.2 is the obvious candidate and is stated for operators, not for `..`.
2. **Whether a literal adapts to the `in` operand.** Read with §16.3, `0..100` is a `Range<int>` and `n in 0..100` over a `long` is a §5.1 error (`long` never converts to `int` implicitly). §16.6 says the pattern `0..100`, which §19.3 admits over a `long`, "agrees exactly with `in`". The two sentences disagree on this program.
3. **How a pattern constant meets its scrutinee.** "The equality test of §8.4" is typed by §3.5's promotion, under which `b = 300` over a `byte` is admitted and false. The compiler rejects `case b of 300`, and has since #1156, with the diagnostic "has a type that is not implicitly convertible to the scrutinee type (§5.1)". Neither §19.3 nor §5.1 says which.

---

## 2. Why this is a proposal

Each question alone could be read as a ruling over silence. Together they are not: the compiler's pattern rule rejects what §19.3's "equality test of §8.4" admits (a constant beyond the scrutinee's range), and the interim `in` rule admits what §17.5 read with §16.3 rejects (`n in 0..100` over a `long`). Whichever way the three are decided, one stated admission narrows or one stated rejection widens, and the decision for one bounds the others — the triage found that the issues' three recorded recommendations, taken separately, contradict §16.6. Under the process's §2 that is a design change, and the stricter gate applies where the class is in doubt.

---

## 3. Options

Each option answers the three questions. The `in` and `Contains` behaviour is written for a literal; a range value's `T` is its static type under every option.

- **A — status quo text, read literally.** `T` is the endpoints' (undefined when they differ); `n in 0..100` over a `long` is rejected; a pattern constant is the §8.4 test typed by promotion, so `case b of 300` is admitted and never matches. Contradicts §16.6 and the compiler's twelve lit pins.
- **E — endpoints only, low endpoint first (the compiler's range typing; #6887 reading A).** `T` is the low endpoint's type and the high endpoint converts to it under §5.1; `in` under the interim rule. Order-dependent: `n..0` over `n: long` is a `Range<long>` while `0..n` is a `Range<int>` whose `long` endpoint does not convert (MEASURED in #6887, unchecked: the IR-verifier failure); no analogue elsewhere.
- **S — strict target typing, no fallback.** The literal is read at the position's type and every endpoint must convert to it, at `in` as in a pattern; otherwise a compile error. One rule, but it rejects `b in -100..0` over a `byte` and `c in 65..90` over a `char`, both admitted today and the first pinned (`range_contains_unsigned_6749_6752_test.cpp:51,71`), and it makes a literal stricter than a stored range of the same spelling (`var r := -100..0; b in r` stays admitted).
- **T — target typing with the ordinary rule as the fallback (decided).** The literal is read at the position's type when every written endpoint converts to it under §5.1; otherwise it is typed by its endpoints as §6.2 types a binary operator's operands, a pair with no common type being an error, and the position then treats it as the `Range<T>` value it is. A pattern has no fallback: a pattern converts its scrutinee to nothing, so a bound or constant that does not convert to the scrutinee's type is a compile error. This is the issue's recommendation for #6897 (the implemented clauses B and W, with "otherwise the ordinary §5.1 check against `T` decides"), the §6.2 common type the issue recommends for #6887, and the §5.1 conversion the issue recommends for #6935.
- **M — mathematical comparison (#6935 reading 3).** A pattern constant and a range test compare mathematical values with no typing constraint, so `case u of -5..10` over a `ulong` is admitted and `in`. Nothing else in the language compares integers of different types without a common type; `u < -5` is a compile error.

The interim rule's clause A — "the endpoints' types share a §5.1 widening target `C` and `O` widens to `C`" (QUOTED, the #6897 comment) — is a variant of T that computes the fallback type by *widening the endpoints' static types* instead of by §6.2's constant narrowing, so `0..3000000000U` is read as `long` against an `int` operand where §6.2 makes `0 + 3000000000U` a `uint` (rule-corpus `s6_2_0003`, banked). It is evaluated as part of T below and declined.

T is the smallest rule that is consistent with the rest of the language and with what the compiler already pins: §16.2 already target-types a range literal, §4.2 already target-types an integer constant, §6.2 already types a mixed pair, and the pattern side is the compiler's rule since #1156. S is one sentence shorter and buys that sentence with a rejection the stored range does not make.

## 5. Decision

**Option T.**

1. **A range literal is target-typed** where its position supplies an integer type or `char`: a declared `Range<T>` location (variable, field, parameter, return, or an argument at a `Range<T>` parameter), an index position, the left operand of `in` and the argument of a `Contains` call on the literal, and a range pattern's scrutinee. It is read as a `Range<T>` when every written endpoint converts to `T` under §5.1 — a constant by its value, any other endpoint by the conversion table.
2. **Otherwise, and in an untyped context, the endpoints give `T`** as the operands of a binary arithmetic operator give theirs (§6.2 with §5.1's constant narrowing): `5..10L` is `Range<long>`, `5U..10` and `0..3000000000U` are `Range<uint>`, `0..18446744073709551615UL` is `Range<ulong>`, `-5..10U` is `Range<int>` (the constant `10U` fits `int`, as `3U + -1` is the `int` `2`), and `-1..3000000000U`, like `-1 + 3000000000U`, has no common type and is a compile error at the range expression. A literal so typed in a target-typed position is an ordinary `Range<T>` value there: at `in` and `Contains` the operand converts to `T` under §5.1, so `b in -100..0` over a `byte` is a `Range<int>` test and `u in -5..10` over a `uint` is a compile error.
3. **A pattern has no fallback.** Over an integer scrutinee, nullable or not, read at its underlying type, a constant pattern's constant, a relational pattern's constant operand and a range pattern's bounds convert to the scrutinee's type under §5.1, and one that does not is a compile error at the pattern, never an arm that cannot match. Over a `char` scrutinee a range pattern's bounds convert to `char` likewise — the pattern is a `Range<char>` membership test ([§16.3]), and an integer bound is no `char` under §5.1, so `case c of 65..90` is rejected (unchecked by the compiler today, zero sites; tracker item (v)); a constant or relational pattern over a `char` stays §8.4's or §3.5's test (`case c of 65` is `c = 65`), a residual this proposal does not decide (filed as a question, lane md).
4. **A range value** — a variable, field, parameter or call result — keeps the `T` of its static type at every use.

The equal-width constant tie the triage named (`5U..10`) takes §6.2's answer, as `5U + 10` does: the rule corpus banks `1U + 2` as `uint` (`s6_2_0003`, `s6_2_0004`), and a range should not type its endpoints differently from a sum of them. This deviates from the triage's sub-recommendation (an error in an untyped context); the ground is that §6.2 already decides it.

## 9. Declined alternatives (one `declined-at-proposal` row each)

- **Option E, `T` from the low endpoint** (#6887 reading A, the compiler). `T` would depend on which endpoint is written first (`n..0` over `n: long` a `Range<long>`, `0..n` rejected), and the conversion of the high endpoint, once checked, would reject `0..n` and `0..5000000000L`, which §6.2 types as `long`. Reopening bar: none stated.
- **Option C of #6887, identical endpoint types required, constants excepted.** Rejects `a..b` over `a: int`, `b: long`, which every binary operator admits. Reopening bar: none stated.
- **#6897 reading 1, `T` fixed by the endpoints at `in`.** Rejects `n in 0..100` over a `long` and `requires(port in 0..65535)` over a `long` port while §19.3 admits the pattern, contradicting §16.6. Reopening bar: none stated.
- **The interim clause A, the operand widening to the endpoints' common widening type** (the #6853 fix-forward). A third common type, by widening alone, that makes `0..3000000000U` a `long` at `in` and a `uint` in arithmetic and at a declaration. Reopening bar: a measured corpus of sign-mixed constant literals whose `in` tests the fallback rejects.
- **Option M, mathematical comparison for pattern constants** (#6935 reading 3), and **#6935 reading 2, the pattern as the promoted expression**, which admits `case b of -5..10` over a `byte` as an arm matching `0..10` and `case b of 300` as an arm that never matches. The language compares integers only at a common type, and a constant that cannot match is §5.1's "never a silent truncation" posture at a pattern. Reopening bar: none stated.
- **An error for the equal-width constant tie in an untyped context** (`5U..10`; the triage's sub-recommendation). §6.2 decides `5U + 10` as `uint` (rule corpus `s6_2_0003`), and a range should type its endpoints as a sum of them is typed. Reopening bar: a ruling that changes §6.2's answer for the operator.

> ▲ **§16.1** (a new paragraph after "`a..b` is inclusive on both ends; `a..<b` is inclusive on the lower bound, exclusive on the upper."). **The element type of a range literal.** A range literal — a `RangeExpr` with at least one written endpoint (grammar [A.7]; the fully open `..` is [§16.2]'s) — takes its `T` from its **position** where the position supplies an integer type or `char`: a declared `Range<T>` location (`var r: Range<long> := 0..10;`, a `Range<T>` field, parameter or return, an argument at a `Range<T>` parameter), an index position ([§15.6]), the left operand of `in` and the argument of a `Contains` call on the literal ([§17.5]), and a range pattern's scrutinee ([§19.3]). The literal is read as a `Range<T>` when every written endpoint converts to `T` under [§5.1] — a constant by its value, any other endpoint by the conversion table — and each endpoint is then a `T`. Otherwise, and in an untyped context (`var r := 0..10;`, the source of a `for`, `(1..10).Sum()`), the **endpoints** give the type: `T` is the common type the operands of a binary arithmetic operator take ([§6.2], with §5.1's constant narrowing), so `5..10L` is a `Range<long>`, `5U..10` and `0..3000000000U` are `Range<uint>`, `-5..10U` is a `Range<int>` (the constant `10U` fits `int`, as in `-5 + 10U`), and a pair with no common type — `-1..3000000000U`, or `a..b` over `a: int` and `b: uint` — is a compile error at the range expression, the diagnostic directing to a suffix, a conversion or an ascription. A literal typed by its endpoints in a target-typed position is an ordinary `Range<T>` value there: at `in` and `Contains` the operand converts to its `T` under §5.1 as to any range value's ([§17.5]), so `b in -100..0` over a `byte` is a `Range<int>` test, while `u in -5..10` over a `uint` is a compile error (`-5` fits no `uint`, and `uint` does not convert to `int`); at a declared `Range<T>` location the mismatch is the ordinary type error; a range pattern admits no such fallback ([§19.3]). A range **value** — a variable, field, parameter or call result — has the `T` of its static type wherever it is used.

> ▲ **§17.5** (a new paragraph after *A nullable left operand*). **A range literal as the right operand.** A range literal written as the right operand of `in`, or as the receiver of the `Contains` call, is target-typed by the left operand ([§16.1]): where the operand's type is an integer type or `char` and every written endpoint converts to it under [§5.1] — a constant by its value — the literal is a `Range<O>` over the operand's type `O`, so `n in 0..100` over a `long` and `b in 100..255` over a `byte` are `Range<long>` and `Range<byte>` tests, and `requires(port in 0..65535)` is well-typed over a `long` port. Otherwise the literal has the type its endpoints give it and the operand converts to that `T` as to any range value's: `b in -100..0` over a `byte` is a `Range<int>` test, and `u in -5..10` over a `uint` is a compile error — `-5` fits no `uint`, and `uint` does not convert to `int`. A range **value** keeps its static `T`: `l in r` over `r: Range<int>` and `l: long` is a compile error whatever `r` holds.

> ▲ **§19.3** (*Constants*, after the grammar A.8 sentence). Over an **integer** scrutinee — nullable or not, read at its underlying type — a constant pattern's constant converts to the scrutinee's type under [§5.1] (a constant by its value, across signedness), and one that does not — `300` over a `byte`, `-1` over a `ulong`, `18446744073709551615UL` over a `long` — is a compile error at the pattern, never an arm that cannot match; the equality test is then performed at the scrutinee's type. A relational pattern's constant operand and a range pattern's bounds are read the same way (below).

> ▲ **§19.3** (*Inclusive and exclusive ranges*, appended). A range pattern's bounds are read at the scrutinee's type as a range literal is target-typed at the left operand of `in` ([§16.1], [§17.5]): over an integer or `char` scrutinee each written bound converts to the scrutinee's type under [§5.1], a constant by its value, and a bound that does not is a compile error at the pattern — a pattern has no fallback to the bounds' own type, since it converts its scrutinee to nothing — so `case u of -5..10` over a `ulong` is rejected at `-5` and `case n of 0..100` over a `long` is admitted, the agreement [§16.6] states.

> ▲ **§19.3** (*Relational*, one clause). Compares the scrutinee against the right-hand operand, under the **intrinsic** ordering of the scrutinee's type ([§3.5]) — over an integer scrutinee a constant operand converts to the scrutinee's type under [§5.1] as a constant pattern's constant does (*Constants*, above), so `> 300` over a `byte` is a compile error;

> ▲ **Appendix C.1** (a bullet). A range literal is typed from its position where that supplies an integer type or `char` and every written endpoint converts to it ([§5.1]), and otherwise from its endpoints as a binary operator's operands are ([§6.2]); a pair with no common type is a compile error at the range expression ([§16.1]).

> ▲ **Appendix C.7** (a bullet). Over an integer scrutinee, a constant pattern's constant, a relational pattern's constant operand and a range pattern's bounds convert to the scrutinee's type under [§5.1], as a range pattern's bounds do over a `char` scrutinee; one that does not is a compile error at the pattern, never an arm that cannot match ([§19.3]).

## 11. Self-critique (adversarial pass)

- **Does the fallback reopen #6853's defect?** No. #6853 was `u in -5..10` over a `uint` *admitted* (the operand never checked against `T`). Under T the literal falls back to `Range<int>` and the operand check `uint → int` rejects it, as the #6853 fix does today.
- **Does the fallback admit a silently vacuous test?** `b in 300..400` over a `byte` is admitted as a `Range<int>` test and is always `false` — as `b > 300` is admitted and always `false` in an expression (§3.5 promotes). The pattern `case b of 300..400` is rejected, as `case b of > 300` is (#1156). The expression/pattern split already exists in the language; this proposal states it rather than widens it. A never-true `in` over constants could carry §16.6's reversed-bounds warning; not added here (no warning is normative for `b > 300` either).
- **Is "every written endpoint converts" sound for a non-constant endpoint of the operand's type?** Yes: `n in 0..hi` over `n: long`, `hi: int` is a `Range<long>` whose `hi` is widened; no value changes. The interim clause W is this case.
- **Neighbouring cells.** A `char` operand against `char` bounds is target-typed at `char`; against integer bounds (`c in 65..90`) the fallback reads a `Range<int>` and `char → int` admits it, as today. A `char` scrutinee's range pattern needs `char` bounds (§16.3 admits `Range<char>`; `65..90` over a `char` scrutinee: `int → char` is no §5.1 conversion, so rejected — the compiler's `caseIntScrutineeRange` excludes `char`, so today it is unchecked; the tracker names the cell). The fully open `..` keeps §16.2's rule; an open-ended `31..` has one written endpoint and follows rule 1 or 2 like a closed one. A nullable operand or scrutinee stays rejected before any typing (§17.5, §19.3). A `step` clause types nothing (`Step: long`, §16.3).
- **The equal-width tie.** `5U..10` is `uint` by §6.2's answer for `5U + 10`. A reader who disagrees with §6.2 there disagrees with the operator, not with this rule.
- **History narration.** The normative edits state the rule; the story is in this file and the changelog.
