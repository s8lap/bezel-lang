## Findings

### F1 (major, fixed): `-5..10U` is a `Range<int>`, not an error; reach is 1 row, not 7
The lane wrote that "`-5..10U`, like `-5 + 10U`, has no common type". §5.1's constant narrowing is "value-based … across signedness" (a `uint`-typed constant `7` is admissible where `int` is expected). Under it the constant `10U` fits `int` and adopts it, so the result is `int`. The compiler pins exactly this:
- `tests/sema/uint_literal_left_operand_6984_test.cpp`, `two_literals_unsuffixed_adopts_spec_5_1`: `Console.WriteLine(3U + -1)` prints `2`, with the comment "-1 does not fit uint, 3U fits int, so the one that fits adopts".
- The triage (R3 #6887) computed `-5 .. 10UL → int` the same way.

The rule-corpus case the lane cited, `s5_1_0130`, is `4000000000U + -7`. There neither constant fits the other's type, which makes it a different cell.

The normative rule ("the common type the operands of a binary arithmetic operator take, §6.2 with §5.1's constant narrowing") was already right. Only its examples and the measurements built on them were wrong. Re-measured with `remeasure.py`, which types every mixed constant literal by the #6984 rule and classifies every `x in lo..hi` site under 0204:
- **Flips to rejected:** only `range_in_contains_agree_6853_test.cpp` `cross_signedness_literal_operands_spec_16_3`, lines 121 and 123. These are `i in 0 .. 3000000000U` and its `Contains` twin over an `int`. The literal is `Range<uint>` by §6.2, and `int → uint` is never implicit; only clause A admitted it.
- **Keep their verdict and value:**
  - `boundaries_three_spellings_agree_spec_17_5`: `var r := -5 .. 10U` is `Range<int>`.
  - `narrow_operands_widen_into_int_range_spec_5_1`: `s in -40000 .. 10U`, `b in -5 .. 10U` and `b in -5 .. 255` are `Range<int>` tests through the fallback (`short`/`byte → int`), and the pinned `True/False/True` is the mathematical answer.
  - `b in -5 .. 255` could not have flipped even under the lane's own reading: both endpoints are `int`.
- **Unchanged:** `u in -5 .. 10` over a `uint` (still rejected) and the 6749 rows.

Fixes:

### F2 (fixed): the option E DECLINED row's grounds were false
The row said "`5U..10` and `-5..10U` differ" and that the checked form "would reject `-5..10L`". Under T, `5U..10` is `uint` and `-5..10U` is `int`, the same answers as option E. A checked option E also admits `-5..10L`, because `10L` fits `int`. Real order-dependence shows with a non-constant endpoint: `n..0` over `n: long` is a `Range<long>`, while `0..n` is rejected because `long` does not convert to `int`. The checked form also rejects `0..5000000000L`. I rewrote the row, proposal §3 and §9, and the rationale clause.

### F3 (fixed): §16.6's "agrees exactly with `in`" was overstated, and `x in r` and `case x of r` do disagree
As drafted, §16.6 said the pattern's bounds "are read … as the literal is read at the left operand's type in `v in lo..hi`". Under the fallback that is false. Programs that disagree:
- `var b: byte := 0;` then `b in -100..0` is admitted (`True`, a `Range<int>` test), while `case b of -100..0 => …` is a compile error.
- `b in 0..<256` against `case b of 0..<256`.
- `c in 65..90` over a `char` against `case c of 65..90`.

§16.6 counts verdict agreement as part of its claim ("what agrees is the verdict" for nullable operands), so the drafted sentence was contradicted. No value disagreement exists. When a pattern is admitted, every bound converts to the scrutinee type `O`, and that is exactly the condition under which `in` target-types the literal at `O`. The two readings are the same `Range<O>`, and the §5.1 conversions are value-preserving.

The new §16.6 text states the split: bounds that do not convert are an error in the pattern, while `in` falls back (with the byte example). It then says that an admitted range pattern agrees exactly with `in`. The split itself is acceptable, and it is the existing `b > 300` against `case b of > 300` split. Option S (no fallback) would remove it, but at the cost of the pinned #6749 rows and of making a literal stricter than a stored range with the same spelling. The lane weighed this correctly.

§19.3 ranges said "exactly as a range literal is read at the left operand of `in`" followed by "no fallback". It now says "as a range literal is target-typed at the left operand of `in`".

### F4 (fixed): `char` was handled three different ways
- The spec edit for §19.3 ranges covers "an integer or `char` scrutinee", so `case c of 65..90` is rejected.
- Proposal §5 rule 3 said "A `char` scrutinee is unchanged".
- Proposal §7 said `caseIntScrutineeRange` "excludes `char`, as rule 3 does".
- The C3.md tracker had item (v) for the `char` gate, but the proposal's tracker did not.
- Appendix C.7's new bullet covered integer scrutinees only.

I kept the spec's rule, which is principled: a range pattern is `Range<char>` membership, and `int → char` is no §5.1 conversion. The compiler leaves this cell unchecked today and it has zero sites. I aligned rule 3, §7, the tracker's item (v), the changelog migration, and the C.7 bullet ("as a range pattern's bounds do over a `char` scrutinee").

One inconsistency remains and is not decided here. Over a `char`, `case c of 65` is still §8.4's admitted `c = 65`, while `case c of 65..65` is rejected. I filed it as question 3 in C3.md. A fold would newly reject constant patterns over `char`, with unmeasured reach.

### F5 (fixed): #6772's sentence said `while true` "is left only through a `break` that targets it"
That is false. `return`, `throw`, `break outer` and `continue outer` all leave it. What is true is that the point after it is reached only through the `break`s that target it. Reworded:
- "has no exit at its condition: the point after it is reached through the `break`s that target it (§21.5: an unlabelled `break` in a nested loop, or a `break` naming an outer label, targets another loop) and through nothing else, a `return` or `throw` leaving it reaching no point after it".
- "Parenthesized or not" was added. The AST has no `ParenExpr`, and `bodyAlwaysExits` keys on `BoolLiteralExpr`, so `while (true)` already behaves this way.

Re-walked cells:
- **Nested loops:** an inner `while true` with only `break outer` completes normally on no path, and the outer loop's exits are the `break outer` edges.
- **`try`/`finally`:** a `break` passes through the `finally` (§7.4, 0198), so its edge carries the `finally`'s kills.
- **`continue`:** reaches the condition, which has no exit, so it adds no path.
- **Definite assignment:** consistent with the compiler's #6140 behaviour (QUOTED in the issue comment) and with §21.7 ("counts as exiting on every path exactly when no path through it completes normally").
- **Back-edges:** "each a path carrying its own facts, in whatever iteration it runs" correctly blocks the entry-state-only reading. Nothing newly rejected.

### F6 (fixed): #7281's sentence against contract-opaque artifacts
"An artifact whose interface does not carry a fact the consumer's rules need … is rejected" could be read as covering a contract-opaque artifact. §2.8.2 and the contract-classification *Degradation* paragraph give that artifact a defined reading (signature-level classification; "must not treat absent records as empty"). I added "(a fact whose absence the rules themselves read, as a contract-opaque artifact's contract records below, is not one)". The rest is sound: the mechanism stays implementation-defined, and refusal conforms under §2.8.5's recompile-the-world model. C.13 is consistent.
